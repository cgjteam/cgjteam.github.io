# Log 029 - The Axiomatic Structure of the Tarski Route

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Project:** CGJteam Lab  
**Status:** Tarski Foundation Reconstruction

---

## 1. Overview

The reconstruction of the current Tarski route begins with two files:

```lean
TarskiAxioms.lean
TarskiInterface.lean
```

The first provides the formal hierarchy of Tarski-style axiom classes.

The second develops a substantial derived theory above those axioms and
introduces the Tarski-specific notions required by later geometric proofs.

The architecture is cleaner than a direct dependence on the Hilbert axioms.
Although `TarskiAxioms.lean` imports `HilbertCore.lean`, it reuses only the
common primitive geometric language needed by the Tarski formulation.

The active dependency is therefore not

```text
Hilbert axioms
      |
      v
Tarski axioms.
```

Instead:

```text
HilbertCore
     |
     | shared primitive language
     v
TarskiAxioms
     |
     v
TarskiInterface.
```

The Tarski route has its own axiom hierarchy.

---

## 2. Shared Primitive Vocabulary

The Tarski axiom classes are formulated using the point type and primitive
relations already available in the common core.

The essential language is:

```text
Point
Between
Congruent
equality
```

Thus the route is point-based.

There are no primitive Tarski line objects, angle objects, midpoint
operations, or parallel relations in the axiom classes.

Schematically:

```text
Point
 |
 +--> Between
 |
 `--> Congruent
```

Higher-level notions are reconstructed from this small language.

---

## 3. TarskiNeutral

The basic class is

```lean
TarskiNeutral.
```

It contains the neutral axioms corresponding to the first seven axioms of the
current Tarski presentation.

The fields include:

```text
Ax.1  congruent_reversal
Ax.2  congruent_transitivity
Ax.3  congruent_identity
Ax.4  segment_construction
Ax.5  five_segment
Ax.6  between_identity
Ax.7  inner_pasch
```

This is the foundational neutral geometry used by the later reconstruction.

The important point is that the substantial order and collinearity theory
developed later is not simply inserted into this class.

It is reconstructed above these basic assumptions.

---

## 4. Dimension Two

The next class is

```lean
TarskiPlane
```

extending `TarskiNeutral`.

It adds the two-dimensional assumptions:

```text
lower_dimension_two
upper_dimension_two.
```

These correspond to the lower- and upper-dimension conditions for the plane.

Thus:

```text
TarskiNeutral
      |
      v
TarskiPlane.
```

The dimensional assumptions are kept separate from the neutral order and
congruence theory.

---

## 5. The Euclidean Level

The final class in the current hierarchy is

```lean
TarskiEuclideanPlane.
```

It extends `TarskiPlane` with the Euclidean axiom:

```text
euclid.
```

Schematically:

```text
TarskiNeutral
      |
      v
TarskiPlane
      |
      v
TarskiEuclideanPlane.
```

Continuity is deliberately not included in the current development.

This separation allows later dependency analysis to ask whether a theorem
requires only neutral Tarski geometry, the plane dimension assumptions, or
the Euclidean axiom.

---

## 6. First Derived Definitions

`TarskiInterface.lean` begins by defining familiar geometric notions directly
from the Tarski primitives.

The first important definitions are:

```lean
TarskiCollinear
TarskiIsMidpoint.
```

Tarski collinearity is expressed through equality and the possible
betweenness orientations of three points.

Midpoint combines betweenness with congruence of the two component segments.

Schematically:

```text
Between
   |
   +--> TarskiCollinear
   |
   +-------------------+
                       |
Congruent -------------+
                       |
                       v
               TarskiIsMidpoint.
```

These notions belong to the Tarski route itself and do not require the shared
synthetic collinearity interface.

---

## 7. The Explicit Bridge

The file also introduces

```lean
TarskiGeometryBaseBridge.
```

Its role is to connect the Tarski-specific notion of collinearity with the
collinearity used by the shared synthetic geometry layer.

Schematically:

```text
TarskiCollinear
        |
        v
TarskiGeometryBaseBridge
        |
        v
shared Collinear.
```

This bridge should be distinguished from the Tarski axiom hierarchy.

It is an interpretation or compatibility boundary between two geometric
languages.

The existence of the bridge does not mean that the Tarski axioms depend on
the Hilbert axiom classes.

---

## 8. Derived Betweenness Theory

A substantial part of `TarskiInterface.lean` reconstructs classical
betweenness results above `TarskiNeutral`.

The development includes results corresponding to:

```text
A12  betweenness reflexivity
A14  betweenness symmetry
A15  inner transitivity
A16  outer transitivity
A17  inner connectivity.
```

These results are not all primitive fields of `TarskiNeutral`.

The emerging chain is:

```text
Ax.1-Ax.7
    |
    v
elementary congruence and order lemmas
    |
    v
betweenness symmetry / transitivity
    |
    v
connectivity
    |
    v
stronger collinearity theory.
```

This is one of the most important features of the current Tarski route:
significant portions of the familiar order theory have already been formally
reconstructed.

---

## 9. The Deferred Outer Connectivity Result

The reconstruction is not yet complete.

The interface contains the explicit assumption

```lean
tarski_between_outer_connectivity.
```

It represents the outer-connectivity result used in the derivation of later
order theorems.

The dependency therefore contains a deferred step:

```text
TarskiNeutral
     |
     v
derived order theory
     |
     +--> tarski_between_outer_connectivity  [AXIOM]
     |
     v
connectivity results
     |
     v
collinearity transitivity.
```

This is the first clearly localized piece of foundational debt in the current
Tarski interface.

The surrounding results may be theorems, but their complete dependency chain
must retain this explicit assumption until it is formally reconstructed.

---

## 10. Tarski Collinearity Theory

Using the derived betweenness results, the interface develops structural
properties of `TarskiCollinear`.

These include permutation and transitivity behavior required by later
geometric constructions.

The important architectural point is:

```text
Between
   |
derived order theory
   |
TarskiCollinear
   |
derived collinearity theory.
```

Thus collinearity is not introduced as a new primitive relation on the Tarski
route.

It remains reducible to betweenness and equality, modulo the current deferred
order result.

---

## 11. Tarski Parallelograms

The interface introduces a Tarski-specific parallelogram notion:

```lean
TarskiParallelogram.
```

Its structure is based on the existence of a common midpoint of the two
diagonals.

Schematically:

```text
M is midpoint of AC
        +
M is midpoint of BD
        |
        v
TarskiParallelogram A B C D.
```

This is particularly natural in the Tarski language because midpoint itself
has already been reduced to:

```text
Between + Congruent.
```

Thus the parallelogram concept remains point-based and does not require
primitive parallelism.

---

## 12. Strict Tarski Parallelism

The interface also defines

```lean
TarskiParallelStrict.
```

This is a Tarski-specific notion of strict parallelism expressed through
nondegenerate lines and absence of an intersection point.

Conceptually:

```text
two nondegenerate point-defined lines
            +
no common point
            |
            v
TarskiParallelStrict.
```

This is important architecturally.

The Tarski route does not need to take the shared `Geo.Parallel` relation as
its foundational notion of parallelism.

It can develop parallel geometry internally from point relations.

---

## 13. Further Deferred Affine Results

Beyond the derived order theory, the current interface contains several
additional explicit assumptions used in the developing affine theory.

Among them are declarations corresponding to:

```text
tarski_central_symmetry_congruent
tarski_central_symmetry_parallel
tarski_parallel_congruent_parallelogram_cases
tarski_midpoint_unique
tarski_parallelogram_opposite_parallel
tarski_parallelogram_of_two_parallel_pairs.
```

These declarations should not be confused with the primitive Tarski axioms in
`TarskiAxioms.lean`.

They are deferred mathematical results in the current interface.

Thus the architecture has two different kinds of assumptions:

```text
TarskiAxioms
    |
    | genuine foundational axiom classes
    v
TarskiInterface
    |
    +--> derived theory
    |
    `--> deferred results still represented as axioms.
```

This distinction is essential for assessing the current strength of later
proofs.

---

## 14. The Current Logical Boundary

The present Tarski route can therefore be summarized as:

```text
HilbertCore
     |
     | Point / Between / Congruent
     v
TarskiAxioms
     |
     +--> TarskiNeutral
     |
     +--> TarskiPlane
     |
     `--> TarskiEuclideanPlane
              |
              v
        TarskiInterface
              |
              +--> TarskiCollinear
              +--> TarskiIsMidpoint
              +--> derived order theory
              +--> derived collinearity theory
              +--> TarskiParallelogram
              +--> TarskiParallelStrict
              |
              `--> deferred affine results
```

Separately, the bridge

```text
TarskiGeometryBaseBridge
```

connects selected Tarski notions with the shared synthetic language.

This is the correct current architectural boundary.

---

## 15. Comparison with the Suppes Route

The contrast with the Suppes reconstruction is useful.

The active Suppes foundation begins with primitive operations:

```text
Mid
Dbl
Col.
```

The Tarski foundation begins lower:

```text
Between
Congruent.
```

Consequently, notions such as midpoint and collinearity must themselves be
constructed on the Tarski route.

Schematically:

```text
Suppes

Mid / Dbl / Col
      |
      v
affine theory
```

versus

```text
Tarski

Between / Congruent
        |
        v
collinearity / midpoint
        |
        v
affine theory.
```

This makes the Tarski route particularly useful for studying how much affine
geometry can be reconstructed from a very small point-based language.

---

## 16. Comparison with the Hilbert Route

The Tarski route also differs sharply from the Hilbert route.

Hilbert geometry begins with a richer geometric vocabulary involving
incidence, order, congruence, and eventually angles and parallelism.

Tarski geometry uses only points together with betweenness and congruence as
its central primitive relations.

Schematically:

```text
Hilbert                       Tarski

Point / Line                  Point
OnLine                        Between
Between                       Congruent
Congruent
angle structure
     |                           |
     v                           v
Hilbert axiom groups          Tarski axiom hierarchy.
```

The later convergence of these routes at a synthetic interface is therefore
a genuine interpretation problem rather than a superficial change of theorem
names.

---

## 17. What Is Already Reconstructed

The current Tarski interface is not merely a collection of compatibility
axioms.

A significant amount of theory has already been formally derived, including:

```text
elementary congruence consequences
betweenness symmetry
betweenness transitivity
connectivity machinery
collinearity transformations
midpoint definitions
point-based affine structures.
```

This matters when evaluating the remaining assumptions.

The foundational debt is increasingly localized in identifiable theorems
rather than spread throughout the entire route.

---

## 18. What Remains Open

The main reduction problem is now clear.

Each explicit assumption in `TarskiInterface.lean` should eventually be
classified as one of:

```text
a theorem derivable from TarskiNeutral,
a theorem requiring TarskiPlane,
a theorem requiring TarskiEuclideanPlane,
an interpretation theorem for the shared interface,
or genuinely additional structure.
```

In particular, the current deferred results concerning:

```text
outer connectivity,
central symmetry,
parallelism,
midpoint uniqueness,
parallelogram recognition
```

must be traced to their minimal Tarski assumptions.

This is the Tarski analogue of the foundational-debt analysis already carried
out for the Suppes route.

---

## 19. Conclusion

The current Tarski route has a clear foundational hierarchy:

```text
TarskiNeutral
      |
      v
TarskiPlane
      |
      v
TarskiEuclideanPlane.
```

Above it, `TarskiInterface.lean` reconstructs a substantial point-based
geometry:

```text
Between / Congruent
        |
        v
collinearity / midpoint
        |
        v
order and affine theory
        |
        v
parallelograms / strict parallelism.
```

The route is logically independent of the Hilbert axiom classes even though
it reuses primitive vocabulary from `HilbertCore`.

At the same time, the interface is not yet completely reduced to the Tarski
axioms. Several general order and affine results remain represented as
explicit assumptions.

The important result of this reconstruction is therefore not merely that a
Tarski route exists.

It is that the boundary between:

```text
primitive Tarski axioms,
formally derived Tarski theory,
deferred Tarski theorems,
and shared-interface bridges
```

is now visible.

The next stage should inspect the Tarski Midsegment development and determine
which portion of this theory is actually required by the route to Finlay's
proof.
