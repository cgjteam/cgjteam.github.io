# Log 024 - Finlay's Proof on the Hilbert Route

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Project:** CGJteam Lab  
**Status:** Finlay Proof Reconstruction

---

## 1. Overview

`FinlayProof.lean` contains the final synthetic argument on the Hilbert
route.

The theorem proves that for every noncollinear triangle `ABC` there
exist three medians and a point common to all three.

Its formal statement is:

```lean
theorem Finlay
    (A B C : Geo.Point)
    (hABC : not Collinear Geo A B C) :
    exists E F G D : Geo.Point,
      IsMedian Geo B E A C /\
      IsMedian Geo C F A B /\
      Collinear Geo B G E /\
      Collinear Geo C G F /\
      IsMedian Geo A D B C /\
      Collinear Geo A G D
```

The proof is constructive in the synthetic sense used by the library.

The points

```text
E
F
G
P
D
```

are not assumed as part of the theorem statement.

They are produced successively by the Hilbert geometry developed in the
lower layers.

The proof has two parts:

```text
construction of the Finlay configuration
                |
                v
five-step Finlay argument
```

The resulting structure closely follows the classical geometric proof.

---

## 2. Foundational Context

The theorem is stated under:

```lean
[HilbertIncidence Geo]
[HilbertEuclideanPlane Geo]
```

Since `HilbertEuclideanPlane` extends the lower Hilbert classes, the
proof has access to the full chain:

```text
HilbertPlaneIncidence
        |
        v
HilbertOrder
        |
        v
HilbertCongruence
        |
        v
HilbertEuclideanPlane
```

The proof does not use this strength uniformly.

Many construction and transport steps belong to the neutral part of
the theory, while the Euclidean level becomes essential in the
parallelogram machinery used later in the argument.

---

## 3. Initial Triangle Data

The only geometric hypothesis supplied to the theorem is:

```lean
hABC : not Collinear Geo A B C
```

From this the proof derives the required nondegeneracy of the sides.

First the order of the last two vertices is changed:

```lean
have hACB : not Collinear Geo A C B
```

Then:

```lean
have hAB : A != B
have hAC : A != C
```

are obtained from noncollinearity.

Thus side nondegeneracy is not added as a separate assumption.

The dependency is:

```text
not Collinear A B C
        |
        +--> A != B
        |
        `--> A != C
```

---

## 4. Construction of the First Two Midpoints

The points `E` and `F` are constructed using:

```lean
HilbertMidpointExists
```

giving:

```text
E midpoint of AC
F midpoint of AB
```

More precisely, the construction produces Hilbert midpoints, so the
available information includes betweenness:

```text
A-E-C
A-F-B
```

together with the corresponding segment congruences.

Schematically:

```text
A != C
   |
   v
HilbertMidpointExists
   |
   v
E midpoint of AC

A != B
   |
   v
HilbertMidpointExists
   |
   v
F midpoint of AB
```

The midpoint construction belongs to the neutral Hilbert geometry
available at the `HilbertCongruence` level.

---

## 5. Construction of the Intersection G

The first two medians are not assumed to intersect.

Their intersection is constructed.

The proof applies:

```lean
hilbert_inner_pasch_strong
```

to the triangle `BAE`.

The midpoint information supplies the required betweenness data, while
the original noncollinearity of `ABC` is used to prove that `BAE` is a
genuine triangle.

The result is a point `G` satisfying:

```text
C-G-F
B-G-E
```

and therefore:

```text
C, G, F are collinear
B, G, E are collinear.
```

The construction is:

```text
midpoint E of AC
midpoint F of AB
        |
        v
strong inner Pasch
        |
        v
        G
       / \
      /   \
   B-G-E C-G-F
```

Thus the concurrency point of the first two medians is itself produced
from the Hilbert order geometry.

No intersection point is assumed in the statement of Finlay's theorem.

---

## 6. Nondegeneracy of the Two Auxiliary Triangles

The Midsegment Theorem will later be applied to the triangles:

```text
AGF
AGE
```

The proof therefore establishes:

```lean
hGFA : not Collinear Geo G F A
hGEA : not Collinear Geo G E A
```

These are not new hypotheses.

Each is derived by assuming collinearity and using uniqueness of the
line through two distinct points to force the original vertices
`A`, `B`, and `C` onto one line.

The contradiction is always with:

```lean
hABC : not Collinear Geo A B C.
```

Thus the auxiliary triangles required by Step 1 are derived from the
original triangle rather than postulated independently.

---

## 7. Construction of the Auxiliary Point P

Finlay's auxiliary point `P` is constructed beyond `G` on the line
`AG`.

First the proof establishes:

```lean
A != G.
```

It then applies:

```lean
ExtendSegmentBeyond Geo A G hAG
```

and obtains a point `P` such that:

```text
A-G-P
AG congruent GP.
```

Consequently `G` is the strict Hilbert midpoint of `AP`:

```lean
have hG : HilbertIsMidpoint Geo G A P :=
  <hAGPbetween, hAG_GP>
```

The construction is exactly the classical Finlay construction:

```text
A ----- G ----- P
      AG = GP
```

At this point the complete auxiliary configuration required by the
five-step argument has been constructed.

---

## 8. The Constructed Configuration

Before Step 1 begins, the proof has obtained:

```text
E midpoint of AC
F midpoint of AB

B-G-E
C-G-F

A-G-P
AG = GP
```

Schematically:

```text
             A
            / \
           E   F
          /     \
         C       B
          \     /
           \   /
             G
             |
             P
```

The diagram is only schematic; the formal proof uses incidence,
betweenness, congruence, and parallelism rather than coordinates.

The important point is that all auxiliary objects have been produced
from the single initial assumption that `ABC` is a triangle.

---

## 9. Step 1: Two Midsegment Applications

The first mathematical step applies the Midsegment Theorem twice.

In triangle `ABP`:

```text
F is midpoint of AB
G is midpoint of AP
```

hence:

```text
FG || BP.
```

In triangle `ACP`:

```text
E is midpoint of AC
G is midpoint of AP
```

hence:

```text
EG || CP.
```

The Lean calls are:

```lean
MidsegmentTheorem Geo A B P F G ...
MidsegmentTheorem Geo A C P E G ...
```

These parallelisms are then transported along the already known
collinearities:

```text
C,F,G collinear
B,E,G collinear.
```

Using:

```lean
ParallelCollinearLeft
```

the proof obtains:

```text
CG || BP
BG || CP.
```

Thus Step 1 has the exact structure:

```text
MidsegmentTheorem
        |
        +--> FG || BP
        |
        +--> EG || CP
        |
        v
parallel transport along collinear lines
        |
        +--> CG || BP
        |
        `--> BG || CP
```

This is the geometric input required for the parallelogram.

---

## 10. Step 2: Recognition of BPCG as a Parallelogram

The two parallelisms from Step 1 are reoriented:

```text
CG || BP  ->  BP || CG
BG || CP  ->  BG || PC.
```

The interface theorems used are:

```lean
ParallelSymmetry
ParallelSwapSecondLine
```

The proof then applies:

```lean
ParallelogramOfParallel
```

and obtains:

```lean
IsParallelogram Geo B P C G.
```

Mathematically:

```text
BP || CG
BG || PC
    |
    v
BPCG is a parallelogram.
```

Because `IsParallelogram` is defined by the two pairs of opposite sides
being parallel, this recognition step is essentially definitional.

No new Euclidean theorem is needed merely to recognize the
parallelogram.

---

## 11. Step 3: Construction of D

The point `D` is not assumed as the intersection of `AP` and `BC`.

It is constructed from the parallelogram.

The theorem:

```lean
ParallelogramDiagonalIntersectionExists
```

applied to `BPCG` produces `D` with:

```text
B-D-C
P-D-G.
```

These are the two diagonal crossing relations of the parallelogram.

The proof then combines the diagonal relation

```text
P-D-G
```

with the already known collinearity/order information on the line
through `A`, `G`, and `P`, using:

```lean
hilbert_between_inner_trans
```

to obtain the required order relation on the line `AP`.

This is converted through the same-ray machinery into collinearity:

```text
A, D, P are collinear.
```

At the same time:

```text
B-D-C
```

gives:

```text
B, D, C are collinear.
```

The proof packages these two facts as:

```lean
IsIntersection Geo A P B C D.
```

Thus Step 3 realizes the geometric statement:

```text
D = AP intersect BC.
```

The formal route is:

```text
BPCG parallelogram
        |
        v
ParallelogramDiagonalIntersectionExists
        |
        +--> B-D-C
        |
        `--> P-D-G
                |
                + A-G-P
                |
                v
          D lies on AP
```

The intersection is therefore constructed rather than postulated.

---

## 12. Step 4: Diagonals of the Parallelogram

The decisive parallelogram step is:

```lean
ParallelogramDiagonals
```

In the current interface this theorem is available at the
`HilbertEuclideanPlane` level.

The theorem is applied to:

```text
BPCG
```

with diagonal intersection `D`.

It yields:

```text
D is the midpoint of BC.
```

In Lean:

```lean
have hMidpoint : IsMidpoint Geo D B C := by
  exact
    ParallelogramDiagonals
      Geo
      B P C G D
      hParallelogram
      hBC
      hPG
```

This is the point at which the classical theorem

```text
the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other
```

enters Finlay's argument.

The dependency is:

```text
BPCG parallelogram
        |
        +--> D lies on BC
        |
        +--> D lies on PG
        |
        v
ParallelogramDiagonals
        |
        v
D midpoint of BC.
```

Unlike the recognition of the parallelogram in Step 2, this is a
substantial theorem of the Euclidean parallelogram theory.

---

## 13. The Third Median

Once `D` has been proved to be the midpoint of `BC`, the third median is
immediate.

The interface theorem:

```lean
MidpointMedian
```

packages the midpoint relation as:

```lean
IsMedian Geo A D B C.
```

Thus:

```text
D midpoint of BC
        |
        v
AD is a median of triangle ABC.
```

No additional geometric construction is required.

---

## 14. Step 5: The Third Median Passes Through G

The final geometric task is to show:

```text
A, G, D are collinear.
```

The proof already knows:

```text
A,G,P collinear
P,D,G collinear.
```

Using:

```lean
CollinearTrans
```

it obtains:

```text
A,G,D collinear.
```

Thus the third median `AD` passes through the point `G`, which was
constructed earlier as the intersection of the first two medians.

The final step is therefore:

```text
A,G,P collinear
P,D,G collinear
        |
        v
CollinearTrans
        |
        v
A,G,D collinear.
```

This completes the concurrency argument.

---

## 15. Packaging the First Two Medians

The points `E` and `F` were originally constructed as strict Hilbert
midpoints.

Before returning the final theorem, the proof converts them to the
public midpoint notion using:

```lean
midpoint_of_hilbert
```

and then applies:

```lean
MidpointMedian.
```

This yields:

```text
BE is a median of triangle ABC
CF is a median of triangle ABC.
```

Together with Step 4 and Step 5, the final result is:

```text
BE is a median
CF is a median
AD is a median

G lies on BE
G lies on CF
G lies on AD.
```

Hence the three medians are concurrent.

## 16. The Five-Step Mathematical Argument

After the auxiliary configuration has been constructed, the proof has a
very compact mathematical structure.

```text
Step 1
Midsegment Theorem twice
        |
        v
CG || BP
BG || CP

Step 2
two pairs of opposite sides parallel
        |
        v
BPCG parallelogram

Step 3
diagonals of BPCG intersect
        |
        v
D lies on BC and AP

Step 4
diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other
        |
        v
D midpoint of BC
        |
        v
AD is the third median

Step 5
A,G,P and P,D,G are collinear
        |
        v
A,G,D are collinear
        |
        v
G lies on the third median.
```

The Lean implementation preserves this mathematical decomposition
explicitly.

## 17. Construction Layer and Proof Layer

The formalization makes visible a distinction that is often suppressed
in a paper proof.

Before the five Finlay steps can begin, the required configuration must
itself be constructed.

The full architecture is therefore:

```text
not Collinear A B C
        |
        +--> construct E midpoint of AC
        |
        +--> construct F midpoint of AB
        |
        +--> construct G by inner Pasch
        |
        +--> construct P beyond G on AG
        |
        v
Finlay configuration
        |
        v
Step 1: Midsegment
        |
        v
Step 2: Parallelogram
        |
        v
Step 3: Diagonal intersection D
        |
        v
Step 4: D midpoint of BC
        |
        v
Step 5: G lies on AD
        |
        v
three concurrent medians.
```

This separation is one of the main structural features of the current
formal proof.

## 18. Where Euclidean Strength Enters

The theorem is globally stated under:

```lean
[HilbertEuclideanPlane Geo]
```

but its internal operations occupy different levels of the Hilbert
hierarchy.

The initial construction uses midpoint existence, incidence, order,
Pasch, segment extension, and collinearity arguments.

The recognition of `BPCG` as a parallelogram is definitional once the
two pairs of parallel sides are known.

The stronger parallelogram theory appears when the proof uses:

```lean
ParallelogramDiagonalIntersectionExists
ParallelogramDiagonals
```

In the current interface these declarations are exposed at the
`HilbertEuclideanPlane` level. This records the dependency of the
present implementation; it does not by itself prove that both results
require Group IV in every possible Hilbert derivation.

Thus the logical profile of the proof is not:

```text
every step = Euclidean axiom IV
```

but rather:

```text
neutral Hilbert geometry
        |
        +--> construct midpoints
        +--> construct G by Pasch
        +--> extend AG to P
        +--> incidence and collinearity
        +--> parallel transport
        |
        v
parallelogram configuration
        |
        v
Euclidean parallelogram theory
        |
        +--> diagonal intersection
        `--> diagonal bisection
        |
        v
Finlay conclusion.
```

In the current implementation, the Euclidean-level dependency is
therefore concentrated in the classical parallelogram machinery rather
than distributed uniformly throughout the proof.

## 20. Conclusion

The current Hilbert formalization of Finlay's proof is not merely a
verification of the final concurrency statement.

It reconstructs the complete synthetic configuration from the
noncollinearity of the original triangle.

The points required by the proof are produced internally:

```text
E and F  by midpoint existence,
G        by strong inner Pasch,
P        by segment extension,
D        by the diagonal-intersection theorem.
```

Once these points exist, the central mathematical argument has the
classical five-step form:

```text
midsegment
    |
parallel sides
    |
parallelogram
    |
diagonal intersection
    |
diagonal bisection
    |
third median
    |
concurrency.
```

The proof also reveals the logical division inside the Hilbert route.

Much of the construction belongs to neutral Hilbert geometry. In the
current interface, the remaining Euclidean-level dependency is
concentrated in the stronger theory of parallelograms, especially the
declarations concerning diagonal intersection and bisection.

The resulting architecture is therefore:

```text
Hilbert foundational geometry
        |
        v
construction of Finlay's configuration
        |
        v
Midsegment geometry
        |
        v
parallelogram recognition
        |
        v
Euclidean parallelogram theory
        |
        v
median concurrency
```

This makes `FinlayProof.lean` a compact synthetic endpoint of the
Hilbert route: the final theorem is expressed in the language of
midpoints, medians, collinearity, parallelism, and parallelograms, while
the lower layers carry the foundational Hilbert dependencies.
