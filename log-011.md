# Log 011 - Primitive Predicates Become Definitions

## Summary

The reverse-engineering work produced its first concrete refactoring result.

Several notions that had initially been represented as primitive predicates could instead be introduced by definitions built from more elementary geometric relations. Once these definitions were introduced, some declarations previously treated as axioms became immediate theorems.

The important result of this experiment was not a claim that the final primitive basis of the geometry had been found. It was narrower:

> the current formalization contained notions whose primitive status was unnecessary.

The complete Finlay formalization continued to compile after the refactoring.

---

## Midpoint

The first case was midpoint.

Instead of treating

```lean
IsMidpoint M A B
```

as an independent primitive predicate, midpoint could be defined through the geometric conditions already available in the theory.

Once midpoint became a definition, elementary consequences such as collinearity and congruence no longer needed to be postulated independently.

Schematically:

```text
more elementary relations
        |
        v
IsMidpoint
        |
        +--> midpoint_collinear
        |
        +--> midpoint_congruent
```

Thus declarations that had previously behaved like axioms became projections or theorems derived from the definition.

---

## Median

The same phenomenon occurred with the notion of a median.

A median can be represented through the midpoint of the opposite side. Therefore `IsMedian` did not need to remain an independent primitive predicate.

The relation between midpoint and median could be organized as:

```text
IsMidpoint
     |
     v
IsMedian
```

and the result expressing that a line through a vertex and the midpoint of the opposite side is a median became a theorem rather than an additional axiom.

---

## Parallelogram

A similar simplification was possible for parallelograms.

Instead of treating `IsParallelogram` as an independent primitive notion, it could be defined by the appropriate pairs of opposite parallel sides.

Schematically:

```text
parallel relations
        |
        v
IsParallelogram
```

Consequently, the result constructing a parallelogram from those parallel relations became a theorem following from the definition.

This was particularly important for the Finlay proof, where the auxiliary quadrilateral is recognized precisely through its opposite parallel sides.

---

## What Changed

The refactoring therefore moved three concepts from primitive status to definitional status:

- `IsMidpoint`,
- `IsMedian`,
- `IsParallelogram`.

Correspondingly, several declarations no longer needed to be treated as independent axioms, including results expressing:

- collinearity associated with a midpoint,
- congruence associated with a midpoint,
- the passage from midpoint to median,
- recognition of a parallelogram from parallel sides.

The exact Lean declarations remained part of the evolving implementation, but the mathematical result was more important than the particular file organization:

> definitions can remove unnecessary primitive assumptions.

---

## What Did Not Change

The overall strategy of the Finlay proof did not need to be redesigned.

The same high-level route remained:

```text
midpoint information
        |
        v
Midsegment Theorem
        |
        v
parallel relations
        |
        v
parallelogram
        |
        v
diagonal bisection
        |
        v
third median
```

What changed was the logical status of some concepts appearing along this route.

This distinction is important.

The mathematical proof structure can remain stable even while the foundational organization beneath it is simplified.

---

## A First Reduction of the Primitive Layer

This refactoring was the first concrete confirmation that reverse engineering could reduce the primitive layer of the library.

It also showed why declarations should not be classified solely by the role they happened to play during the construction of a proof.

A notion may initially be introduced as primitive because that is convenient during development. Later analysis may reveal that it is definable from notions already present.

The relevant question is therefore not:

> Was this declaration originally introduced as an axiom?

but:

> Does it need to remain an axiom?

For `IsMidpoint`, `IsMedian`, and `IsParallelogram`, the answer at this stage was no.

---

## Caution

This result should not be interpreted as a complete reduction of the geometry to a minimal axiomatic basis.

Other declarations still required analysis, and some apparently elementary interface results could hide substantial dependencies.

The experiment established a method:

1. inspect the formal definition or declaration,
2. identify more elementary relations already available,
3. replace unnecessary primitive predicates by definitions,
4. derive their elementary consequences as theorems,
5. verify that the existing formal proofs still compile.

This method would become increasingly important in the subsequent analysis of the geometry library.

---

## Conclusion

Log 011 records the first successful reduction produced by reverse engineering.

Three high-level geometric predicates were moved from primitive status to definitions, and several associated axioms became derived results.

The Finlay proof survived the change.

This provided concrete evidence that the architecture of the library could be simplified without changing the mathematical strategy of the proof.
