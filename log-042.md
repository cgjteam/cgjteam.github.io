# Log 042 --- Book Zero and the Search for a Working Layer of Classical Geometry

## 1. Motivation

One of the questions present from the beginning of the CGJteamLab
project has been how a library of classical synthetic geometry should be
organized.

The project deliberately did not begin by designing a complete geometry
library. Instead, the first experiments started from individual
classical proofs and asked what mathematical structure was actually
required to formalize them.

This methodological choice appeared already in the first development
logs. The basic principle was that reusable mathematical structure
should emerge from proofs rather than be imposed in advance.

The Finlay experiment made this question concrete. A relatively short
classical argument required a growing collection of intermediate results
concerning collinearity, betweenness, congruence, parallelism,
midpoints, parallelograms, and intersections. Some of these results
clearly belonged to the particular proof, while others behaved as
general-purpose geometric operations.

This led to a recurring architectural question:

> Where should the boundary between axioms, reusable geometric
> infrastructure, and proof-specific arguments be placed?

The Hilbert route provided one provisional answer in the form

``` text
Hilbert axioms
      |
      v
Hilbert interface
      |
      v
classical proofs
```

but the interface was necessarily constructed incrementally from the
requirements of the proofs being studied. Although this was consistent
with the original methodology of the project, it left open a more
difficult question:

> How large should such an interface be, and which elementary geometric
> results naturally belong to it?

The present experiment approaches this question using the formal
reconstruction of Euclid developed by Michael Beeson, Julien Narboux,
and Freek Wiedijk.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. The source: Proof-checking Euclid

Michael Beeson, Julien Narboux, and Freek Wiedijk developed a formal
reconstruction of Euclid in the project described in

> Michael Beeson, Julien Narboux, Freek Wiedijk,\
> *Proof-checking Euclid*,\
> Annals of Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence 85 (2019), 213--257.

The associated project materials include a theorem inventory and `.prf`
proof files containing the formal proofs used in their reconstruction.

The material used in the present experiment was obtained from Michael
Beeson's project page:

`https://www.michaelbeeson.com/research/FoundationsOfGeometry/index.php?include=CheckingEuclid`

An important part of their reconstruction is **Book Zero**, a collection
of preliminary results established before the propositions of Euclid's
Book I are formalized.

Book Zero arose from the requirements of the BNW project of
proof-checking Euclid. Its motivation and role must therefore be
understood in the terms given by Beeson, Narboux, and Wiedijk
themselves. It should not be retrospectively described as an
implementation of the CGJteamLab architecture.

In particular, the interpretation developed below --- Book Zero as a
possible supplement to the Hilbert foundation or as part of a working
interface for classical geometry --- is an interpretation made inside
CGJteamLab. It is not terminology or an architectural claim made by BNW.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. CGJteamLab interpretation of Book Zero

From the point of view of CGJteamLab, Book Zero can be interpreted as
something close to a **working supplement to the Hilbert foundation**.

Hilbert's axioms provide a foundation for geometry. They are not
intended to enumerate every elementary fact routinely used in ordinary
synthetic reasoning. Between the foundational axioms and a classical
proof there is therefore a substantial layer of derived mathematics.

Schematically,

``` text
Hilbert axioms
      |
      |  elementary derived geometry
      |
      v
classical synthetic proofs
```

The experiments with the Finlay proof encountered precisely this layer.
The first CGJteamLab interfaces were attempts to construct enough of it
to make classical proofs manageable.

The importance of the BNW work is that they investigated this territory
in practice on a much larger classical corpus. Their formal
reconstruction of Euclid forced many normally implicit steps to become
explicit and formally justified.

Book Zero is the first clearly separated collection of such preliminary
results.

This makes it valuable for our purposes.

Instead of continuing to guess how the Hilbert interface should grow, we
can perform a controlled experiment:

> implement the Book Zero proofs of Beeson, Narboux, and Wiedijk over
> the CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation and observe what geometric
> infrastructure is required.

However, Book Zero should not yet be identified with the complete
interface we are looking for. The full BNW formalization contains
substantially more derived mathematics than Book Zero alone.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. The larger BNW corpus

This point is essential for the present experiment.

Euclid's Book I contains 48 propositions. In the published BNW account,
their formal reconstruction of Book I contains **235 theorems** in
total.

The additional results are not all Book Zero.

BNW distinguish several kinds of additional theorems, including:

-   the preliminary results collected in Book Zero;
-   results used implicitly by Euclid but not stated as propositions;
-   more substantial results required to fill logical gaps in Euclid's
    arguments;
-   variants of Euclid's propositions needed elsewhere in the
    development.

Thus the structure exposed by proof-checking Euclid is richer than

``` text
axioms
  |
  v
Book Zero
  |
  v
48 propositions of Book I
```

A more realistic picture is

``` text
foundational axioms
        |
        v
Book Zero
        |
        v
further derived Euclidean lemmas
        |
        v
propositions of Book I
```

The boundaries between the last two levels need not be perfectly sharp.
The important fact for CGJteamLab is that BNW provide a much larger
proof corpus from which reusable geometric mechanisms can eventually be
studied.

Book Zero is therefore the **first layer being reconstructed**, not a
priori the final answer to the interface problem.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. The current experiment

The present experiment does **not** consist in designing a new Book
Zero.

Nor do we merely copy the statements of the BNW theorems and postulate
them as an interface.

For each Book Zero result we examine the corresponding theorem and
`.prf` proof, translate its geometric content into the language of the
CGJteamLab Hilbert development, and attempt to prove it from

``` text
HilbertAxioms
+
existing HilbertInterface results
+
earlier reconstructed Book Zero results.
```

The current dependency structure is therefore approximately

``` text
              HilbertAxioms
                    |
                    v
             HilbertInterface
                    |
                    v
          reconstructed Book Zero
```

The crucial point is that the sequence of results is not chosen ad hoc
from the requirements of one CGJteamLab proof. It is supplied by an
independent formal reconstruction of Euclid.

The experiment therefore tests the existing Hilbert infrastructure
against an externally developed corpus of elementary synthetic geometry.

At a later stage, the same method can be extended beyond Book Zero to
the additional lemmas appearing in the full BNW reconstruction of Book
I.

## 6. Current state: Book Zero 1--32

At the time of this log, the reconstruction has reached **Book Zero
theorem 32**.

The early part of the sequence contains many apparently small results:
reversals of congruence, elementary consequences of betweenness,
permutations and propagation of collinearity, non-collinearity lemmas,
and related structural facts.

Their importance is not their individual mathematical depth. They form
part of the routine vocabulary required by later arguments.

By the time the reconstruction reaches the twenties, the cumulative
effect becomes visible. Earlier results begin to function as a proof
layer rather than as isolated helper lemmas.

Several recent examples are particularly informative.

### Book Zero 27 --- `NChelper`

This result propagates non-collinearity when the pair of points
determining a line is replaced by another pair on the same line.

Its Lean proof uses the previously reconstructed collinearity machinery
rather than returning directly to the incidence axioms.

### Book Zero 28 --- `fiveline`

The primitive five-line principle is converted into a more convenient
theorem applicable to a general collinear configuration.

The proof handles the possible orderings and degenerate cases
internally. The resulting theorem is much closer to the form in which a
synthetic geometer wants to use the five-line argument.

This is a characteristic example of the distinction between a
foundational principle and a working theorem.

### Book Zero 29 --- `twolines`

The uniqueness of the intersection of two distinct lines is obtained
from the developed collinearity theory.

Again, the proof is expressed largely through accumulated derived
infrastructure.

### Book Zero 30 --- `lessthancongruence`

This was a substantially more demanding test.

The theorem transports strict comparison of segments through congruence
of the larger segment:

``` text
AB < CD
CD ≅ EF
--------
AB < EF
```

A naive shortcut through the existing Lean infrastructure was
insufficient. The successful proof followed the structure of the BNW
`.prf` argument, using segment construction, addition of segments, order
properties, the five-line principle, and preservation of betweenness.

This is important evidence for the experiment. The reconstruction is not
succeeding merely because the first Book Zero statements happen to
coincide with trivial consequences already present in
`HilbertInterface`.

A nontrivial BNW proof could be reconstructed over the present Hilbert
foundation.

### Book Zero 31 --- `trichotomy1`

The next theorem states that two non-null segments which are not shorter
than one another must be congruent.

Its proof uses the already available segment-order infrastructure and
theorem 30. Thus a theorem whose reconstruction required significant
work immediately becomes a reusable component of the next argument.

### Book Zero 32 --- `lessthancongruence2`

The corresponding transport rule for the smaller segment reduces
directly to an already available segment-order lemma.

This contrast is instructive:

``` text
#30  substantial derived argument
#31  uses the resulting order infrastructure
#32  becomes a short interface-level consequence
```

This is precisely the behaviour expected from a useful intermediate
mathematical layer.

## 7. Relation to the original project strategy

The significance of the experiment is best understood by returning to
the earliest CGJteamLab logs.

The initial strategy was not to specify a complete hierarchy of
geometric abstractions and then formalize examples inside it. Instead,
classical proofs were used to discover reusable mathematical structure.

That strategy produced the first interfaces, but it also exposed its own
limitation: a single proof can reveal only the portion of elementary
geometry that it happens to use.

The BNW corpus offers a way to extend the same methodology without
abandoning it.

The source is still **actual proof practice**.

The difference is the size and systematic character of the corpus.

Finlay supplied one classical proof from which reusable mechanisms could
be extracted. BNW supply a large formal reconstruction generated by the
detailed proof-checking of Euclid's Book I.

Thus the current work is not a departure from the original CGJteamLab
strategy. It is an opportunity to test that strategy against a
substantially larger and independently developed body of proofs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. What has been learned so far

After 32 reconstructed Book Zero theorems, several observations can
already be made.

First, the existing Hilbert foundation has been strong enough to support
the reconstruction so far.

Second, the existing `HilbertInterface` is useful but was developed from
a much smaller proof corpus. Book Zero provides an external sequence
against which its coverage can be tested and extended.

Third, the reconstructed Book Zero results increasingly compose with one
another. Later proofs can use earlier derived results instead of
repeatedly descending to the foundational axioms.

Fourth, the experiment confirms the practical importance of a layer
between foundational principles and ordinary classical arguments:

``` text
foundational principles
        |
        v
derived synthetic machinery
        |
        v
classical geometric proofs
```

What remains open is the **extent and internal structure** of that
middle layer.

Book Zero clearly supplies part of it, but the full BNW development
shows that Book Zero is not the whole story. Their reconstruction
contains many further derived results beyond the preliminary Book Zero
collection.

For the original CGJteamLab interface problem, those later results may
be at least as important as Book Zero itself.

## 9. The next stages of the experiment

The immediate task is to complete the reconstruction of Book Zero.

That will answer a limited but important question:

> Can the BNW preliminary geometry be reconstructed over the CGJteamLab
> Hilbert foundation and existing interface?

The next stage is broader.

Rather than moving directly from completed Book Zero to the assumption
that the desired interface has been found, we should inspect the **full
BNW theorem corpus associated with Book I**.

The questions then become:

1.  Which additional lemmas are used repeatedly across different
    Euclidean propositions?
2.  Which are merely local repairs of a particular Euclidean proof?
3.  Which express general synthetic mechanisms independent of the
    proposition in which they first occur?
4.  Which results belong naturally with Book Zero, even if BNW
    introduced them later?
5.  Which results should remain theorem-level consequences rather than
    become part of a reusable interface?

This is much closer to the original CGJteamLab question.

The desired object is not necessarily Book Zero itself. It may instead
be a larger structure extracted from the whole proof corpus:

``` text
Hilbert foundation
        |
        v
Book Zero
        |
        +--------------------+
        |                    |
        v                    v
further reusable       proof-specific
synthetic lemmas       derived lemmas
        |
        v
classical geometry interface
        |
        v
Euclid and other classical proofs
```

This diagram is still a hypothesis. The purpose of the next stages is to
determine whether the BNW corpus actually supports such a separation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Working hypothesis

The working hypothesis should therefore be stated cautiously.

Book Zero is not being assumed to be the complete classical geometry
interface sought by CGJteamLab.

Rather:

> The Beeson--Narboux--Wiedijk reconstruction of Euclid provides an
> independently developed proof corpus from which the working layer of
> classical synthetic geometry can be investigated systematically. Book
> Zero is the first, explicitly separated collection of elementary
> results in that corpus and therefore the natural starting point for
> the experiment.

From the CGJteamLab perspective, Book Zero can be viewed as a practical
supplement to the Hilbert foundation: a body of derived geometry needed
before substantial Euclidean proofs can be carried out.

But the broader goal is larger.

The original project asked whether reusable geometric structure could be
**extracted from actual classical proofs rather than designed in
advance**. The BNW corpus now gives us an opportunity to perform that
extraction on a scale much larger than the original Finlay experiment.

The reconstruction of Book Zero 1--32 is therefore not evidence that the
final interface has already been found.

It is the first substantial step in testing whether the complete BNW
proof corpus can reveal what that interface should contain.
