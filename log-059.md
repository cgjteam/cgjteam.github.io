# Log 059 --- E^3 Revisited: Wyler Flats and the Dimension-Free Incidence Bridge

## 1. Why revisit a completed A3 construction?

Log 058 ended with a complete three-dimensional Coxeter system of type A3:

```text
regular tetrahedron
        |
        v
three mirror planes
        |
        v
three plane reflections
        |
        v
A3 relations
        |
        v
generated group ~= S4
```

That already settles the global geometric and group-theoretic problem.

There is, however, a second question.

Why do the three pairwise Coxeter labels have the values

$$
m_{12}=3,\qquad m_{23}=3,\qquad m_{13}=2?
$$

The global proof answers this through the action on the four tetrahedral
vertices and four-point rigidity.

The Wyler reinterpretation asks for a more local explanation.

For each pair of mirrors it separates the geometry into

```text
common fixed flat
        +
active two-dimensional section.
```

This does not replace the A3 proof.

It reveals the pairwise geometry hidden inside it.

## 2. "Wyler" in E^3 is a calculus, not a new ambient axiom system

The production module

```text
CGJteamLab/Coxeter/CoxeterA3Wyler.lean
```

works with the same Hilbert three-space used in Log 058.

The word "Wyler" refers here to the language of generated flats, meets,
joins, carriers, and exchange-type reasoning.

The ambient geometry is still ordinary synthetic E^3.

No second model of space is introduced, and no coordinate realization is
used.

The purpose is structural:

```text
spatial mirror arrangement
        |
        v
flat incidence skeleton
        |
        v
fixed-flat / active-section decomposition
        |
        v
local Coxeter labels.
```

This viewpoint becomes important precisely because the same pattern can be
asked for in dimensions that are no longer directly visualizable.

E^3 is therefore the laboratory in which the flat calculus can be checked
against a configuration we already understand geometrically.

## 3. The three mirror meets

Let the three tetrahedral mirror planes be

$$
\pi_1,\qquad \pi_2,\qquad \pi_3.
$$

Their pairwise intersections are represented by three line carriers:

$$
q_{12}=\pi_1\cap\pi_2,
$$

$$
q_{23}=\pi_2\cap\pi_3,
$$

$$
q_{13}=\pi_1\cap\pi_3.
$$

The distant pair has an especially concrete description.

If `F1` and `F3` are the midpoint feet belonging to the first and third
mirrors, then production proves

$$
\pi_1\cap\pi_3=\operatorname{Span}\{F_1,F_3\}.
$$

Thus the common line is not merely an existential intersection.

It is identified with a generated flat determined by the tetrahedral data.

The adjacent mirror intersections are also constructed explicitly, and the
three resulting meet lines are proved pairwise distinct.

At this stage the three planes have become an incidence configuration rather
than three unrelated mirrors.

## 4. Joins reconstruct the mirrors

The next step reverses the calculation.

The three meet lines determine the original mirror planes by joins:

$$
\pi_1=q_{12}\vee q_{13},
$$

$$
\pi_2=q_{12}\vee q_{23},
$$

$$
\pi_3=q_{23}\vee q_{13}.
$$

This is packaged by

```text
wyler_A3_mirror_join_meet_calculus
```

and gives a compact incidence skeleton for the whole A3 arrangement.

Schematically,

```text
          pi2
        /     \
      q12     q23
      /         \
    pi1---q13---pi3
```

The point is not merely that the intersections exist.

The meet data and join reconstruction recover the mirror arrangement itself.

No normal vectors, coordinates, determinants, or dimension formulas are
needed.

## 5. The meet lines are exact common fixed loci

Incidence alone is not yet reflection geometry.

The decisive bridge is the fixed-point theorem for plane reflection:

```text
P is fixed by reflection in pi
        iff
P lies in pi.
```

Therefore a point is fixed by both `r_i` and `r_j` exactly when it lies in
the intersection of the two mirror planes.

Production proves this extensionally for all three pairs:

$$
q_{ij}=\operatorname{Fix}(r_i)\cap\operatorname{Fix}(r_j).
$$

This gives the meet lines a transformation-theoretic meaning.

They are the exact common fixed flats of the corresponding generator pairs.

The static pairwise geometry is therefore

```text
two mirror planes
        |
        v
their meet line
        |
        v
exact common fixed locus.
```

The nontrivial Coxeter action must occur in a complementary direction.

That is the role of the active section.

## 6. Adjacent mirrors reduce to the planar p = 3 theorem

For the adjacent pair `pi1,pi2`, choose the plane

$$
\sigma_{12}=\operatorname{plane}(A,B,C).
$$

Inside this plane the mirror traces are

$$
\pi_1\cap\sigma_{12}=CF_1,
$$

$$
\pi_2\cap\sigma_{12}=AF_2.
$$

These lines are packaged as actual reflection axes in the induced planar
geometry

```text
PlaneGeo Geo sigma12.
```

The spatial plane reflections are then related to the corresponding line
reflections in this slice.

The triangle `ABC` needed by the planar order-three theorem is not inserted
as an extra equilateral-triangle assumption.

Its equilateral geometry follows from the already established spatial
reflection isometries.

Thus the three-dimensional pair is reduced to the planar mechanism already
developed before A3.

The result is

$$
m_{12}=3.
$$

The second adjacent pair works in exactly the same way.

Choose

$$
\sigma_{23}=\operatorname{plane}(B,D,C).
$$

The exact traces are

$$
\pi_2\cap\sigma_{23}=DF_2,
$$

$$
\pi_3\cap\sigma_{23}=BF_3,
$$

and the induced triangle is again equilateral for geometric reasons.

The planar exact-period theorem gives

$$
m_{23}=3.
$$

So both adjacent labels are explained locally by the same two-dimensional
equilateral-median reflection mechanism.

## 7. The distant pair needs a genuine normal section

The pair `pi1,pi3` behaves differently.

Its common fixed line is

$$
q_{13}=\operatorname{Span}\{F_1,F_3\}.
$$

A plane containing this line would not isolate the active motion, because both
mirror traces would retain the same fixed direction.

Instead one constructs a plane transverse to the common fixed line.

Inside that plane the exact traces of `pi1` and `pi3` are proved
perpendicular.

The already established planar `p=2` theorem then gives exact period two:

$$
m_{13}=2.
$$

This is the first clear appearance of the general higher-dimensional pattern:

```text
pair of mirrors
        |
        +--> common fixed flat
        |
        +--> transverse active 2-flat
                  |
                  v
          planar Coxeter relation.
```

For the adjacent pairs the active section exhibits the equilateral
order-three mechanism.

For the distant pair it exhibits perpendicular axes and order two.

## 9. The next question: which part is really three-dimensional?

Once the A3 geometry has been rewritten in terms of flats, another question
becomes unavoidable.

Which parts of the argument depend on the fact that the ambient space is
exactly E^3, and which parts belong to a dimension-independent incidence
calculus?

The crucial three-dimensional fact is the Hilbert spatial incidence
principle usually referred to here as Hilbert I.7:

```text
two distinct planes with a common point
have a second common point.
```

In E^3 this is natural: two distinct intersecting planes meet in a line.

But this statement cannot be used unchanged as a dimension-free primitive.

Already in E^4 two two-dimensional planes may intersect in only one point.

Thus the passage from E^3 to a general flat calculus requires separating

```text
three-dimensional source incidence
```

from

```text
dimension-free consequences.
```

## 10. From Hilbert E^3 incidence to Smith I.5

The production bridge is built in

```text
CGJteamLab/Hilbert3DSmithBridge.lean
```

A preliminary theorem proves that every three ambient points in Hilbert
E^3 are coplanar.

For noncollinear points this follows directly from the plane-through-three-
points incidence principle.

For collinear points one adds a point outside the common line and obtains a
plane containing the line.

The central theorem is

```text
hilbert3D_smith_i5
```

which derives the Smith I.5 configuration from ordinary Hilbert
three-dimensional incidence.

The proof constructs two planes through the distinguished point.

If they are distinct, Hilbert I.7 gives a second common point.

If they coincide, one chooses a point outside the common plane, constructs a
second plane through the distinguished point and that external point, and
applies Hilbert I.7 to this new pair.

Thus Smith I.5 is not an additional incidence axiom in E^3.

It is a theorem.

## 13. Two different statements numbered I.7

At this point a source-level distinction becomes important.

There are two different principles carrying the label "I.7" in the project
discussion.

The first is Hilbert I.7, a specifically three-dimensional incidence
principle about intersecting planes.

It is used to prove Smith I.5 in E^3.

The second is Wyler I.7, used in the higher-dimensional incidence route.

It belongs to the primitive interface

```text
HilbertWylerAxioms
```

and from it the project derives Smith I.5 and exchange in

```text
HilbertWylerTheory.
```

The two routes therefore have the same middle theory but different sources:

```text
Hilbert E^3 incidence
        |
        v
Smith-Wyler theory

HilbertWylerAxioms
        |
        v
Smith-Wyler theory.
```

This separation is essential.

The dimension-free library must not obtain its generality by secretly
importing a theorem that is true only because the ambient space is E^3.

## 14. Why the generic layer remains independent

The implication

```text
Hilbert E^3 -> Smith-Wyler flat calculus
```

does not make the generic theory redundant.

It shows only that E^3 is one model in which the dimension-free consequences
can be derived from a more concrete spatial incidence theory.

The direction of abstraction is

```text
dimension-specific source
        |
        v
Smith-Wyler incidence consequences
        |
        v
generated-flat exchange.
```

The middle and right-hand layers are intended to survive changes of ambient
dimension.

This is why the generic flat modules remain independent of the E^3 bridge.

The bridge points upward into the generic theory.

The generic theory does not point back down into E^3.
