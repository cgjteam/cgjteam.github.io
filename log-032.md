# Log 032 - Architectural Separation of the Hilbert and Tarski Formalizations

**Date:** 2026-07-28

## Objective

The purpose of this work was to investigate whether the Hilbert and Tarski formalizations could share a common foundational module containing the basic geometric structures.

Rather than introducing a permanent architectural change, the goal was to experimentally evaluate whether such a common core would naturally emerge from the existing code base.

---

## Initial Hypothesis

A new module, `GeometryCore`, was extracted from `HilbertCore`.

Its purpose was to contain only the primitive geometric structure

- `Geometry.Geo`

together with the associated basic definitions.

The initial expectation was that this module might eventually become a common foundation for multiple geometric axiom systems.

---

## Architectural Experiment

After introducing `GeometryCore`, the Hilbert formalization was successfully adapted to import the new module.

The next step was to integrate the Tarski formalization with the same architecture.

During this process a namespace conflict appeared because both formalizations defined their own primitive object

```lean
Geometry.Geo
```

Although this conflict could be resolved mechanically, it raised a more fundamental architectural question.

---

## Analysis

A detailed inspection of both formalizations showed that the apparent similarity of their primitive structures was misleading.

The Hilbert and Tarski developments do not merely provide different collections of axioms.

They represent independent formalizations with different logical organization and different long-term development goals.

Consequently, forcing both frameworks to share the same primitive geometric structure would introduce an artificial dependency without providing any genuine architectural benefit.

The experiment therefore demonstrated that `GeometryCore` did not represent a true common abstraction.

Instead, it was simply the initial part of the Hilbert formalization extracted into a temporary module.

---

## Architectural Decision

The project architecture was revised accordingly.

The Tarski formalization was moved into its own namespace

```text
Geometry.Tarski
```

while the temporary `GeometryCore` module was removed.

Its contents were restored to `HilbertCore`, which again became the natural entry point of the Hilbert formalization.

The resulting architecture consists of three independent foundational frameworks:

- Hilbert
- Tarski
- Suppes

Each framework now owns its own primitive geometric model and can evolve independently.

---

## Technical Outcome

The refactoring included:

- migration of the complete Tarski framework into the `Geometry.Tarski` namespace;
- restoration of `HilbertCore` as the owner of `Geometry.Geo`;
- removal of the temporary `GeometryCore` module;
- elimination of namespace conflicts between the Hilbert and Tarski developments.

After the refactoring, the entire project compiled successfully using

```text
lake build
```

without errors.

---

## Conclusions

This experiment established that a shared geometry core is not an appropriate abstraction for the current project.

Instead of converging toward a single foundational layer, the architecture naturally evolved into three independent formalization paths.

This separation better reflects the mathematical independence of the Hilbert, Tarski and Suppes frameworks, while providing a cleaner foundation for future development and comparative studies of different geometric axiom systems.
