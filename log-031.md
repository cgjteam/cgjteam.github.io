# Log 031 - Finlay's Proof on the Tarski Route

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Project:** CGJteam Lab  
**Status:** Tarski Finlay Reconstruction

---

## 1. Overview

`FinlayProofTarski.lean` contains the current Tarski-based reconstruction of
Finlay's proof.

The active import is

```lean
import CGJteamLab.MidSegmentTarski
```

which confirms the current proof route:

```text
TarskiAxioms
     |
     v
TarskiInterface
     |
     v
MidSegmentTarski
     |
     v
FinlayProofTarski.
```

The older `MidsegmentParallelTarski.lean` is therefore not part of this active
dependency chain.

The current Tarski proof is formulated under

```lean
[TarskiNeutral Geo]
```

and uses Tarski-specific midpoint, collinearity, strict parallelism, and
parallelogram notions.

---

## 2. The Finlay Configuration

The proof uses the classical midpoint configuration associated with a triangle
\(ABC\).

The relevant midpoint points satisfy relations of the form

$$
E=\operatorname{Mid}(A,B),
$$

$$
F=\operatorname{Mid}(A,C),
$$

and the auxiliary point \(P\) is related to the midpoint structure involving
\(G\).

The high-level objective is again to show that the point \(G\), already
obtained from two medians, also lies on the third median.

The proof retains the familiar Finlay pattern:

```text
midsegment relations
        |
        v
auxiliary parallelogram
        |
        v
diagonal midpoint information
        |
        v
third median.
```

The internal implementation, however, differs significantly from the Hilbert
and Suppes routes.

---

## 3. Step 1 - Midsegment Parallel Relations

The first step uses

```lean
MidsegmentTheoremTarski.
```

This is the theorem reconstructed in Log 030.

It produces the Tarski strict-parallel relations associated with the
midsegment configuration.

The proof then transfers these relations along the relevant collinear points
to obtain the two parallel relations required for the auxiliary
quadrilateral.

Schematically:

```text
MidsegmentTheoremTarski
        |
        v
strict midpoint parallelism
        |
        v
collinear extension
        |
        +--> BP || CG
        |
        `--> BG || CP.
```

Unlike the earlier abandoned bridge-based Tarski route, this step remains in
the Tarski-specific language.

---

## 4. Step 2 - Recognizing the Parallelogram

The two pairs of strict parallel sides are passed to

```lean
tarski_parallelogram_of_two_parallel_pairs.
```

This yields

```text
TarskiParallelogram B P C G.
```

Thus:

```text
BP || CG
BG || CP
    |
    v
tarski_parallelogram_of_two_parallel_pairs
    |
    v
TarskiParallelogram B P C G.
```

This is the Tarski analogue of the classical recognition step in Finlay's
proof.

In the current `TarskiInterface`, however,
`tarski_parallelogram_of_two_parallel_pairs` is still represented as a
deferred axiom.

Therefore the high-level Step 2 is compact, but its complete reduction to the
primitive Tarski axioms remains open.

---

## 5. Why the Tarski Parallelogram Is Special

The definition of

```lean
TarskiParallelogram
```

is based on the existence of a common midpoint of the diagonals.

Thus a proof of

```text
TarskiParallelogram B P C G
```

already contains a point \(D\) satisfying midpoint relations for the
diagonals.

Conceptually:

```text
TarskiParallelogram B P C G
        |
        v
there exists D such that
        |
        +--> D = midpoint BC
        |
        `--> D = midpoint PG.
```

This has an important consequence for the structure of Finlay's proof.

In the Hilbert route, one first recognizes the parallelogram and later invokes
separate diagonal-intersection and diagonal-bisection theorems.

In the Tarski route, the midpoint description of the diagonals is built into
the parallelogram concept itself.

---

## 6. Step 3 - Extracting the Diagonal Midpoint

After obtaining

```text
TarskiParallelogram B P C G,
```

the proof unpacks its defining data.

The witness \(D\) immediately provides the two midpoint facts:

```text
D is midpoint of BC
D is midpoint of PG.
```

Equivalently,

$$
D=\operatorname{Mid}(B,C)
$$

and

$$
D=\operatorname{Mid}(P,G)
$$

in the geometric sense encoded by `TarskiIsMidpoint`.

Thus the classical operations

```text
find the diagonal intersection
prove the diagonals bisect each other
```

collapse into one structural extraction.

This is one of the most characteristic differences between the current
Tarski and Hilbert implementations.

---

## 7. From Midpoint to Collinearity

A Tarski midpoint contains betweenness information.

Therefore:

```text
D midpoint PG
```

immediately gives collinearity of

```text
P, D, G.
```

Similarly, the midpoint relation defining the auxiliary point \(G\) on the
appropriate segment provides the required collinearity involving

```text
A, G, P.
```

The final stage therefore has the form

```text
A,G,P collinear
P,D,G collinear
        |
        v
collinearity transitivity
        |
        v
A,G,D collinear.
```

The proof uses

```lean
tarski_collinear_trans
```

for this step.

---

## 8. Step 4 - The Third Median

From the parallelogram data we already know that \(D\) is the midpoint of
\(BC\).

The collinearity result

```text
A,G,D
```

therefore says precisely that \(G\) lies on the line from \(A\) to the
midpoint of \(BC\).

Hence \(G\) lies on the third median.

The final mathematical step is consequently very short:

```text
D = midpoint BC
        +
A,G,D collinear
        |
        v
G lies on the median from A.
```

No additional diagonal theorem is needed.

---

## 9. Compression of the Classical Five Steps

The Tarski representation compresses the classical Finlay architecture.

A paper-level decomposition might distinguish:

```text
1. obtain two parallel relations,
2. recognize the parallelogram,
3. locate the diagonal intersection,
4. prove diagonal bisection,
5. identify the third median.
```

In the current Tarski formalization, Steps 3 and 4 are largely merged because
of the definition of `TarskiParallelogram`.

Thus the implementation is closer to:

```text
Step 1
obtain the parallel relations

Step 2
recognize TarskiParallelogram

Step 3
extract the common diagonal midpoint

Step 4
use collinearity to obtain the third median.
```

This is not merely code compression.

It reflects a different choice of mathematical representation.

---

## 10. Comparison with the Hilbert Route

The Hilbert route defines a parallelogram through opposite parallel sides.

Consequently, diagonal behavior must later be proved as additional geometry:

```text
opposite sides parallel
        |
        v
IsParallelogram
        |
        v
diagonal intersection
        |
        v
diagonal bisection.
```

The Tarski route instead uses:

```text
common diagonal midpoint
        |
        v
TarskiParallelogram.
```

Therefore the midpoint property required by Finlay is already part of the
representation.

Schematically:

```text
Hilbert                         Tarski

parallel sides                 common diagonal midpoint
     |                                  |
     v                                  v
parallelogram                    parallelogram
     |                                  |
     v                                  |
diagonal theorem                         |
     |                                  |
     +---------------+------------------+
                     |
                     v
               Finlay midpoint.
```

This is a concrete example of how a definition can change the visible proof
architecture.

## 11. Comparison with the Suppes Route

The Suppes route also defines its primitive parallelogram through equality of
diagonal midpoints.

This gives Suppes and Tarski a structural similarity at the parallelogram
level.

However, their foundations remain very different:

```text
Suppes

Mid / Dbl / Col
      |
      v
PrimParallelogram
```

versus

```text
Tarski

Between / Congruent
        |
        v
TarskiIsMidpoint
        |
        v
TarskiParallelogram.
```

Thus the same affine idea appears at different depths in the two theories.


No `TarskiPlane` or `TarskiEuclideanPlane` instance appears in the theorem

## 17. The Obsolete Bridge-Based Midsegment Module

The current `FinlayProofTarski.lean` imports

```lean
MidSegmentTarski
```

directly.

It does not use the older module

```lean
MidsegmentParallelTarski.lean.
```

That older file translated Tarski midpoint data into the shared synthetic
interface and then invoked Hilbert-oriented machinery such as SAS, equal-angle
parallelism, and Hilbert parallelogram results.

It therefore represented a mixed route:

```text
Tarski input
     |
GeometryBase bridge
     |
Hilbert machinery
     |
Midsegment.
```

The current active route is instead:

```text
Tarski input
     |
Tarski theory
     |
Tarski Midsegment
     |
Tarski Finlay.
```

Thus `MidsegmentParallelTarski.lean` is not part of the present dependency
chain.

It may be retained temporarily as a historical experiment, but it should not
be presented as part of the current Tarski route.

## 18. Three Finlay Routes

The project now exposes three different formal architectures for the same
classical argument.

### Hilbert

```text
construction
 -> Midsegment through SAS and angles
 -> parallel-side parallelogram
 -> diagonal theorems
 -> third median.
```

### Suppes

```text
midpoint/doubling theory
 -> Midsegment through Theorem 11
 -> parallel-side recognition
 -> diagonal midpoint assumption
 -> third median.
```

### Tarski

```text
betweenness/congruence
 -> symmetric-point Midsegment proof
 -> strict parallelism
 -> midpoint-defined parallelogram
 -> extract diagonal midpoint
 -> collinearity
 -> third median.
```

The high-level theorem is the same, but the internal mathematical
organization changes with the foundational language.

---

## 19. Architectural Consequence

The current Tarski Finlay file is already appropriately high-level.

The remaining unresolved principles are general Tarski geometry and should
not be expanded inside `FinlayProofTarski.lean`.

In particular:

```text
parallelogram recognition from parallel sides,
outer connectivity,
central symmetry,
midpoint uniqueness,
parallelogram side properties
```

belong below the final proof.

The appropriate research direction is therefore reduction of the Tarski
interface rather than enlargement of the Finlay module.

---

## 20. Conclusion

`FinlayProofTarski.lean` completes the reconstruction of the active Tarski
route at the level of the classical proof.

The route is:

```text
TarskiAxioms
     |
     v
TarskiInterface
     |
     v
MidSegmentTarski
     |
     v
FinlayProofTarski.
```

Its most distinctive feature is the definition of `TarskiParallelogram`
through a common midpoint of the diagonals.

Because of this choice, the classical diagonal-intersection and
diagonal-bisection stages collapse into the extraction of data already
contained in the parallelogram structure.

The remaining foundational debt is no longer hidden inside the final proof.
It is concentrated in general interface results, especially:

```text
the deferred dependencies inherited from MidSegmentTarski,
tarski_parallelogram_of_two_parallel_pairs,
and the outer-connectivity dependency below collinearity transitivity.
```

Thus the Tarski Finlay proof is structurally complete at the high level, while
the remaining research problem is to reduce these general affine and order
principles to the appropriate Tarski axiom classes.
