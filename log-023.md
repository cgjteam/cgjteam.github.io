# Log 023 - The Hilbert Interface: From Axioms to Synthetic Geometry

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Project:** CGJteam Lab  
**Status:** Hilbert Interface Reconstruction

---

## 1. Overview

`HilbertInterface.lean` forms the boundary between the foundational
Hilbert development and the synthetic language used by higher-level
geometric proofs.

Its role is not to introduce another independent axiomatic system.

Instead, the interface preserves a convenient synthetic API while
reducing its declarations to the geometric structure already developed
in `HilbertCore.lean` and `HilbertAxioms.lean`.

The resulting architecture is:

```text
HilbertCore
    |
    v
HilbertAxioms
    |
    v
HilbertInterface
    |
    v
Synthetic Geometry
```

A central feature of the current file is the replacement of earlier
provisional interface axioms by proved theorems.

Historical declarations of the form

```lean
axiom ...
```

remain in comments in several places, but the active declarations are
theorems derived from the Hilbert foundation.

Thus the interface records a transition:

```text
provisional synthetic assumptions
              |
              v
formal Hilbert derivations
              |
              v
stable synthetic API
```

## 3. Collinearity

The public collinearity relation is given by:

```lean
abbrev Collinear := PrimCollinear
```

Thus `Collinear` is not a new primitive relation and does not require an
additional interpretation theorem.

Its dependency is direct:

```text
HilbertIncidence.OnLine
        |
        v
PrimCollinear
        ||
        v
Collinear
```

Within the current Hilbert route, the public synthetic notion of
collinearity is therefore the incidence-based `PrimCollinear` notion
developed in the foundational layer.

This is an important simplification of this architecture: the higher
synthetic language does not introduce another collinearity predicate at
this boundary.

---

## 4. Midpoints

The interface distinguishes two notions of midpoint.

The public synthetic notion is:

```lean
IsMidpoint M A B :=
  Collinear A M B /\ Congruent A M M B
```

The stronger Hilbert notion is:

```lean
HilbertIsMidpoint M A B :=
  Between A M B /\ Congruent A M M B
```

The relation between them is:

```text
HilbertIsMidpoint
        |
        | Between -> Collinear
        v
IsMidpoint
```

and is expressed by:

```lean
midpoint_of_hilbert
```

This separates the order-theoretic content of the Hilbert foundation
from the weaker notion needed by many synthetic arguments.

Higher-level proofs can therefore use `IsMidpoint` without carrying
explicit betweenness hypotheses through every theorem.

---

## 5. Parallelograms

The interface defines:

```lean
OppositeSidesParallel A B C D :=
  Parallel A B C D /\
  Parallel B C D A
```

and:

```lean
IsParallelogram A B C D :=
  OppositeSidesParallel A B C D
```

Thus a parallelogram is defined by the two pairs of opposite sides being
parallel.

Schematically:

```text
AB || CD
BC || DA
    |
    v
IsParallelogram A B C D
```

Consequently:

```lean
ParallelogramOfParallel
```

is essentially a packaging theorem for the definition.

Recognition of a parallelogram from the two required parallelisms is
therefore not a deep Euclidean theorem in this architecture.

The substantial theory begins only when further properties of a
parallelogram are derived.

## 6. Representation-Level Results

A first group of interface theorems requires no substantial Hilbert
geometry.

Examples include:

```lean
CongruentReverseFirst
CongruentReverseBoth
CongruentSwapSecond

AngleCongruentReverse

ParallelSymmetry
ParallelSwapFirstLine
ParallelSwapSecondLine
```

Their origin lies in the representations introduced in `HilbertCore`.

Segments use unordered endpoints, angles use unordered rays, and
point-defined lines are extensional objects.

The logical structure is therefore:

```text
representation choices
        |
        +--> segment reversal
        |
        +--> angle reversal
        |
        `--> line endpoint exchange
```

These results are consequences of the chosen formal representations
rather than additional Hilbert-style geometric assumptions.

The same observation applies to results that merely unpack or repack
definitions such as the parallel-side definition of a parallelogram.

## 7. Incidence-Level Results

Some interface theorems require genuine Hilbert incidence geometry.

For example:

```lean
CollinearTrans
```

is available at the level:

```lean
[HilbertPlaneIncidence Geo]
```

Its proof ultimately uses the uniqueness of the line through two
distinct points.

The underlying dependency is:

```text
Hilbert incidence
        |
        v
uniqueness of a line through two points
        |
        v
transitivity of collinearity
```

Similarly, results such as:

```lean
IntersectionOnSameLine
```

are consequences of the incidence structure rather than independent
interface assumptions.

Thus the interface exposes ordinary synthetic incidence lemmas while
their proofs terminate in Group I of the Hilbert hierarchy.

## 8. Order-Level Results

At the level

```lean
[HilbertOrder Geo]
```

the interface derives results involving collinearity, betweenness, and
transport along a line.

Important examples include:

```text
midpoint_of_hilbert
parallel_endpoints_sameSide
ParallelCollinearLeft
collinear_parallel_trans
onePairParallelCongruent_of_crossing
```

The parallel transport theorems are particularly important.

A typical geometric pattern is:

```text
AB || DE
C, A, B collinear
------------------
CB || DE
```

In the current derivation, the proof does not invoke the Euclidean
parallel axiom.

Instead, the incidence and order structure is used to show that the
different pairs of points determine the same underlying line.

Thus transport of parallelism along collinear points belongs to the
neutral part of the Hilbert development.

The interface exposes a theorem named:


Earlier versions of the interface treated this functionality

The current active theorem reduces the public SAS result to the

In particular, it packages results including:

```lean
hilbert_sas_remaining_angles
```

```text

        |
        v
```

The synthetic proof layer can consequently use `SAS` as a compact
operation without treating it as an independent assumption.

The interface theorem:


It packages the neutral Hilbert theorem that equal alternate angles
imply parallel lines.

Thus:

```text
equal alternate angles
        |
        v

```

belongs to the neutral interface.

No instance of:

```lean

```

is required for this direction.

This is a central logical feature of the Hilbert route because it
permits parallelism to be established inside the geometry of Groups
I-III.

## 13. The Euclidean Boundary

The interface marks a sharp logical boundary at:

```lean
[HilbertEuclideanPlane Geo]
```

A basic example is:

```lean
equal_angles_from_parallel
```

which expresses the converse direction:

```text
parallel lines
        |
        v
equal alternate angles
```

Unlike `parallel_from_equal_angles`, this result requires Hilbert Group
IV.

The two directions therefore occupy different logical levels:

```text
Groups I-III:

equal alternate angles
        |
        v
parallel

Group IV:

parallel
   |
   v
equal alternate angles
```

This distinction is preserved explicitly by the interface.

Among the relevant declarations are:

```lean

ParallelogramDiagonalIntersectionExists

```

These results require:

```lean
[HilbertEuclideanPlane Geo]
```

This creates an important distinction between the definition of a
parallelogram and its classical Euclidean theory.

The definition is simply:

```text
two pairs of opposite sides parallel
                |
                v
          parallelogram
```

whereas the derived theory contains statements such as:

```text
parallelogram
     |
     +--> opposite sides congruent
     |
     +--> diagonals intersect
     |
     `--> diagonals bisect each other
```

The latter results require genuinely stronger geometry.

Thus `IsParallelogram` itself does not introduce Euclidean strength,
while the diagonal and congruence theorems do.

## 17. Architectural Meaning of the Interface

The interface establishes a clean separation between foundational
formalization and synthetic proof language.

At the foundational level, the library works with:

```text
incidence
betweenness
segment congruence
angle congruence
Hilbert axiom groups
```

At the synthetic level, proofs can use:

```text
collinear
midpoint
median
parallel
SAS
parallelogram
intersection
```

The interface connects these levels without introducing an independent
geometric theory.

Its architecture is therefore:

```text
Hilbert foundation
        |
        | formal derivation
        v
HilbertInterface
        |
        | mathematical packaging
        v
synthetic geometric language
```

This makes it possible for higher-level proofs to retain a readable
classical form while their logical dependencies remain traceable to the
Hilbert foundation.

---

## 18. Conclusion

In its current active declarations, `HilbertInterface.lean` is not an
additional independent axiomatic layer.

It is the reduction boundary between Hilbert's foundational geometry
and the reusable synthetic API of the library.

The current implementation replaces earlier provisional assumptions by
theorems whose dependencies terminate at specific levels of the Hilbert
hierarchy.

The resulting structure distinguishes four important kinds of
synthetic operations:

```text
representation-level operations
incidence-level operations
neutral Hilbert operations
Euclidean Hilbert operations
```

In particular, the interface shows that several tools central to
classical constructions already belong to neutral geometry:

```text
SAS
midpoint existence
equal alternate angles -> parallel
parallel transport along collinear points
```

By contrast, the converse angle theorem and the stronger classical
theory of parallelograms require the Euclidean parallel axiom.

The central architectural result is therefore:

```text
HilbertCore
        |
        v
HilbertAxioms
        |
        | proved foundational geometry
        v
HilbertInterface
        |
        | no new independent axiomatic layer
        v
reusable synthetic geometry
```

The interface preserves the language of ordinary synthetic proofs while
making their actual Hilbert dependencies explicit.
