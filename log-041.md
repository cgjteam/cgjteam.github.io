# Log 041 — Isolating the Euclidean Parallelism Assumption

## Objective

After eliminating derivable assumptions from the Tarski route, attention turned to a different remaining dependency:

`parallelogram_of_two_parallel_pairs`.

This statement recognizes a parallelogram from two pairs of opposite parallel sides.

Unlike the previously eliminated midpoint assumption, its status was not obviously neutral.

The objective of this session was therefore to determine where this result belongs in the logical structure of Tarski geometry.

## The remaining statement

The local assumption has the form:

```lean
axiom parallelogram_of_two_parallel_pairs
    [TarskiNeutral Geo]
    (A B C D : Geo.Point)
    (hABCD : TarskiParallelStrict Geo A B C D)
    (hBCAD : TarskiParallelStrict Geo B C A D) :
    TarskiParallelogram Geo A B C D
```

At the level of ordinary Euclidean geometry, the statement is familiar: a quadrilateral whose opposite sides are parallel is a parallelogram.

In the formal development, however, the conclusion is expressed through the midpoint structure of the diagonals.

The question is therefore not merely whether the statement is classically true, but which axiomatic resources are required to derive it.

## Neutral geometry is not enough by default

The investigation showed that this assumption cannot simply be treated as another routine lemma of the neutral Tarski interface.

Parallelism results occupy a different logical position from elementary consequences of betweenness and congruence.

In particular, results connecting parallel lines, alternate constructions and parallelogram structure belong to the broader theory surrounding the Euclidean parallel postulate.

This means that declaring the theorem under `[TarskiNeutral Geo]` risks hiding a genuinely Euclidean dependency.

## From implementation search to mathematical analysis

The initial strategy had been to inspect existing formal developments for a corresponding theorem and reconstruct its dependencies.

For this result, that approach exposed a larger issue.

The theorem was not an isolated missing lemma.

It belonged to a network of results about Euclidean parallelism.

Consequently, continuing to reverse engineer individual library lemmas would obscure the mathematical question rather than clarify it.

The investigation therefore shifted from implementation reconstruction to the classical theory itself.

## Beeson and constructive Tarski geometry

Michael Beeson's work on constructive versions of Tarski geometry provides a useful framework for this analysis.

The relevant question becomes:

> Which form of the parallel postulate is sufficient to derive the parallelogram recognition theorem used by Finlay's proof?

This requires distinguishing several levels of the theory:

- neutral Tarski geometry,
- definitions of strict parallelism,
- Euclidean parallel principles,
- consequences of those principles for parallelograms and midpoint constructions.

The local Lean axiom can then be classified according to this mathematical hierarchy rather than merely according to where it happens to occur in the code.

## A change in reduction strategy

The previous reductions followed a direct pattern:

```
local axiom
    ↓
known proof
    ↓
missing lemmas
    ↓
formal reconstruction
```

For the parallelism assumption, the appropriate path is different:

```
local axiom
    ↓
identify mathematical theorem
    ↓
locate it in the theory of Euclidean parallelism
    ↓
determine required parallel postulate
    ↓
formal reconstruction
```

This distinction is important.

Not every remaining axiom should be eliminated by searching for a sufficiently long chain of lemmas.

Some assumptions first need to be classified logically.

## Consequence for the Tarski hierarchy

The investigation suggests that the Tarski development should make the neutral/Euclidean boundary explicit.

A theorem depending on a Euclidean parallel principle should not appear to follow from the neutral theory merely because it is convenient for the current proof.

The desired architecture is therefore closer to:

```
Tarski neutral geometry
        ↓
derived neutral theory
        ↓
Euclidean parallel principle
        ↓
Euclidean parallelism theory
        ↓
parallelogram recognition
        ↓
Finlay proof
```

This would make the logical strength of the Finlay route visible in the Lean typeclass structure.

## Result

`parallelogram_of_two_parallel_pairs` was identified as qualitatively different from the previously eliminated midpoint assumption.

Its reconstruction requires analysis of Euclidean parallelism rather than only local neutral Tarski geometry.

The assumption is therefore retained provisionally while its precise relationship to the parallel postulate is investigated.

This marks a transition from dependency reduction inside the existing Tarski interface to a mathematical reconstruction of the Euclidean layer required by Finlay's proof.
