# Log 058 --- Coxeter Geometry in E^3: The Tetrahedral A3 System and S4

## 1. From the planar Coxeter mechanism to E^3

Log 057 described the planar stage of the Coxeter development: line
reflections, reflection words, the braid relation, and finite pairwise
periods.

The next step is genuinely spatial.

In E^3 the mirrors are no longer lines. They are planes, and the basic
Coxeter system of type A3 requires three such reflecting planes.

The target is

```text
three synthetic plane reflections
        |
        v
Coxeter system A3
        |
        v
action on four tetrahedral vertices
        |
        v
generated reflection group
        |
        v
S4
```

The purpose of this log is deliberately limited to that one story.

It records the construction of the three-dimensional tetrahedral Coxeter
frame and the identification of its generated reflection group with the
symmetric group on four vertices.

The later Wyler-flat reinterpretation of the same configuration is not part
of this log.

## 2. Why A3 is the first genuinely spatial Coxeter system

The planar theory already explains pairwise relations between two
reflections.

A3 is different because one must control three generators simultaneously:

$$
r_1,\quad r_2,\quad r_3.
$$

The desired Coxeter relations are

$$
r_i^2=1,
$$

$$
(r_1r_2)^3=1,\qquad (r_2r_3)^3=1,
$$

and

$$
(r_1r_3)^2=1.
$$

Equivalently, the Coxeter diagram is the chain

```text
r1 ---3--- r2 ---3--- r3
```

with the distant pair `r1,r3` commuting.

In coordinates one could realize this by matrices or normals to planes.

That route is intentionally avoided.

The production development remains synthetic. It uses ambient Hilbert
incidence, order and congruence, plane reflection, Euclid Book XI
perpendicularity, and rigidity of four noncoplanar points.

No vectors, scalar products, matrices, coordinates, or trigonometric angle
measure are introduced.

## 3. The tetrahedral mirror frame

The central geometric object is

```text
CoxeterA3TetrahedralFrame
```

with four noncoplanar points

```text
A, B, C, D
```

and three reflecting planes

```text
pi1, pi2, pi3.
```

Their meaning is simple.

`pi1` is the perpendicular-bisector plane of `AB` and contains `C,D`.

`pi2` is the perpendicular-bisector plane of `BC` and contains `A,D`.

`pi3` is the perpendicular-bisector plane of `CD` and contains `A,B`.

Thus the three plane reflections act on the four vertices as

```text
r1 : A <-> B,   C,D fixed
r2 : B <-> C,   A,D fixed
r3 : C <-> D,   A,B fixed
```

This is the geometric form of the three adjacent transpositions

$$
(AB),\qquad (BC),\qquad (CD).
$$

An important design decision is that the permutation action is not assumed
inside the frame.

The frame contains geometric mirror data only.

The transposition behavior is derived from the geometry of plane reflection.

## 4. Constructing the frame from a regular tetrahedron

The existence problem is separated from the algebraic use of the frame.

The production module introduces a synthetic regular tetrahedron in Hilbert
3-space:

```text
HilbertRegularTetrahedron3D
```

It consists of four noncoplanar vertices whose six edges are congruent.

From such a tetrahedron the three mirror planes are constructed
synthetically.

For the edge `AB`, let `F` be its midpoint.

Because `C` and `D` are each equidistant from `A` and `B`, the lines `FC`
and `FD` are perpendicular to `AB`.

The points `F,C,D` are noncollinear, so the two lines determine a plane.

Euclid XI.4 then gives

```text
AB perpendicular to plane(F,C,D) at F.
```

Therefore the plane through `F,C,D` is exactly the perpendicular-bisector
mirror of `AB`.

The same construction is repeated for the edges `BC` and `CD`.

Schematically,

```text
regular tetrahedron
        |
        +--> midpoint of AB
        |       |
        |       +--> opposite vertices equidistant from A,B
        |       |
        |       +--> two perpendicular lines through midpoint
        |       |
        |       +--> XI.4
        |       |
        |       v
        |     mirror pi1
        |
        +--> mirror pi2
        |
        +--> mirror pi3
        |
        v
CoxeterA3TetrahedralFrame
```

This step is the geometric heart of the existence proof.

## 5. Plane reflection becomes the three Coxeter generators

Reflection in an ambient plane had already been reconstructed as a genuine
point transformation.

For the tetrahedral frame the three generators are therefore defined by

```text
r1 = reflection in pi1
r2 = reflection in pi2
r3 = reflection in pi3.
```

Each is an equivalence of the ambient point set.

The first Coxeter relation follows immediately from the general involution
theorem for plane reflection:

$$
r_1^2=r_2^2=r_3^2=1.
$$

The more important fact is the action on the four anchors.

Because `pi1` is the perpendicular-bisector plane of `AB`, reflection in
`pi1` exchanges `A` and `B`.

Because `C,D` lie in `pi1`, both are fixed.

Hence

$$
r_1=(AB)
$$

on the four tetrahedral vertices.

Similarly,

$$
r_2=(BC),\qquad r_3=(CD).
$$

This is where the geometry begins to expose the abstract A3 permutation
pattern.

## 6. The Coxeter relations are read from the tetrahedral action

Once the action on the four vertices has been established, the expected
relations become transparent.

For the adjacent pair `r1,r2` the induced permutations are

$$
(AB),\qquad (BC).
$$

Their product has order three.

Likewise `r2,r3` induce

$$
(BC),\qquad (CD),
$$

and again their product has order three.

The distant pair `r1,r3` induces disjoint transpositions

$$
(AB),\qquad (CD),
$$

so the two generators commute and

$$
(r_1r_3)^2=1.
$$

The formal development does not merely import these permutation identities
as an abstract replacement for geometry.

The geometric reflections have already been constructed as actual
transformations of the ambient Hilbert space, and their action on the
tetrahedral anchors is proved.

The permutation pattern is therefore a consequence of the synthetic mirror
geometry.

## 7. Why four noncoplanar anchors are enough

To identify the full generated geometric group with S4, knowing the action on
the four vertices is not enough by itself.

One must prove that a generated ambient isometry that fixes all four anchors
is the identity.

This is the role of four-point rigidity.

The four tetrahedral vertices are noncoplanar.

A synthetic isometry of Hilbert 3-space in the generated reflection group is
therefore determined by its values on these four anchors.

Consequently,

```text
same action on A,B,C,D
        |
        v
same ambient transformation.
```

This is the decisive faithfulness step.

It prevents the vertex action from forgetting some nontrivial ambient
reflection word.

Thus the tetrahedral vertices are not merely a convenient finite test set.
They are a complete rigid frame for the generated action.

## 8. The vertex action homomorphism

The four geometric vertices are represented by an abstract four-element type

```text
CoxeterA3Vertex
```

with elements corresponding to

```text
A, B, C, D.
```

The three abstract permutations are

```text
sigma1 = (A B)
sigma2 = (B C)
sigma3 = (C D).
```

The generated geometric reflection group acts on the four vertices, giving a
homomorphism

$$
G_T \longrightarrow \operatorname{Perm}\{A,B,C,D\}.
$$

The proof has two independent halves.

First, the action is injective.

This is exactly the four-anchor rigidity argument: if a generated geometric
transformation acts trivially on the four vertices, it is the identity on the
ambient point space.

Second, the action is surjective.

The images of the three geometric generators are the adjacent
transpositions

$$
(AB),\qquad (BC),\qquad (CD),
$$

and these generate the full symmetric group on four elements.

Therefore the vertex action is bijective.

## 9. The final identification with S4

The production endpoint is the multiplicative equivalence

```text
generatedGroupMulEquivS4
```

which identifies the subgroup generated by the three geometric plane
reflections with the full permutation group of the four abstract tetrahedral
vertices.

Thus

$$
G_T \cong S_4.
$$

The identification remembers the geometric meaning of the generators:

```text
r1 <-> (A B)
r2 <-> (B C)
r3 <-> (C D).
```

This is stronger than proving only that an abstract Coxeter presentation of
type A3 has a model in S4.

The group on the left is the actual subgroup of permutations of the ambient
Hilbert point space generated by synthetically constructed plane
reflections.

The route is therefore

```text
Hilbert 3-space
        |
        v
regular tetrahedron
        |
        v
three perpendicular-bisector mirror planes
        |
        v
three ambient plane reflections
        |
        v
tetrahedral vertex action
        |
        +--> faithfulness from four-point rigidity
        |
        +--> surjectivity from adjacent transpositions
        |
        v
G_T ~= S4
```

## 10. Production structure

The main production chain for this stage is

```text
Coxeter/PlaneReflection.lean
Coxeter/PlaneReflectionIsometry.lean
        |
        v
Coxeter/CoxeterRelations3D.lean
        |
        v
Coxeter/CoxeterRelations3DExistence.lean
        |
        v
Coxeter/CoxeterRelations3DS4.lean
```

The roles of the last three files are distinct.

`CoxeterRelations3D.lean` contains the spatial reflection and tetrahedral
frame layer.

`CoxeterRelations3DExistence.lean` proves that the required mirror frame is
obtained from a synthetic regular tetrahedron and establishes existence of
such a frame.

`CoxeterRelations3DS4.lean` packages the generated group, constructs the
vertex action, proves injectivity and surjectivity, and gives the final
multiplicative equivalence with S4.

This separation was useful because it kept three logically different tasks
apart:

```text
geometry of the frame
existence of the frame
group identification.
```

## 11. What this E^3 stage established

The A3 development provided the first complete higher-dimensional Coxeter
system in CGJteamLab.

The essential achievements are:

```text
plane reflection as a synthetic ambient transformation

a regular tetrahedron constructed in Hilbert 3-space

perpendicular-bisector mirror planes derived synthetically

three geometric A3 generators

the tetrahedral adjacent-transposition action

four-anchor rigidity

a faithful vertex action

surjectivity onto the full symmetric group

G_T ~= S4
```

Conceptually, the important point is that the passage

```text
geometry -> transformations -> group
```

is now complete in a genuinely spatial setting.

The group is not introduced first and then geometrically interpreted.

The geometry produces the transformations, and the transformations produce
the group.

## 12. Boundary of this log

This log stops at the completed tetrahedral A3 system and the identification

$$
G_T\cong S_4.
$$

It does not yet discuss the later reinterpretation of the same configuration
through Wyler flats, meet and join constructions, or the dimension-free
incidence bridge.

Those ideas form a second stage with a different purpose.

The natural continuation is therefore:

```text
Log 058:
E^3, tetrahedral A3, geometric reflection group ~= S4

E^3 revisited through Wyler flats and the dimension-free bridge
```

Keeping these stages separate makes the mathematical progression clearer.

The first asks:

```text
Can the Coxeter system A3 be constructed synthetically in E^3?
```

The answer is yes.

The second asks a different question:

```text
Which part of that construction is really three-dimensional,
and which part belongs to a dimension-free flat calculus?
```

That question is left for the next log.
