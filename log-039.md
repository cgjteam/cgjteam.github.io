# Log 039 — Reconstructing the Tarski Dependencies

## Objective

After simplifying the active Tarski architecture, the next task was to investigate the remaining deferred assumptions in the dependency chain of `FinlayProofTarski`.

The objective was no longer architectural cleanup alone.

Each surviving local axiom was treated as a mathematical claim whose status had to be determined:

- whether it was already derivable from the existing Tarski theory,
- whether it could be reconstructed from known Tarski results,
- whether it required additional geometric theory,
- or whether it represented a genuinely stronger assumption.

## From interface axioms to proof obligations

Earlier stages of the Tarski formalization used local axioms to bridge gaps while reconstructing the high-level proof.

This was useful for establishing the overall route, but it did not reveal the exact logical strength of the resulting development.

After the dependency audit, these axioms could be examined individually.

The working principle became:

> Do not reconstruct the whole surrounding theory. Reconstruct only the results that occur on the actual dependency path of the proof.

This made it possible to investigate the formal assumptions without importing a large external geometry development.

## Using existing formalizations as proof maps

The Tarski literature and existing formalizations were used to identify the mathematical provenance of the missing results.

In particular, GeoCoq and formal developments related to Tarski's system provided useful proof maps.

The purpose was not to reproduce their library architecture.

Instead, when a required result corresponded to a known theorem, its proof could be inspected to determine:

1. which lemmas it actually used,
2. which of those lemmas already existed locally,
3. which missing lemmas were elementary enough to reconstruct,
4. and which dependencies required a separate mathematical investigation.

This converted external formalizations from implementation targets into sources for dependency analysis.

## Local reconstruction strategy

For each deferred theorem, the reconstruction followed a small dependency loop:

```
required theorem
      ↓
inspect known proof
      ↓
list immediate dependencies
      ↓
match against local library
      ↓
reconstruct only missing dependencies
      ↓
replace local axiom by theorem
```

The process was deliberately incremental.

After each replacement or auxiliary lemma, the Lean development was compiled again. This ensured that the reconstruction followed the real dependency structure rather than a hypothetical one.

## Neutral and Euclidean dependencies

The investigation also sharpened the distinction between neutral Tarski geometry and results requiring Euclidean parallelism.

Not every deferred result had the same logical status.

Some missing facts belonged to the ordinary derived theory of betweenness, congruence, midpoint and central symmetry.

Others involved parallel lines and parallelogram recognition and therefore required closer examination of their relation to the Euclidean parallel postulate.

This distinction became important for deciding whether a remaining assumption should simply be proved from the current neutral theory or isolated as part of an explicitly Euclidean layer.

## Consequence for the project methodology

The Tarski route now serves a second purpose beyond providing another proof of Finlay's theorem.

It provides a controlled environment for reducing a working synthetic proof to its actual foundational dependencies.

The direction of development has therefore reversed.

The earlier question was:

> What theory is needed to make the proof work?

The new question is:

> How much of the theory currently supporting the proof can be derived from weaker assumptions?

This turns temporary axioms into research targets rather than permanent features of the library.

## Result

The remaining Tarski assumptions were reorganized as explicit proof obligations.

A systematic reconstruction method was established using the actual dependency graph of `FinlayProofTarski` together with known Tarski proofs as guides.

The next stage is the elimination of individual deferred axioms by reconstructing precisely the lemmas required for their proofs.
