# Log 044 --- Completing Book Zero

## 1. Completing the first reconstruction stage

The reconstruction of the Beeson--Narboux--Wiedijk Book Zero has now
been completed over the CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation.

From the beginning of this experiment, the objective was not merely to
reproduce the BNW theorem statements in Lean 4. The purpose was to
determine whether the elementary synthetic geometry isolated by Beeson,
Narboux, and Wiedijk could be reconstructed directly from the Hilbert
axioms together with the previously developed derived theory.

The completion of Book Zero therefore marks more than the end of a
particular collection of theorems.

It provides the first opportunity to evaluate one of the original
hypotheses of CGJteamLab:

> Can a substantial working language of classical geometry be
> reconstructed from a relatively small foundational axiom system, or
> must such a language be introduced through an ever-growing collection
> of additional interface axioms?

The reconstruction of Book Zero now allows this question to be examined
using an independently developed proof corpus rather than isolated
examples.

## 2. What Book Zero is

An important distinction should be maintained throughout this
discussion.

According to Beeson, Narboux, and Wiedijk, Book Zero is not a
replacement for Euclid's Book I and it is not presented as a new
axiomatization of geometry.

Instead, Book Zero consists of elementary derived results that Euclid
uses implicitly but does not formulate explicitly as propositions. Its
purpose is to make those preliminary facts available before the formal
verification of Book I begins.

Consequently, the completion of Book Zero does not mean that the whole
of classical geometry has been reconstructed.

It means that the preliminary synthetic machinery isolated by BNW is now
available over the CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation.

## 3. What the experiment has shown

The significance of the reconstruction is therefore not that another
theorem corpus has been translated into Lean.

The more interesting observation concerns the behaviour of the temporary
Hilbert interface developed during the earlier stages of CGJteamLab.

Originally, several interface axioms were introduced whenever an
elementary geometric result required by a classical proof had not yet
been reconstructed.

These axioms were never intended to become permanent additions to the
foundation. Instead, they represented derived mathematics whose
reconstruction had merely been postponed.

Book Zero has now provided the first systematic opportunity to revisit
those decisions.

As the reconstruction progressed, the temporary interface axioms
gradually disappeared. Results concerning ray order, half-plane
reasoning, segment comparison, congruence transport, and angle addition
became ordinary derived theorems built from the accumulated Book Zero
infrastructure rather than remaining primitive assumptions.

## 4. The remaining primitive assumptions

The reconstruction has now reached a point where the remaining primitive
assumptions can be examined individually.

This is an important moment in the experiment.

If the temporary interface had continued to grow throughout the
reconstruction, one possible conclusion would have been that the Hilbert
foundation by itself is not a sufficiently convenient basis for formal
synthetic geometry.
