# Log 044 --- Completing Book Zero

## 1. Completing Book Zero

The reconstruction of the Beeson--Narboux--Wiedijk Book Zero over the
CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation has now been completed.

Book Zero was originally introduced by Beeson, Narboux and Wiedijk as the
collection of elementary geometric results that Euclid repeatedly uses without
stating them explicitly. The purpose of the present reconstruction was not
simply to translate this material into Lean 4, but to determine whether the
same preliminary geometry could be derived directly from the Hilbert axioms
and the synthetic infrastructure already developed inside CGJteamLab.

The completion of Book Zero therefore marks the end of the first major stage
of the project.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Temporary interface and Book Zero

During the first stages of the project the Hilbert interface contained several
temporary axioms introduced whenever a classical proof required elementary
geometry that had not yet been reconstructed.

This was always regarded as a provisional solution. The expectation was that
most of these assumptions would eventually become ordinary derived theorems as
the underlying synthetic theory matured.

The reconstruction of Book Zero provided the first opportunity to verify this
expectation on a larger and independent proof corpus.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Results of the reconstruction

The outcome turned out to be stronger than originally expected.

The temporary interface gradually disappeared as additional Book Zero theorems
became available. Results concerning rays, same-side arguments, segment
comparison, congruence transformations and angle addition could all be proved
within the reconstructed theory instead of remaining primitive assumptions.

The most technically demanding part of the reconstruction was not the
individual theorems themselves, but the gradual construction of the reusable
machinery needed to support them. Once this infrastructure was available,
later interface axioms could often be eliminated with relatively small local
changes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Remaining primitive assumptions

After completing Book Zero only two local interface assumptions remain.

```lean
bookZero_nullSegment1
bookZero_nullSegment2
```

Unlike the eliminated interface axioms, these do not correspond to missing
Book Zero theorems. They represent explicit assumptions concerning null
segments adopted in the current formalization and therefore remain outside the
BNW reconstruction itself.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Current architecture

The reconstruction suggests the following working architecture.

```text
Hilbert axioms
      |
      v
Book Zero
      |
      v
Derived synthetic interface
      |
      v
Classical geometric proofs
```

This picture should still be regarded as a working hypothesis rather than a
final design. Nevertheless, the experiment demonstrates that a substantial
part of the practical language of classical geometry can be reconstructed
instead of postulated.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Lessons learned

The completion of Book Zero confirms that introducing temporary interface
axioms early in the project was a useful engineering decision, but not a
permanent mathematical one.

As the synthetic theory expanded, most of these assumptions naturally evolved
into ordinary theorems. The interface therefore became progressively smaller,
while the reusable derived geometry became substantially richer.

This experience supports the strategy adopted in CGJteamLab: reconstruct the
elementary theory first, and introduce new primitive assumptions only when
their necessity has been carefully verified.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Next stage

The next step is the reconstruction of the remaining BNW development leading
through Euclid's Book I.

The principal objective is no longer to build elementary infrastructure but to
identify which higher-level constructions repeatedly appear in classical
geometry and therefore deserve to become stable components of the long-term
CGJteamLab interface.

Book Zero closes the first stage of the project. The reconstruction of
Euclid's propositions begins the second.
