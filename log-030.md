# Log 030 - The Midsegment Theorem on the Tarski Route

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Project:** CGJteam Lab  
**Status:** Tarski Midsegment Reconstruction

---

## 1. Overview

`MidSegmentTarski.lean` contains the Tarski-based reconstruction of the
Midsegment Theorem.

The theorem is exposed under the neutral typeclass

```lean
[TarskiNeutral Geo]
```

and works entirely with the point-based language developed in
`TarskiInterface`.

Its proof is substantially different from both the Hilbert and Suppes routes.

The central idea is to construct a new point by central symmetry and then
recognize a parallelogram.

For

$$
P=\operatorname{Mid}(B,C),
\qquad
Q=\operatorname{Mid}(A,C),
$$

the proof constructs a point \(X\) such that

$$
Q=\operatorname{Mid}(P,X).
$$

The resulting configuration is then used to prove the required parallel
relation.

---

## 2. The Target Configuration

The theorem begins with a nondegenerate triangle \(ABC\) and the midpoint
points

$$
P=\operatorname{Mid}(B,C),
\qquad
Q=\operatorname{Mid}(A,C).
$$

The desired conclusion is the Tarski strict-parallel relation between the
midsegment \(QP\) and the side \(AB\).

Schematically:

```text
        A
       / \
      /   \
     Q-----P
    /       \
   C---------B
```

with

```text
Q = midpoint AC
P = midpoint BC
```

and the target

```text
AB || QP.
```

Parallelism here is the Tarski-specific strict parallel relation rather than a
primitive Euclidean parallel predicate.

---

## 3. Constructing the Auxiliary Point

The main auxiliary construction introduces a point \(X\) symmetric to \(P\)
with respect to \(Q\).

The construction gives

$$
Q=\operatorname{Mid}(P,X).
$$

Thus \(P,Q,X\) lie on one line and

$$
PQ \equiv QX.
$$

The proof architecture begins:

```text
P = Mid(B,C)
Q = Mid(A,C)
       |
       v
construct X
       |
       v
Q = Mid(P,X).
```

This auxiliary point plays the role that a parallelogram construction plays
more directly in other foundations.

---

## 4. Central Symmetry and Congruence

The midpoint configuration allows the proof to compare the segment \(AX\)
with \(BP\).

The relevant step uses the central-symmetry congruence machinery developed in
the Tarski interface.

Geometrically, \(Q\) is simultaneously involved in the midpoint structure of
\(AC\) and \(PX\), so the central symmetry centered at \(Q\) exchanges the
corresponding endpoints.

This yields the required segment congruence

$$
AX \equiv BP.
$$

Schematically:

```text
Q = Mid(A,C)
Q = Mid(P,X)
       |
       v
central symmetry
       |
       v
AX congruent BP.
```

This is one of the points where the Tarski proof uses congruence rather than
angles.

---

## 5. Obtaining Parallelism

The same central-symmetry configuration is used to obtain a strict parallel
relation between the corresponding segments.

The proof reaches

```text
AX || BP.
```

Thus the auxiliary construction now supplies both:

```text
AX congruent BP
AX parallel BP.
```

These two facts are exactly the input required by the later parallelogram
recognition theorem.

---

## 6. Parallelogram Cases

The theorem

```lean
tarski_parallel_congruent_parallelogram_cases
```

does not immediately return one oriented parallelogram.

Instead, it produces two possible vertex orders.

Schematically:

```text
AX congruent BP
AX || BP
      |
      v
TarskiParallelogram A X B P
        OR
TarskiParallelogram A X P B.
```

This disjunction is mathematically significant.

The proof must determine which orientation is compatible with the original
triangle configuration.

---

## 7. Eliminating the Wrong Orientation

The first possible orientation,

```text
TarskiParallelogram A X B P,
```

is shown to be impossible.

The argument uses the midpoint structure of the diagonals together with
midpoint uniqueness and the collinearity information already present in the
configuration.

Assuming the wrong parallelogram orientation eventually forces a forbidden
collinearity relation among the vertices of the original triangle.

This contradicts the nondegeneracy hypothesis.

Thus:

```text
TarskiParallelogram A X B P
        |
        v
midpoint consequences
        |
        v
collinearity contradiction
        |
        v
False.
```

The proof therefore selects the second case.

---

## 8. The Correct Parallelogram

After eliminating the wrong orientation, the proof obtains

```text
TarskiParallelogram A X P B.
```

This is the decisive geometric configuration.

From the opposite-side theorem for Tarski parallelograms, the proof derives

```text
AB || XP.
```

Thus:

```text
TarskiParallelogram A X P B
        |
        v
opposite sides parallel
        |
        v
AB || XP.
```

The auxiliary point \(X\) has now served its main purpose.

---

## 9. Returning to the Midsegment

Since

$$
Q=\operatorname{Mid}(P,X),
$$

the points \(P,Q,X\) are collinear.

Therefore the line determined by \(XP\) is the same line as the one determined
by \(QP\).

Using the Tarski collinearity-transfer machinery, the proof converts

```text
AB || XP
```

into

```text
AB || QP.
```

This is the desired Midsegment Theorem.

The final route is therefore:

```text
AB || XP
   +
P,Q,X collinear
   |
   v
AB || QP.
```

---

## 10. Complete Mathematical Route

The complete proof can be summarized as:

```text
triangle ABC
     |
     +--> P = Mid(B,C)
     |
     `--> Q = Mid(A,C)
              |
              v
        construct X
              |
              v
        Q = Mid(P,X)
              |
              +--> AX congruent BP
              |
              +--> AX || BP
              |
              v
      parallelogram cases
              |
              v
      eliminate wrong orientation
              |
              v
      TarskiParallelogram A X P B
              |
              v
          AB || XP
              |
              + P,Q,X collinear
              |
              v
          AB || QP.
```

This is a genuine synthetic Tarski proof rather than a wrapper around a
pre-existing shared Midsegment Theorem.

## 11. The Neutral Typeclass

The theorem is declared under

```lean
[TarskiNeutral Geo].
```

This is an important feature of the current code.

Neither

```text
TarskiPlane
```

nor

```text
TarskiEuclideanPlane
```

appears in the theorem signature.

At the level of Lean typeclass requirements, the proof is therefore
formulated as neutral Tarski geometry.

However, this does not yet establish that the theorem has been reduced solely
to Ax.1-Ax.7.

Several results used by the proof are still represented as deferred axioms in
`TarskiInterface`.

The distinction is:

```text
typeclass level:
    TarskiNeutral

complete logical reduction:
    not yet established.
```

## 12. Deferred Dependencies Used by the Proof

The current Midsegment route uses interface results whose dependency trees
still contain explicit assumptions.

These include the deferred declarations

```text
tarski_central_symmetry_congruent
tarski_central_symmetry_parallel
tarski_parallel_congruent_parallelogram_cases
tarski_midpoint_unique
tarski_parallelogram_opposite_parallel.
```

The proof also uses

```text
tarski_collinear_trans,
```

whose current reconstruction passes through the deferred outer-connectivity
result identified in Log 029.

Thus the theorem should not yet be described as a complete derivation from
Ax.1-Ax.7 alone.

A more accurate dependency picture is:

```text
TarskiNeutral
      |
      v
substantial derived Tarski theory
      |
      +--> deferred central-symmetry results
      +--> deferred parallelogram result
      +--> deferred midpoint uniqueness
      +--> deferred outer connectivity
      |
      v
MidsegmentTheoremTarski.
```

## 14. Comparison with the Suppes Route

The Suppes proof is much shorter:

```text
PrimTriangle
    |
theorem11
    |
PrimParallelogram
    |
parallel side theorem
    |
Midsegment.
```

This compactness comes from the fact that midpoint and doubling are primitive
operations in the active Suppes language and a strong midpoint-based
parallelogram theory has already been developed.

The Tarski route starts lower.

Midpoint itself is defined from betweenness and congruence, and the proof must
construct and analyze the auxiliary affine configuration explicitly.

Thus:

```text
Suppes
midpoint algebra -> parallelogram -> parallelism

Tarski
betweenness/congruence
    -> midpoint
    -> central symmetry
    -> parallelogram cases
    -> parallelism.
```

---

## 15. Comparison with the Hilbert Route

The Hilbert Midsegment proof proceeds through congruence and angle theory:

```text
auxiliary construction
    |
SAS
    |
equal angles
    |
parallelism
    |
parallelogram reasoning
    |
Midsegment.
```

The Tarski route contains no primitive angle theory.

Instead:

```text
auxiliary symmetric point
    |
segment congruence
    |
strict parallelism
    |
parallelogram cases
    |
Midsegment.
```

The two proofs therefore use genuinely different geometric mechanisms.

---

## 16. Three Routes to the Same Configuration

The project now contains three structurally distinct Midsegment routes:

```text
Hilbert

construction
 -> SAS
 -> equal angles
 -> parallelogram / parallel transfer
 -> Midsegment
```

```text
Suppes

Theorem 11
 -> primitive parallelogram
 -> parallelism
 -> Midsegment
```

```text
Tarski

symmetric point
 -> congruence + strict parallelism
 -> parallelogram cases
 -> orientation elimination
 -> opposite-side parallelism
 -> collinear transfer
 -> Midsegment
```

This comparison is one of the main mathematical results of the multi-route
formalization.

The theorem statement is classical, but the internal proof architecture
depends strongly on the chosen foundational language.

---

## 17. Architectural Consequence

`MidSegmentTarski.lean` should remain a high-level proof module.

The deferred principles it uses are general Tarski geometry:

```text
central symmetry,
midpoint uniqueness,
parallelogram recognition,
opposite-side parallelism,
collinearity connectivity.
```

They do not belong specifically to the Midsegment Theorem.

Therefore the correct long-term direction is to reduce these results inside
the Tarski theory or interface while leaving the high-level Midsegment proof
essentially unchanged.

This follows the same design principle already used on the Suppes route:
general missing mathematics should be moved downward rather than expanded
inside a classical proof.

---

## 18. Conclusion

The Tarski Midsegment proof is a substantial synthetic argument built from a
very small primitive language.

Its characteristic construction is the introduction of a point \(X\) such
that

$$
Q=\operatorname{Mid}(P,X).
$$

From this central-symmetry configuration the proof obtains congruence and
parallelism, recognizes the correct parallelogram orientation, and finally
transfers parallelism back to the actual midpoint segment.

The mathematical route is:

```text
midpoints
   |
central symmetry
   |
congruence + parallelism
   |
parallelogram cases
   |
orientation elimination
   |
opposite sides parallel
   |
collinear transfer
   |
Midsegment Theorem.
```

The theorem is exposed at the `TarskiNeutral` level, but its complete
reduction to the neutral Tarski axioms remains unfinished because several
general interface theorems are still represented as explicit assumptions.

The next stage is to inspect the Tarski version of Finlay's proof and
determine which additional parts of the Tarski affine theory are required
beyond this Midsegment result.
