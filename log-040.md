# Log 038 --- Eliminating a Tarski Midpoint Axiom

## Objective

The next stage of the Tarski reduction focused on one of the remaining
local axioms supporting the active proof route.

The goal was to determine whether the required midpoint result could be
reconstructed from the existing neutral Tarski theory rather than
retained as an independent assumption.

This was the first direct application of the dependency-reconstruction
method established in the previous session.

## Isolating the target

The target axiom occurred on the actual dependency path of
`FinlayProofTarski`.

Instead of developing a general theory around it, the investigation
began from the exact statement required by the current proof and traced
only the dependencies needed to establish that statement.

This kept the reconstruction local and made the logical cost of the
result visible.

## Midpoints and central symmetry

The proof uses the Tarski representation of midpoint through betweenness
and congruence.

A midpoint relation therefore carries two pieces of information:

-   collinearity through betweenness,
-   equality of the two corresponding segments.

The reconstruction also uses the central-symmetry pattern already
present in the Tarski development: given a center and a point, an
opposite point can be introduced so that the center is the midpoint of
the resulting segment.

This provides a way to transfer geometric information between a
configuration and its image under point reflection.

## Degenerate configurations

A substantial part of the formal proof concerned cases that are usually
suppressed in an informal Euclidean argument.

Lean requires these configurations to be handled explicitly.

In particular, midpoint constructions can collapse when points coincide,
and collinearity statements must still be transported through the
resulting equalities.

The proof was therefore organized by separating the nondegenerate
geometric case from the degenerate cases and reducing the latter
directly by midpoint identities and congruence properties.

## Collinearity transport

The reconstruction required repeated manipulation of Tarski
collinearity.

Since Tarski collinearity is derived from betweenness rather than taken
as a primitive symmetric relation, permutations of collinear triples
must be justified through the corresponding derived lemmas.

The existing collinearity symmetry and cycle results were sufficient for
these transformations.

This confirmed an earlier architectural decision: these properties
belong to the Tarski theory itself rather than to the common
representation layer.

## Dependency reduction

The important result of the reconstruction was not merely that the
target statement could be proved.

It was proved using infrastructure already available below the local
axiom.

Consequently, no new foundational assumption was required.

The dependency structure changed from

    Tarski theory
          ↓
    local midpoint axiom
          ↓
    higher-level Tarski results

to

    Tarski theory
          ↓
    derived midpoint theorem
          ↓
    higher-level Tarski results

## Verification

The local axiom was replaced by the reconstructed theorem.

The complete development compiled successfully after the replacement.

This verified that the axiom had not been carrying any hidden additional
role elsewhere in the active Tarski route.

## Result

A deferred midpoint assumption was eliminated from the Tarski
development.

The result demonstrates that the current reduction strategy can remove
assumptions without redesigning the high-level Finlay proof.

More importantly, it confirms that at least part of the remaining
interface debt consists of derivable Tarski geometry rather than
genuinely additional axiomatic structure.

The remaining assumptions can now be investigated in the same way, while
keeping separate those that may depend on specifically Euclidean
parallelism.
