# Log 017 - Suppes as an Alternative Midsegment Route

## 1. Overview

The reconstruction of Suppes' Theorem 11 has now been completed in Lean.

The result produces the primitive-parallelogram configuration associated with the three midpoint constructions and provides the basis for a Suppes-style route to the Midsegment Theorem.

This development is kept separate from the existing proof in

```lean
MidsegmentParallel.lean
```

so that the two formal routes can be studied independently.

The purpose is comparison rather than replacement.

---

## 2. Two Routes to the Midsegment Configuration

The existing development proves the midsegment result through a synthetic chain involving construction, SAS, angle reasoning, parallelism, and parallelograms.

The Suppes development starts from a different primitive language based on midpoint and doubling operations together with collinearity.

Schematically:

```text
Hilbert-oriented route

construction
    |
    v
SAS and angle reasoning
    |
    v
parallelism
    |
    v
Midsegment result
```

and

```text
Suppes route

midpoint / doubling operations
    |
    v
midpoint identities
    |
    v
primitive parallelogram
    |
    v
Suppes affine consequences
```

The important point is not that these dependency chains are identical.

They are not.

They provide two formal routes toward a common higher-level geometric result.

---

## 3. Connecting the Languages

To use the Suppes development in the existing geometry environment, some relation between the two vocabularies is required.

The Suppes language and the existing synthetic interface do not begin with the same primitives. In particular, the role played by parallelism has to be related explicitly to the structures constructed on the Suppes side.

At this stage the project introduced a bridge layer for that purpose.

The bridge should be understood carefully: it is not yet a proof that the two foundations are equivalent.

Rather, it records the compatibility assumptions or interpretation results required to expose Suppes-derived information through the higher-level geometry interface.

This distinction is essential:

```text
Suppes theory
     |
     v
bridge / interpretation
     |
     v
shared higher-level vocabulary
```

A bridge may contain genuine theorems, explicit assumptions, or temporary compatibility conditions. Reverse engineering must distinguish these cases later.

---

## 4. The Degenerate Case

The comparison also exposed a technical difference in the treatment of parallelism.

Suppes' formulation of parallel segments is tied to the nondegenerate affine configuration used in his definitions. Consequently, collinear or degenerate cases cannot simply be identified with the behavior of the existing `Geo.Parallel` relation without further analysis.

This is not merely a Lean inconvenience.

It is evidence that apparently familiar geometric vocabulary can encode slightly different logical conditions in different axiomatic systems.

Any common interface must therefore specify how such boundary cases are interpreted.

---

## 5. A Suppes-Based Finlay Experiment

A separate file,

```lean
FinlayProofSuppes.lean
```

was introduced to test how far the Finlay argument could be reproduced using the Suppes-based midsegment route.

The original

```lean
FinlayProof.lean
```

was left unchanged.

This separation is methodologically important. It prevents the established development from being rewritten merely to accommodate a new foundation and makes the comparison between the two paths explicit.

At this stage, the Suppes file should be understood as an experimental path rather than as evidence that the complete Finlay proof has already been reduced to Suppes' axioms alone.

---

## 6. What Was Established

By the end of this stage:

- Suppes' Theorem 11 had been reconstructed in Lean,
- the Suppes development had its own namespace and modules,
- the existing midsegment development remained intact,
- a bridge toward the shared geometry vocabulary had been introduced,
- a separate Suppes-based Finlay path had been opened for experimentation.

This is more precise than saying simply that there are now "two proofs of the same theorem."

At the foundational level, the routes still have different languages and require an explicit interpretation layer before their high-level conclusions can be compared directly.

---

## 7. Architectural Consequence

The experiment suggests an architecture with three conceptually distinct levels:

```text
foundation A          foundation B
    |                     |
    v                     v
interpretation / bridge layers
           \             /
            \           /
             v         v
        shared synthetic interface
                 |
                 v
          higher-level proofs
```

This diagram is still provisional.

The Suppes experiment shows why such a separation may be useful, but it does not yet establish which declarations belong permanently in each layer.

In particular, bridge assumptions must not be mistaken for theorems of either foundation.

---

## 8. Outlook

The immediate next task is to inspect the bridge assumptions and determine their logical status.

For each bridge declaration we need to ask:

- Is it derivable from the Suppes axioms?
- Is it an interpretation theorem?
- Is it a temporary compatibility assumption?
- Does the shared interface express exactly the same notion on both sides?

Only after this analysis will it be possible to say precisely how much of the existing Finlay development can be supported independently by the Suppes route.
