# Log-037: Completing the Tarski Route to Finlay's Proof

## Objective

Complete the reconstruction of the Tarski-based proof of Finlay's theorem by removing unnecessary hypotheses from the public interface and organizing the proof into a stable sequence of reusable lemmas.

Unlike the previous refactoring stage, the goal was not to simplify the implementation itself, but to complete the logical dependency chain leading to the final theorem.

---

## Work completed

### Completion of the proof

The remaining part of the Tarski route was reconstructed from the existing development.

The proof now proceeds through the following stages:

1. construction of the auxiliary point,

2. derivation of two pairs of parallel lines,

3. recognition of the parallelogram,

4. extraction of the common midpoint of the diagonals,

5. proof that this midpoint lies on the third median,

6. construction of the final theorem.

The resulting public theorem is

```lean

finlay_tarski

```

which states that if a point lies on the medians from two vertices of a non-collinear triangle, then it also lies on the third median.

---

### Eliminating unnecessary hypotheses

During the reconstruction, the auxiliary assumptions

```lean

A ≠ G

B ≠ G

```

were removed from the public interface.

Instead, they are now derived internally from the geometric assumptions by means of two auxiliary lemmas:

```lean

A_ne_G_from_medians

B_ne_G_from_medians

```

This makes the final theorem mathematically cleaner while preserving the internal proof structure.

---

### Refactoring of the proof structure

The proof was reorganized into independent logical layers.

The current structure of `TarskiFinlayProof.lean` is:

1. Local temporary axioms.

2. Parallel-line lemmas.

3. Parallelogram recognition.

4. Diagonal midpoint extraction.

5. Collinearity lemmas.

6. Non-equality lemmas.

7. `third_median_point`.

8. `finlay_tarski`.

This dependency order now reflects the mathematical structure of the proof rather than the historical order in which it was developed.

---

## Methodological observations

No additional geometric axioms were introduced during the completion of the proof.

The remaining local axioms are intentionally left explicit because they represent statements that have not yet been reconstructed from the primitive Tarski theory.

This follows the project's methodology:

- temporary assumptions remain local to the proof,

- only reconstructed results become part of the reusable interface.

---

## Current status

The Tarski route to Finlay's theorem is now complete in its current reconstructed form.

Future work will focus on replacing the remaining local axioms by formal derivations from the Tarski axiom system and incorporating the resulting theorems into the reusable geometry library.
