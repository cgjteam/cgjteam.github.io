# Log 046 --- The Equilateral Triangle as the First Book I Test

## 1. The first proposition

The reconstruction of Book I begins with Euclid's first proposition:

> On a given finite straight line, construct an equilateral triangle.

Given two distinct points `A` and `B`, the classical construction uses
two circles:

- the circle with centre `A` through `B`,
- the circle with centre `B` through `A`.

Choose an intersection point `C`.

Then

```text
AC = AB
BC = AB
```

and therefore the triangle `ABC` is equilateral.

On paper this is one of the simplest constructions in classical
geometry.

As the first proposition reconstructed after Book Zero, however, it
provides a useful first test of the formal architecture.

## 2. Construction and deduction

The formal reconstruction immediately separates the proposition into two
different problems.

First comes the construction problem:

```text
find C such that

AC = AB
and
BC = AB.
```

Then comes the deductive problem:

```text
AC = AB
BC = AB
----------------
AC = BC.
```

The second part is elementary.

Once the point `C` exists with the two required congruences, the
equilateral conclusion follows from the congruence machinery already
available in the Hilbert development.

The mathematical difficulty of Proposition 1 is therefore concentrated
in the existence of `C`.

## 3. The hidden circle construction

This matters because the CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation is point-based.

The primitive language was not designed around Euclid's circles.
Consequently, Proposition 1 cannot simply reproduce the instruction

```text
draw the circle with centre A through B
```

as a primitive operation.

What Euclid describes geometrically must instead be expressed through an
existence statement in the Hilbert framework.

The required point `C` must satisfy simultaneously

```text
AC congruent AB
BC congruent AB.
```

The first proposition therefore exposes a boundary between two kinds of
infrastructure:

```text
Book Zero
    |
    | elementary synthetic reasoning
    v
segment congruence, order, rays, collinearity, ...

Book I Proposition 1
    |
    | new construction requirement
    v
existence of a point satisfying two distance constraints
```

This construction requirement is not merely another Book Zero rewriting
lemma.

It expresses geometric existence.

## 4. Book Zero does not replace constructions

This is the first important lesson from the implementation of Book I.

Book Zero provides a substantial language for manipulating an existing
configuration.

It tells us how to reason about points, segments, rays, betweenness,
collinearity, and congruence once the relevant objects have been
constructed.

But Proposition 1 requires something different.

It asks for a new point satisfying simultaneous geometric conditions.

The distinction can be summarized as:

```text
Book Zero:
reason about a configuration

Book I:
construct a configuration and then reason about it
```

The first proposition therefore tests not only the theorem layer
developed in Book Zero, but also the construction strength of the
underlying Hilbert theory.

## 5. Once the point exists, the proof collapses

The contrast between construction and deduction is especially clear in
the final part of the proof.

Suppose `C` has already been obtained with

```text
AC congruent AB
BC congruent AB.
```

No sophisticated argument remains.

By symmetry and transitivity of segment congruence, the three sides are
mutually congruent.

The classical conclusion that `ABC` is equilateral is therefore almost
immediate.

The difficulty of Proposition 1 is not distributed uniformly throughout
the proof.

The deductive part is already largely handled by the existing synthetic
infrastructure.

The new obligation is sharply localized in the construction step.

## 6. A first architectural boundary

Proposition 1 therefore reveals a boundary that was much less visible
during the reconstruction of Book Zero.

There is a difference between derived synthetic reasoning and geometric
existence principles.

Book Zero is dominated by the former.

Euclid's propositions introduce the latter explicitly through
constructions.

This suggests that the reconstruction of Book I may reveal not only a
larger theorem interface, but also a distinct collection of reusable
construction principles.

Such a layer should not be designed in advance.

Proposition 1 gives the first concrete example from which its structure
can be studied.

## 7. The first Book I experiment

The equilateral triangle construction is therefore more informative than
its elementary appearance suggests.

The emerging architecture can be represented as:

```text
Hilbert foundation
        |
        v
geometric existence
        |
        v
constructed configuration
        |
        v
Book Zero / derived congruence theory
        |
        v
classical proposition
```

This is a different picture from the one obtained while reconstructing
Book Zero alone.

The first proposition of Book I shows that a working language of
classical geometry requires both reusable deduction and reusable
construction.

The reconstruction of the equilateral triangle is the first point at
which these two components meet.
