# Log 046 --- First Impressions from Reconstructing the Propositions

## 1. Book Zero enters actual use

The reconstruction of Book Zero was initially an experiment in deriving
the elementary synthetic geometry isolated by Beeson, Narboux, and
Wiedijk over the CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation.

With the beginning of the reconstruction of the propositions of Book I,
the character of the experiment changes.

Book Zero is no longer the object being reconstructed.

It is now being used.

This distinction is important because the usefulness of an elementary
theorem layer cannot be judged only by the fact that its theorems can be
proved. The real test begins when larger synthetic arguments attempt to
use them.

The first propositions of Book I therefore provide the first opportunity
to observe Book Zero as a working proof language.

## 2. The propositions look different after Book Zero

One immediate impression is that the propositions do not appear as
isolated large proofs.

After reconstructing Book Zero, substantial parts of their elementary
reasoning are already available as named results.

Steps involving betweenness, collinearity, rays, segment congruence,
transport of congruence, and elementary order properties no longer have
to be reconstructed independently inside each proposition.

This changes the structure of the formalization.

Instead of repeatedly descending from a classical argument directly to
the Hilbert axioms, the proof can often move through an intermediate
synthetic layer:

        |
