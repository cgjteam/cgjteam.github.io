# Log 028 - Finlay's Proof on the Suppes Route

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Project:** CGJteam Lab  
**Status:** Suppes Finlay Reconstruction

---

## 1. Overview

`FinlayProofSuppes.lean` contains the current reconstruction of Finlay's
argument on the Suppes-oriented route.

The high-level proof is now compact and follows the classical five-step
structure closely.

However, unlike the Hilbert route, the Suppes route is not yet fully reduced
to the axioms declared in `SuppesAxioms.lean`.

The current proof combines:

- derived Suppes theorems,
- general bridge or interface results,
- explicit hypotheses describing the constructed configuration,
- a small number of remaining explicit axioms.

The purpose of this log is to identify exactly where these different logical
statuses enter the proof.

---

## 2. The Current Theorem Shape

The theorem is stated using the Suppes vocabulary of midpoint,
collinearity, parallelism, and primitive parallelograms.

The proof receives a configuration containing points

```text
A B C E F G P D
```

together with hypotheses describing:

- midpoint relations,
- collinearities,
- diagonal incidence,
- the final line through the third median.

Unlike the Hilbert theorem reconstructed in Log 024, the current Suppes
version does not construct the entire configuration internally from
noncollinearity of the original triangle.

Instead, substantial parts of the configuration are supplied as theorem
hypotheses.

This distinction is essential when comparing the two routes.

---

## 3. The Five-Step Structure

The proof still follows the classical Finlay architecture:

```text
Step 1
midsegment parallel relations

Step 2
recognize BPCG as a parallelogram

Step 3
use the diagonal configuration

Step 4
obtain the midpoint of BC

Step 5
identify the third median line
```

The mathematical narrative therefore remains stable even though the
foundational support differs from the Hilbert implementation.

---

## 4. Step 1 - Midsegment Parallelism

The first step uses the theorem

```lean
MidsegmentTheoremSuppes
```

to obtain the basic midpoint-generated parallel relation.

As established in Log 027, this theorem itself has a clean route through

```text
PrimTriangle
    |
    v
theorem11
    |
    v
PrimParallelogram
    |
    v
parallelogram_parallel_second
    |
    v
MidsegmentTheoremSuppes.
```

However, Finlay needs the parallel relation transferred from the segment
joining the midpoint points to the larger collinear side.

The current proof uses

```lean
parallel_extend_midsegment
```

for this operation.

At present this declaration is an explicit axiom.

Thus the actual dependency of Step 1 is

```text
MidsegmentTheoremSuppes
        |
        v
midsegment parallel relation
        |
        v
parallel_extend_midsegment   [AXIOM]
        |
        +--> BP || CG
        |
        `--> CP || BG.
```

This identifies the first remaining foundational obligation in the full
Suppes Finlay route.

---

## 5. Step 2 - Recognition of the Parallelogram

The two parallel relations are reoriented using the available
`SuppesParallel` interface.

The proof then invokes

```lean
parallelogram_of_opposite_sides_parallel.
```

This theorem is no longer introduced locally inside `FinlayProofSuppes`.

It is isolated in

```lean
ParallelogramRecognitionSuppes.lean.
```

This is an architectural improvement because the theorem is mathematically
general and not specific to Finlay.

The high-level dependency is

```text
BP || CG
BG || PC
    |
    v
parallelogram_of_opposite_sides_parallel
    |
    v
PrimParallelogram B P C G.
```

---

## 6. The Remaining Assumption Behind Recognition

Although the recognition theorem is now separated from the Finlay proof,
its current derivation is not yet completely reduced to `SuppesAxioms`.

The recognition module contains the explicit assumption

```lean
parallel_vertex_unique.
```

Its role is to identify the point determined by the required parallel
conditions with the canonical fourth vertex constructed by the Suppes
midpoint/doubling machinery.

Schematically:

```text
constructed canonical fourth vertex
        +
point satisfying two parallel conditions
        |
        v
parallel_vertex_unique   [AXIOM]
        |
        v
same point
        |
        v
PrimParallelogram.
```

Therefore Step 2 is theorem-level at the Finlay interface, but its complete
dependency tree still contains one explicit assumption.

---

## 7. Step 3 - The Diagonal Configuration

In the current Suppes theorem, the diagonal configuration is not constructed
inside the proof.

The relevant facts are supplied as hypotheses, including information of the
form

```text
D lies on BC
D lies on PG
A, D, G are collinear
```

or their corresponding Suppes formulations.

Thus Step 3 differs substantially from the Hilbert route.

In the Hilbert proof, the diagonal intersection point `D` is produced from
the parallelogram by a general existence theorem.

In the current Suppes proof, the required incidence configuration is already
part of the theorem input.

This means that the present theorem proves:

> given a Finlay configuration satisfying the required Suppes relations,
> the final median conclusion follows.

It does not yet reconstruct the complete configuration from the primitive
Suppes axioms.

---

## 8. Step 4 - Diagonal Bisection

The central midpoint step uses

```lean
parallelogram_diagonals_bisect.
```

At present this declaration is an explicit axiom.

It supplies the classical theorem:

```text
PrimParallelogram B P C G
        +
D on both diagonals
        |
        v
D is midpoint of BC.
```

In the current implementation, the result is expressed through equality with
the midpoint operation:

$$
D = \operatorname{Mid}(B,C).
$$

Thus Step 4 has the dependency

```text
PrimParallelogram B P C G
        |
        + diagonal incidence hypotheses
        |
        v
parallelogram_diagonals_bisect   [AXIOM]
        |
        v
D = Mid(B,C).
```

This is the third major unreduced mathematical principle visible in the
complete Suppes Finlay route.

---

## 9. Step 5 - The Third Median

Once

$$
D = \operatorname{Mid}(B,C)
$$

has been obtained, the final step is simple.

The proof rewrites the supplied collinearity of `A`, `D`, and `G` using the
midpoint identity.

The result is the collinearity of

```text
A
Mid B C
G.
```

Thus the final median statement follows directly from:

```text
hADG
        +
D = Mid(B,C)
        |
        v
A, Mid(B,C), G collinear.
```

No new geometric construction is required at this stage.

---

## 10. An Unused Triangle Hypothesis

The current theorem includes a hypothesis

```lean
_hT : PrimTriangle A B C
```

which is not used in the proof body.

This is a small but useful dependency observation.

It may indicate one of several possibilities:

- the theorem statement still reflects an earlier proof shape,
- the nondegeneracy information is already encoded in stronger hypotheses,
- the assumption will become necessary again after further reconstruction,
- or it can eventually be removed from the public theorem.

At this stage the correct action is only to record the fact.

The theorem should not be simplified until the intended final interface is
clear.

---

## 11. Three Remaining Foundational Obligations

The current full Suppes route exposes three particularly clear pieces of
foundational debt.

### 1. Parallel extension along the midsegment configuration

```lean
parallel_extend_midsegment
```

currently supplies the passage from the basic Suppes Midsegment Theorem to the
extended parallel relations used by Finlay.

### 2. Uniqueness in parallelogram recognition

```lean
parallel_vertex_unique
```

supports the theorem converting two pairs of opposite parallel sides into a
`PrimParallelogram`.

### 3. Diagonal bisection

```lean
parallelogram_diagonals_bisect
```

supplies the classical midpoint property of the diagonals.

These are not Finlay-specific mathematical ideas.

All three are general affine or parallelogram principles.

This strongly suggests that their eventual home should be the Suppes theory or
interface layer rather than the final proof file.

---

## 12. What Is Already Fully Derived

It is equally important to record what no longer belongs to the foundational
debt.

The current route already derives:

```text
midpoint algebra
primitive parallelogram constructions
Suppes Theorem 11
MidsegmentTheoremSuppes
several parallel reversal operations
substantial parts of parallelogram recognition
```

Thus the proof is not merely a high-level theorem wrapped around arbitrary
assumptions.

A significant amount of the Suppes affine theory has already been
formalized.

The remaining assumptions are concentrated at specific transitions.

## 14. Comparison with the Hilbert Route

The difference between the two current formalizations is now clear.

### Hilbert route

The theorem constructs much of the configuration internally:

```text
triangle
   |
construct E,F
   |
construct G by Pasch
   |
construct P
   |
construct D from parallelogram diagonals
   |
Finlay.
```

### Suppes route

The current theorem starts higher:

```text
given midpoint and collinearity configuration
   |
MidsegmentTheoremSuppes
   |
explicit parallel-extension assumption
   |
parallelogram recognition
   |
given diagonal configuration
   |
explicit diagonal-bisection assumption
   |
Finlay.
```

The two theorems therefore have the same mathematical narrative but not yet
the same constructive strength.

This distinction should remain explicit until the Suppes branch reconstructs
the missing existence and affine theorems.

---

## 15. Architectural Interpretation

The present structure supports a useful design principle.

`FinlayProofSuppes.lean` should remain a short high-level theorem.

Whenever a missing result is mathematically general, it should be moved out of
the Finlay proof and investigated independently.

This has already happened with

```lean
parallelogram_of_opposite_sides_parallel
```

which now lives in its own recognition module.

The same long-term principle applies to:

```lean
parallel_extend_midsegment
parallelogram_diagonals_bisect.
```

They are general theory, not properties peculiar to the centroid theorem.

The exact final module organization can be decided later.

For the current research stage, keeping unresolved components visible is more
important than prematurely optimizing the file structure.

---

## 16. Status of the Suppes Route

The correct status of the current branch is therefore:

> The high-level Finlay argument is formally assembled on the Suppes route,
> but the route is not yet a derivation of the theorem from
> `SuppesAxioms.lean` alone.

The remaining gap is small enough to state precisely.

It consists primarily of general affine results concerning:

```text
parallel extension,
parallel-based parallelogram recognition,
diagonal bisection.
```

This is a substantial improvement over the earlier state of the project, where
the boundary between derived mathematics and compatibility assumptions was
unclear.

---

## 17. Conclusion

The reconstruction of `FinlayProofSuppes.lean` shows that the Suppes route is
now structurally mature enough to expose its exact remaining obligations.

The five-step Finlay argument survives:

```text
midsegment
    |
parallel sides
    |
parallelogram
    |
diagonal midpoint
    |
third median.
```

But the logical support beneath these steps is not yet uniform.

The current route contains three principal unreduced transitions:

```text
midsegment parallelism
        |
parallel_extend_midsegment            [AXIOM]

parallel sides
        |
parallel_vertex_unique                [AXIOM]
        |
parallelogram recognition

parallelogram + diagonal incidence
        |
parallelogram_diagonals_bisect        [AXIOM]
        |
midpoint.
```

This is the central result of the Suppes reconstruction.

The problem is no longer to make the Finlay proof compile.

The problem is now sharply mathematical:

> can these remaining general affine principles be derived from the current
> Suppes axioms?

Until that question is answered, `FinlayProofSuppes` should be read as a
successful high-level reconstruction with explicit, localized foundational
debt.
