# Log 060 --- Entering E^4: a New Incidence Boundary and Synthetic Hyperplanes

## 1. The first nonvisual change is axiomatic, not metric

The passage from E^3 to E^4 is not obtained by taking the completed
three-dimensional library and adding one more coordinate.

The first change occurs earlier.

It occurs already at the level of incidence.

In E^3 the spatial Hilbert axioms contain a genuinely three-dimensional
principle:

```text
two distinct planes with a common point
have a second common point.
```

Geometrically, two distinct intersecting planes in E^3 meet in a line.

That statement is correct in three-space.

It is not a dimension-free ambient principle.

In E^4 two two-dimensional planes may meet in a single point.

Therefore the existing three-dimensional incidence hierarchy cannot simply be
reused as the ambient foundation of E^4.

This is the first structural correction.

The E^4 development must separate

```text
common point-line-plane incidence
```

from

```text
incidence principles that are specifically three-dimensional.
```

The resulting architecture begins with a new primitive boundary.

## 2. The primitive higher-dimensional incidence boundary

The public E^4 incidence route starts from

```text
HilbertIncidence
HilbertPlaneIncidence
HilbertSpacePrimitive
HilbertWylerAxioms
```

The first three interfaces supply the common point-line-plane language.

They provide the objects and the elementary incidence notions already used
throughout the project.

The genuinely new ingredient is

```text
HilbertWylerAxioms.
```

It is defined in

```text
CGJteamLab/HilbertWylerAxioms.lean
```

and is intended as the higher-dimensional replacement for the
three-dimensional spatial part of Hilbert Group I.

Its common fields say, in the current formal language, that

```text
every line contains two distinct points,

every primitive plane contains three noncollinear points,

three noncollinear points lie in a plane,

three noncollinear points determine their plane uniquely,

a plane containing two distinct points of a line contains the whole line.
```

These clauses are the reusable point-line-plane incidence skeleton.

They do not yet say that the ambient geometry is four-dimensional.

They also do not contain Smith I.5, exchange, order, congruence, metric,
orthogonality, reflection, or continuity.

The last field is the important one.

## 3. Wyler I.7 replaces the three-dimensional plane-intersection axiom

The higher-dimensional bridge is encoded by

```text
wyler_i7_intersection_line.
```

Its geometric content is the following.

Take two distinct lines `a` and `b` in a plane `pi`.

Take a point `P` outside `pi`.

Let `alpha` be a plane containing `a` and `P`, and let `beta` be a plane
containing `b` and `P`.

Then `alpha` and `beta` intersect in exactly one line carrier.

Schematically,

```text
            P
           / \
      alpha   beta
        |       |
        a       b
         \     /
            pi
```

and the conclusion is

```text
alpha intersection beta = one line carrier.
```

This is the role played by Wyler I.7 in the new foundation.

The important point is that this is not Hilbert I.7 transported unchanged to
a larger dimension.

It is a different incidence principle, chosen precisely because it still makes
sense in the dimension-free flat calculus.

Thus the change of axioms at the entrance to E^4 is not cosmetic.

The old E^3 ambient plane-intersection principle is removed from the primitive
boundary and replaced by a higher-dimensional Wyler principle.

## 4. Smith I.5 and exchange are no longer axioms

The next module is

```text
CGJteamLab/HilbertWylerTheory.lean
```

and its role is theoretical rather than axiomatic.

From `HilbertWylerAxioms` it constructs the existing Smith incidence core and
then proves

```text
hilbertWyler_implies_smithI5
```

and

```text
hilbertWyler_implies_smithSpanExchange.
```

Thus the logical chain is

```text
common Hilbert point-line-plane incidence
        +
Wyler I.7
        |
        v
Smith I.5
        |
        v
generated-flat theory
        |
        v
Mac Lane-Steinitz exchange.
```

This distinction matters.

The E^4 foundation does not assume simultaneously

```text
Wyler I.7
Smith I.5
exchange.
```

Only the first belongs to the new primitive incidence boundary.

The latter two are consequences.

This is the first major simplification of the four-dimensional architecture.

The flat calculus is therefore theory built on top of incidence, not a second
independent axiom system.

## 5. Exact dimension four is imposed separately

Nothing in `HilbertWylerAxioms` says that the ambient space has dimension four.

The exact dimension is isolated in

```text
CGJteamLab/E4Dimension.lean
```

through the class

```text
E4Dimension.
```

The formulation is closure-theoretic.

No numerical dimension function, basis cardinality, coordinates, vectors, or
linear algebra are introduced.

The key objects are generated Smith spans.

A generated 3-flat is represented by four points

$$
A,B,C,D
$$

such that

```text
A,B,C are noncollinear,
D is outside Span(A,B,C).
```

Formally this is

```text
E4Generated3Flat.
```

A five-point E^4 frame is represented by

$$
A,B,C,D,P
$$

with

```text
A,B,C noncollinear,
D outside Span(A,B,C),
P outside Span(A,B,C,D).
```

Formally this is

```text
E4Frame.
```

The dimension class then has two logically separate clauses.

## 6. The lower bound: dimension at least four

The field

```text
frame_exists
```

asserts that an E^4 frame exists.

Geometrically,

```text
three noncollinear points
        |
        v
a genuine plane
        |
        + external point D
        v
a genuine generated 3-flat
        |
        + external point P
        v
strict growth once more.
```

This prevents the ambient geometry from collapsing to dimension at most
three.

It is the lower-dimensional nondegeneracy condition

```text
dim >= 4.
```

No hyperplane object is assumed here.

The statement is entirely about growth of generated spans.

## 7. The upper bound: dimension at most four

The second field is

```text
generated3Flat_adjoin_external_eq_univ.
```

It says that once `F` is a genuine generated 3-flat, adjoining any point
outside `F` generates the entire ambient point space:

```text
F a generated 3-flat
P not in F
        |
        v
Span(F union {P}) = univ.
```

This prevents a further independent growth step.

It is the upper bound

```text
dim <= 4.
```

Together the two clauses give exact dimension four:

```text
frame_exists
        +
generated3Flat_adjoin_external_eq_univ
        |
        v
dim = 4.
```

This separation is mathematically cleaner than hiding dimension inside a large
four-dimensional incidence class.

Incidence says how flats behave.

`E4Dimension` says how far independent generation can continue.

## 8. A five-point frame spans the whole space

The first important consequence is

```text
e4Frame_span_eq_univ.
```

Every E^4 frame

$$
A,B,C,D,P
$$

spans the whole ambient point space.

Thus the five-point frame is not merely a witness that the geometry has grown
four times.

It is a spanning frame.

The production theorem

```text
e4Dimension_exists_spanning_frame
```

packages existence of such a frame.

The synthetic picture is therefore

```text
A,B,C
   |
   v
2-flat

+ D
   |
   v
3-flat

+ P
   |
   v
whole E^4.
```

This is the basic dimension-four geometry before any metric structure is
added.

## 9. Hyperplanes are derived, not primitive

The next architectural change is just as important.

The current E^4 route does not postulate a primitive hyperplane type.

Instead,

```text
CGJteamLab/E4Hyperplane.lean
```

defines an E^4 hyperplane as a derived object.

An

```text
E4Hyperplane
```

consists of a carrier which is

```text
a generated 3-flat
```

and

```text
proper, i.e. not equal to the whole ambient point space.
```

So in the present architecture

```text
hyperplane = proper generated 3-flat.
```

This is the correct closure-theoretic analogue of a codimension-one affine
subspace in dimension four.

No separate hyperplane incidence axiom is introduced.

The line and plane closure properties of a hyperplane follow because its
carrier is a Smith flat.

## 10. Four noncoplanar points generate the candidate hyperplane

The file

```text
CGJteamLab/E4HyperplaneIncidence.lean
```

connects the ordinary point-plane language with the closure-theoretic
definition.

Suppose

$$
A,B,C,D
$$

are not coplanar.

First one proves that

```text
A,B,C are noncollinear.
```

Then one proves that

```text
D is outside Span(A,B,C).
```

Hence the four points generate an `E4Generated3Flat`.

The production theorem is

```text
hilbertWyler_noncoplanar4_generates_3flat.
```

This step uses only Hilbert-Wyler incidence.

It does not yet use the upper bound of dimension four.

Thus there is an important logical separation:

```text
noncoplanar quadruple
        |
        v
generated 3-flat
```

is incidence theory,

whereas

```text
generated 3-flat is proper in E^4
```

is a dimension statement.

## 11. Properness is the first genuinely rank-theoretic theorem

The file

```text
CGJteamLab/E4HyperplaneProper.lean
```

proves that an arbitrary generated 3-flat is proper under

```text
HilbertWylerAxioms + E4Dimension.
```

This is the first place where the exchange law and exact dimension interact
substantially.

The proof uses Steinitz-style replacement.

Conceptually, assume that a four-generator 3-flat were already the whole
space.

Take the five-point E^4 frame supplied by `E4Dimension`.

Exchange allows generators from the spanning five-point frame to be replaced
against the alleged four-generator spanning set.

That would force the ambient geometry to be generated with too few independent
growth steps, contradicting the defining E^4 frame.

Thus a genuine generated 3-flat cannot equal the whole space.

This is exactly the synthetic rank argument one expects:

```text
4 independent affine generators
        |
        v
3-flat

not

whole 4-space.
```

The proof is closure-theoretic rather than numerical.

## 12. Hyperplanes become maximal proper flats

Once properness is established, the dimension-four upper bound has an
immediate geometric consequence.

If `Sigma` is an E^4 hyperplane and `P` lies outside it, then

```text
Span(Sigma union {P}) = univ.
```

Therefore there is no proper Smith flat strictly between a hyperplane and the
whole ambient space.

Production proves

```text
e4Hyperplane_maximal.
```

So the derived object has exactly the expected codimension-one behavior:

```text
proper 3-flat
        |
        + one external point
        v
whole E^4.
```

This maximality is not inserted into the definition.

It is a theorem derived from exact dimension four.

## 13. Four noncoplanar points determine a unique hyperplane

The basic incidence core is completed in

```text
CGJteamLab/E4HyperplaneCore.lean.
```

Take four noncoplanar points

$$
A,B,C,D.
$$

Their canonical carrier is

$$
\operatorname{Span}(A,B,C,D).
$$

The preceding results show that this carrier is a proper generated 3-flat,
hence an E^4 hyperplane.

If another E^4 hyperplane contains the same four points, it must contain their
Smith span.

Maximality then forces equality.

This yields the production theorem

```text
hilbertWyler_e4_hyperplane_unique_noncoplanar4.
```

Thus the familiar statement

```text
four noncoplanar points determine a hyperplane
```

is recovered as a theorem.

It is not an axiom of the new E^4 foundation.

## 14. Five nonhyperplanar points also become a theorem

The same file defines

```text
E4Hyperplanar5
```

to mean that five points lie in one derived E^4 hyperplane.

An E^4 frame cannot be hyperplanar.

If all five frame points lay in one hyperplane, then their span would be
contained in that hyperplane.

But the frame spans the whole ambient point space.

The hyperplane would therefore equal the whole space, contradicting its
properness.

Hence `E4Dimension` yields

```text
e4Dimension_exists_five_nonhyperplanar.
```

The familiar four-dimensional nondegeneracy statement

```text
there exist five points not lying in one hyperplane
```

is therefore also derived from the closure-theoretic dimension axiom.

## 15. What has changed relative to E^3?

The transition can now be stated precisely.

In E^3 the ambient spatial incidence theory already contains
three-dimensional plane behavior.

The route is approximately

```text
Hilbert Group I
        |
        v
three-dimensional spatial incidence
        |
        v
planes, plane intersections, spatial geometry.
```

In E^4 that architecture is deliberately not reused.

Instead,

```text
common point-line-plane incidence
        +
HilbertWylerAxioms
        |
        v
Smith I.5 and exchange
        |
        v
E4Dimension
        |
        v
generated 3-flats
        |
        v
derived hyperplanes.
```

This is the central change of Log 060.

The new geometry is not produced by a larger list of four-dimensional
primitives.

It is produced by a smaller and cleaner primitive incidence boundary plus a
separate dimension axiom.

## 16. Why the old Salas route is no longer the public foundation

Historically, the E^4 development passed through a Salas-based facade.

That route was useful during proof search because it led to the same
Smith-Wyler consequences.

After the foundation audit, however, the public production boundary was
replaced.

The current route is

```text
HilbertWylerAxioms
        |
        v
HilbertWylerTheory
        |
        v
E4Dimension.
```

The old Salas files remain useful as a record of an alternative source route,
but they are no longer the public E^4 foundation.

This is not merely a renaming.

The refactor makes the logical roles explicit:

```text
Wyler I.7          = primitive higher-dimensional incidence,

Smith I.5          = derived,

exchange           = derived,

exact dimension 4  = separate assumption,

hyperplane         = derived object.
```

That is the new axiom architecture.

## 17. The geometric checkpoint reached before metric structure

At the end of this stage we already have a substantial synthetic E^4
geometry.

We have

```text
a dimension-independent incidence core,

Wyler I.7 as the higher-dimensional bridge,

Smith I.5 as a theorem,

Steinitz exchange as a theorem,

exact dimension four,

five-point spanning frames,

generated 3-flats,

derived E^4 hyperplanes,

line closure inside hyperplanes,

plane closure inside hyperplanes,

maximality of hyperplanes,

existence of a hyperplane,

a unique hyperplane through four noncoplanar points,

five points not contained in one hyperplane.
```

None of this requires reflection geometry.

At this checkpoint we have not yet added the independent Hilbert metric layers
needed for perpendicularity and reflection.

In particular, Log 060 stops before

```text
order,
congruence,
Euclidean metric,
normal lines,
normal sections,
hyperplane reflections,
Coxeter A4.
```

The point is to isolate the genuinely geometric change first.
