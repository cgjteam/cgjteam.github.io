# Log 027 - The Midsegment Theorem on the Suppes Route

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Project:** CGJteam Lab  
**Status:** Suppes Midsegment Reconstruction

---

## 1. Overview

After reconstructing `SuppesAxioms.lean` and `SuppesInterface.lean`, the next
step is to inspect the actual Midsegment Theorem used on the Suppes route.

The relevant module is

```lean
MidsegmentParallelSuppes.lean.
```

The result is unexpectedly compact.

The theorem does not reconstruct parallelism directly from midpoint algebra.
Instead, it passes through the primitive parallelogram supplied by Suppes'
Theorem 11.

The complete mathematical route is

```text
PrimTriangle A B C
        |
        v
Suppes Theorem 11
        |
        v
PrimParallelogram
  A (Mid A B) (Mid B C) (Mid A C)
        |
        v
parallelogram_parallel_second
        |
        v
SuppesParallel
  (Mid A B) (Mid B C) A (Mid A C)
```

Thus the Midsegment Theorem itself is only a short composition of two results
already established in `SuppesInterface`.

---

## 2. The Theorem Statement

The Lean theorem is

```lean
theorem MidsegmentTheoremSuppes
    (A B C : Point)
    (hT : PrimTriangle A B C) :
    SuppesParallel
      (Mid A B)
      (Mid B C)
      A
      (Mid A C)
```

The only geometric hypothesis is

```lean
PrimTriangle A B C.
```

Since

```text
PrimTriangle A B C
```

means that \(A,B,C\) are noncollinear, the theorem begins directly with a
nondegenerate triangle.

The conclusion states that the segment joining

$$
\operatorname{Mid}(A,B)
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Mid}(B,C)
$$

is Suppes-parallel to the segment joining

$$
A
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Mid}(A,C).
$$

This is the midpoint configuration required later by the Finlay construction.

---

## 3. The First Step: Suppes Theorem 11

The proof first invokes

```lean
theorem11 A B C hT.
```

This produces

```lean
PrimParallelogram
  A
  (Mid A B)
  (Mid B C)
  (Mid A C).
```

Thus the three midpoint constructions are immediately assembled into a
primitive parallelogram.

Schematically:

```text
             Mid(A,B) -------- Mid(B,C)
                /                  /
               /                  /
              A ----------- Mid(A,C)
```

with the vertex ordering encoded by

```text
PrimParallelogram
  A
  (Mid A B)
  (Mid B C)
  (Mid A C).
```

The substantial midpoint algebra required for this fact has already been
proved in `SuppesInterface`.

The Midsegment module does not repeat it.

---

## 4. The Second Step: Extracting Parallelism

The proof then applies

```lean
parallelogram_parallel_second.
```

to the primitive parallelogram obtained above.

This converts the midpoint-based parallelogram description into the derived
parallel relation

```text
SuppesParallel
  (Mid A B)
  (Mid B C)
  A
  (Mid A C).
```

Therefore the complete Lean proof has the form

```text
triangle
   |
   v
primitive parallelogram
   |
   v
parallel relation.
```

No additional geometric construction is required inside
`MidsegmentParallelSuppes.lean`.

---

## 5. Comparison with the Hilbert Midsegment Route

The contrast with the Hilbert-oriented proof is substantial.

The Hilbert route develops the midsegment result through a longer synthetic
chain involving construction, congruence, angles, parallelogram recognition,
and transfer of parallelism.

Schematically:

```text
Hilbert route

midpoint data
    |
auxiliary construction
    |
SAS
    |
equal angles
    |
parallelogram reasoning
    |
parallel transfer
    |
midsegment parallelism
```

The Suppes route is:

```text
Suppes route

PrimTriangle
    |
theorem11
    |
PrimParallelogram
    |
parallelogram_parallel_second
    |
midsegment parallelism
```

This difference does not mean that the Suppes proof contains less
mathematics.

Much of the work has simply moved downward into the theory of midpoint
operations and primitive parallelograms developed in `SuppesInterface`.

---

## 6. Where the Mathematical Work Lives

The compactness of `MidsegmentParallelSuppes.lean` is therefore an
architectural result.

The theorem itself contains only two substantive invocations:

```lean
theorem11
parallelogram_parallel_second.
```

The underlying dependency is closer to

```text
SuppesAxioms
     |
     v
midpoint identities
     |
     v
MidpointParallelogram
     |
     v
theorem11
     |
     v
parallelogram_parallel_second
     |
     v
MidsegmentTheoremSuppes.
```

The proof-specific module is short because the reusable affine theory has
already absorbed the difficult part of the argument.

---

## 7. Dependency on the Remaining Interface Assumptions

`SuppesInterface` still contains explicit assumptions, notably

```lean
suppes_parallel_transitivity
collinear_doubling_common_center.
```

However, inspection of the actual Midsegment proof shows that
`MidsegmentTheoremSuppes` does not invoke the later parallel-symmetry or
parallel-transitivity machinery.

Its direct route is

```text
theorem11
        |
        v
parallelogram_parallel_second.
```

Thus the remaining interface assumptions identified in Log 026 do not lie on
the visible dependency path of this theorem.

This is an important refinement of the axiomatic picture.

The fact that an interface contains additional assumptions does not imply
that every theorem importing the interface depends on all of them.

The precise dependency of `theorem11` and
`parallelogram_parallel_second` remains the relevant question.

---

## 8. A Cleaner Boundary Than Expected

Earlier work on the Suppes route encountered difficult issues involving:

```text
parallel symmetry
parallel reversal
parallel transitivity
parallelogram recognition
central reflection.
```

None of these complications appears in the Midsegment module itself.

The theorem requires only the direction

```text
PrimParallelogram
        |
        v
SuppesParallel.
```

It does not require the converse recognition problem

```text
two pairs of parallel sides
        |
        v
PrimParallelogram.
```

That converse becomes important only later in the Finlay argument.

This separates two logically different problems that had previously appeared
close together during development.

---

## 9. The Recognition Problem Belongs to the Next Stage

The separate module

```lean
ParallelogramRecognitionSuppes.lean
```

addresses the converse problem required later by Finlay:

```text
AB || CD
BC || AD
    |
    v
PrimParallelogram A B C D.
```

That module currently contains an explicit uniqueness assumption,

```lean
parallel_vertex_unique.
```

This assumption is therefore not part of the Midsegment Theorem proved in the
present file.

The dependency boundary is:

```text
MidsegmentTheoremSuppes
        |
        `-- theorem11
        `-- parallelogram_parallel_second

Finlay parallelogram step
        |
        `-- ParallelogramRecognitionSuppes
                |
                `-- parallel_vertex_unique [explicit assumption]
```

This distinction is important for assessing the actual axiomatic strength of
the Suppes route.

---

## 10. Architectural Consequence

The current module structure reveals a useful separation:

```text
SuppesInterface
      |
      v
MidsegmentParallelSuppes
      |
      v
parallel relations required by Finlay
      |
      v
ParallelogramRecognitionSuppes
      |
      v
later Finlay steps
```

`ParallelogramRecognitionSuppes.lean` is mathematically general and may
eventually belong in the Suppes interface itself.

For the current research stage, keeping it separate is useful because it
makes its additional logical dependency visible.

Module organization can be simplified later after the theory and its
remaining assumptions have been fully reconstructed.

---

## 11. Comparison of Axiomatic Debt

The reconstruction now distinguishes two levels of the Suppes route.

### Midsegment level

The active proof uses:

```text
PrimTriangle
theorem11
parallelogram_parallel_second.
```

No additional recognition or transitivity assumption appears in the theorem
module.

### Later Finlay level

Recovering the classical parallelogram from two pairs of parallel sides
requires additional theory.

In the current implementation this includes the separate recognition module
and its explicit assumption

```text
parallel_vertex_unique.
```

Thus the foundational debt is not distributed uniformly across the Suppes
development.

It appears at specific transitions between geometric descriptions.

---

## 12. Conclusion

The Suppes Midsegment Theorem is one of the cleanest parts of the current
alternative route.

Its proof is exactly:

```text
PrimTriangle
     |
     v
Theorem 11
     |
     v
PrimParallelogram
     |
     v
parallel side theorem
     |
     v
MidsegmentTheoremSuppes.
```

The module therefore confirms that Suppes' midpoint-based parallelogram theory
provides a natural route to the midsegment configuration.

More importantly, the reconstruction locates the remaining difficulty
elsewhere.

The problematic converse step

```text
parallel sides -> parallelogram
```

is not required here.

It enters only when the Finlay proof attempts to recognize its auxiliary
quadrilateral as a primitive parallelogram.

The next reconstruction should therefore inspect the current
`FinlayProofSuppes.lean` together with the recognition module and determine
exactly where each remaining explicit assumption enters the complete Finlay
route.
