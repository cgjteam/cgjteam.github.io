# Log 020 - Suppes Parallelism and Parallelogram Recognition

## 1. Overview

The Suppes route to Finlay's proof exposed a structural difference between two descriptions of a parallelogram.

In the synthetic language used by the Finlay argument, a parallelogram is recognized from two pairs of opposite parallel sides. In the Suppes development, `PrimParallelogram` is expressed through a nondegeneracy condition and equality of the diagonal midpoints.

The problem was therefore to connect these descriptions strongly enough to recover the classical Finlay argument above the Suppes foundations.

This work led to three related results:

- a Suppes-oriented parallelogram recognition theorem,
- a usable symmetry interface for `SuppesParallel`,
- a clearer separation between derived results and assumptions that still represented unformalized parts of the affine theory.

After the changes, the project passed `lake build`.

This log uses file and declaration names current at the time of the experiment. Some were reorganized later.

---

## 2. Two Descriptions of a Parallelogram

The recognition principle needed by Finlay has the form

```text
AB || CD
BC || AD
-----------
ABCD is a parallelogram
```

The Suppes representation is different. Schematically,

```text
PrimParallelogram A B C D
```

contains a nondegeneracy condition together with

$$
\operatorname{Mid}(A,C)=\operatorname{Mid}(B,D).
$$

Thus the two pairs of parallel sides produced in the Finlay construction do not unfold directly to `PrimParallelogram`.

A theorem is required to connect the parallel-side characterization with the midpoint-of-diagonals characterization.

This is a genuine mathematical interface problem, not merely a mismatch of Lean syntax.

---

## 3. SuppesParallel

`SuppesParallel` is a derived relation rather than a primitive one.

As a consequence, familiar transformations of a parallel relation are not available by definitional equality. They have to be established as theorems.

The interface required operations corresponding to:

```text
AB || CD  ->  CD || AB
AB || CD  ->  BA || CD
AB || CD  ->  AB || DC
```

together with an appropriate transitivity principle.

In Lean these appeared through results including:

```lean
parallel_symm
parallel_reverse_first
parallel_reverse_second
parallel_transitive
```

Establishing these operations was necessary before the Suppes parallel relation could be used in higher-level synthetic arguments without repeatedly unfolding its internal definition.

---

## 4. Reversing a Segment

One of the technically substantial cases was reversal of the second segment:

```text
AB || CD  ->  AB || DC.
```

After unfolding `SuppesParallel`, the essential difficulty reduced to a collinearity statement involving midpoint and doubling operations.

This affine content was isolated in

```lean
collinear_double_mid_swap
```

with a schematic form

```text
Col C (Dbl A (Mid B D)) D
        |
        v
Col D (Dbl A (Mid B C)) C.
```

The proof uses midpoint identities and elementary collinearity results.

The significance of the lemma is that it separates the affine calculation required for reversal from the higher-level definition of parallelism.

---

## 5. Symmetry and Central Reflection

The symmetry operation

```text
AB || CD  ->  CD || AB
```

led to another affine mechanism.

The midpoint relations involved in the definition can be interpreted through central reflection about a common midpoint. This motivated the auxiliary result

```lean
collinear_central_reflection
```

and the underlying affine principle

```lean
collinear_doubling_common_center.
```

Schematically, the latter expresses preservation of collinearity under a common central reflection:

```text
Col C X D
        |
        v
Col (Dbl X M) (Dbl C M) (Dbl D M).
```

Once symmetry and reversal of the second segment were available, reversal of the first segment could be obtained by composition rather than by duplicating the internal argument.

---

## 6. Transitivity and the Remaining Theoretical Boundary

The transitivity principle required by the interface has the form

```text
ab || pq
ab || rs
T(p,q,r)
---------
pq || rs.
```

and corresponds to a result in Suppes' theory of parallelism.

At the stage recorded by this log, its lower-level derivation had not yet been reconstructed in Lean.

Instead of introducing several implementation-specific assumptions after unfolding `SuppesParallel`, the missing result was kept as one explicit theoretical assumption at the level of the complete geometric statement.

This was an important methodological choice.

If a theorem has not yet been derived, the remaining assumption should expose the mathematical result that is missing, not accidental fragments created by the current Lean representation.

---

## 7. Parallelogram Recognition

A separate development isolated the recognition principle required by Finlay:

```text
two pairs of opposite parallel sides
              |
              v
       PrimParallelogram
```

under the nondegeneracy conditions required by the Suppes definitions.

The proof uses the constructive affine machinery to obtain a canonical fourth vertex and compares its parallelism properties with those supplied in the hypotheses.

The essential remaining issue is uniqueness: the point satisfying the required parallel conditions must agree with the canonical constructed point.

This explains why the symmetry and transitivity properties of `SuppesParallel` are not peripheral conveniences. They are part of the mathematical mechanism needed to connect the two descriptions of a parallelogram.

---

## 8. Returning to Finlay

With the parallelism interface and parallelogram recognition available, the central part of the Finlay argument regains its familiar form:

```text
Midsegment construction
        |
        v
two pairs of opposite parallel sides
        |
        v
parallelogram recognition
        |
        v
PrimParallelogram B P C G
```

The high-level proof no longer needs to unfold the midpoint-based definition of `PrimParallelogram`.

The next required ingredient is diagonal bisection.

In the Suppes-oriented interface this was represented as a general parallelogram principle: an appropriate intersection point of the diagonals is the midpoint of the relevant diagonal.

Once this midpoint information is available, the final median argument follows in the same mathematical configuration as the original Finlay proof.

---

## 9. Derived Results and Explicit Assumptions

The work clarified three different statuses of declarations in the Suppes development.

First are results derived directly from the formalized midpoint and collinearity machinery, including midpoint identities and lemmas such as

```lean
collinear_double_mid_swap.
```

Second are higher-level geometric interface theorems assembled from those results, including symmetry and reversal properties of `SuppesParallel` and the parallelogram recognition theorem.

Third are general affine or Suppes principles whose derivations had not yet been reconstructed and were therefore kept as explicit assumptions.

This distinction is more important than the raw number of axioms or the fact that the project compiles.

It makes the remaining foundational debt visible and identifies the precise mathematical statements still requiring proof.

---

## 10. Status of the Suppes Finlay Route

By the end of this stage, the high-level Finlay route could be completed using the Suppes-oriented interface, and the project passed

```text
lake build
```

without unresolved proof holes in the high-level development.

This does **not** mean that Finlay had been derived from the primitive Suppes axioms alone.

Some general affine principles used by the interface still remained explicit assumptions.

The correct status is therefore:

> the Suppes-oriented Finlay route is structurally complete, while the reduction of all interface assumptions to the underlying Suppes axioms remains an open reverse-engineering problem.

This distinction is essential for interpreting the compilation result.

---

## 11. What This Stage Established

The main result of the work is not the collection of individual technical lemmas.

It is the recovery of a stable mathematical boundary between the Suppes machinery and the classical Finlay narrative:

```text
Suppes midpoint / doubling / collinearity
                |
                v
      SuppesParallel interface
                |
                v
   parallelogram recognition
                |
                v
      classical Finlay argument
```

The experiment shows that the high-level proof can be kept short even while the lower-level theory remains under investigation.

It also provides a precise list of the remaining theoretical obligations.

---

## 12. Outlook

The next task is no longer to add Finlay-specific lemmas.

The remaining assumptions should be studied as independent mathematical questions.

In particular:

- can `collinear_doubling_common_center` be derived from the existing Suppes axioms?
- can the transitivity principle for `SuppesParallel` be reconstructed formally?
- can diagonal bisection be derived at the appropriate level of the Suppes theory?
- which compatibility assumptions are genuinely necessary, and which can be eliminated?

At this point `FinlayProofSuppes` has changed role.

It is no longer primarily a target to be made to compile. It has become a test case for locating the exact boundary between what has already been derived from Suppes' formal system and what still enters through explicit theoretical assumptions.
