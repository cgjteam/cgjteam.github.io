# Log-020
# Suppes Parallelism and Parallelogram Recognition

**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Project:** CGJteam Lab
**Status:** Suppes Finlay Route Completed

---

## 1. Overview

The Suppes route to Finlay's proof exposed a structural difference
between the classical theory of parallelograms and Suppes' primitive
affine formulation.

In the Hilbert-based development, a parallelogram can be recognized from
two pairs of opposite parallel sides. In the Suppes development,
`PrimParallelogram` is defined instead through a triangle condition and
equality of the midpoints of the diagonals.

The immediate problem was therefore not to prove another version of
Finlay's theorem, but to connect these two descriptions strongly enough
for the Finlay construction.

This led to three related developments:

- a recognition theorem for Suppes parallelograms,
- a systematic implementation of the symmetry properties of
  `SuppesParallel`,
- an explicit separation between derived results and the remaining
  affine assumptions used by the interface.

After these changes, the complete project passes `lake build`.

---

## 2. Two Descriptions of a Parallelogram

The classical recognition principle used in Finlay's proof is:

```text
AB || CD
BC || AD
-----------
ABCD is a parallelogram
```

Suppes uses a different primitive definition. Schematically,

```text
PrimParallelogram A B C D
```

requires a nondegenerate triangle together with the condition

```text
Mid A C = Mid B D.
```

Thus the statement

```text
AB || CD and BC || AD
```

does not unfold directly to `PrimParallelogram A B C D`.

This difference became visible in Finlay Step 2. The two applications of
the Suppes Midsegment Theorem provide the required pairs of parallel
sides of `BPCG`, but Lean still needs a theorem converting that data into
the midpoint-based Suppes parallelogram predicate.

The issue is mathematical rather than syntactic: two standard
characterizations of a parallelogram have to be connected inside the
formal theory.

---

## 3. The Role of Suppes Theorem 11

The analysis also clarified the relation between the existing
Midsegment Theorem and Suppes Theorem 11.

Theorem 11 provides the basic midpoint-parallel construction in the
Suppes system. The current `MidsegmentTheoremSuppes` packages essentially
the same affine mechanism in the interface required by the geometry
library.

In Finlay's construction it is applied twice. These two applications
produce the parallel relations needed for the quadrilateral `BPCG`.

This observation removed the need for an additional Finlay-specific
parallelism assumption. The two parallel pairs are consequences of the
midsegment construction itself.

---

## 4. ParallelogramRecognitionSuppes

A separate module was introduced:

```lean
ParallelogramRecognitionSuppes.lean
```

Its purpose is to isolate the classical recognition principle from the
Finlay proof.

The public result has the mathematical form

```text
AB || CD
BC || AD
-----------
PrimParallelogram A B C D
```

under the nondegeneracy hypotheses required by the Suppes definitions.

The proof uses the constructive character of the Suppes theory. Given a
triangle, Suppes Theorem 7 constructs a canonical fourth vertex by
midpoint and doubling operations. The parallelism properties of that
constructed parallelogram are then compared with the two parallel pairs
supplied in the hypotheses.

The remaining issue is uniqueness: the point determined by the two
parallel conditions must coincide with the canonical fourth vertex.
This is where the structural properties of `SuppesParallel` become
essential.

The recognition theorem is therefore not an arbitrary bridge added for
Finlay's proof. It expresses the classical equivalence between two
descriptions of a parallelogram inside the Suppes language.

---

## 5. SuppesParallel as a Derived Geometric Relation

`SuppesParallel` is not a primitive relation. It packages the
construction used by Suppes to define parallel segments through
`PrimTriangle`, `PrimParallelogram`, doubling and collinearity.

Consequently, elementary-looking operations such as reversing one
segment or exchanging the two parallel segments are not definitional
equalities. They require theorems.

The development was organized around the properties collected in
Suppes Theorem 16.

The relevant interface now includes the following transformations:

```text
AB || CD  ->  CD || AB
AB || CD  ->  BA || CD
AB || CD  ->  AB || DC
```

together with the appropriate transitivity property for parallel
segments.

In Lean these are represented by results including:

```lean
parallel_symm
parallel_reverse_first
parallel_reverse_second
parallel_transitive
```

This turns `SuppesParallel` into a usable geometric relation rather than
a definition that must be unfolded repeatedly in higher-level proofs.

## 6. Reversing the Second Segment

A substantial part of the work concerned the property

```text
AB || CD  ->  AB || DC
```

corresponding to Suppes Theorem 16(v).

After unfolding `SuppesParallel`, the main difficulty is not the
parallelogram component but the collinearity component. The required
change of orientation leads to the affine identity encoded by:

```lean
collinear_double_mid_swap
```

Schematically, if

```text
C, Dbl A (Mid B D), D
```

are collinear, then

```text
D, Dbl A (Mid B C), C
```

are collinear.

The proof is carried out using the algebra of midpoint operations and
the basic collinearity lemmas. A key intermediate identity is

```text
Mid X C = Mid D Y
```

where

```text
X = Dbl A (Mid B D)
Y = Dbl A (Mid B C).
```

This isolates the affine content of the reversal operation from the
definition of parallelism itself.

---

## 7. Symmetry and Central Reflection

The symmetry property

```text
AB || CD  ->  CD || AB
```

corresponding to Suppes Theorem 16(ii) requires another affine
observation.

The midpoint conditions appearing in the two descriptions can be read
as central reflections about a common midpoint. If

```text
Mid A X = M
Mid B D = M
Mid C Y = M,
```

then the pairs

```text
A <-> X
B <-> D
C <-> Y
```

are related by the same central reflection.

This led to the auxiliary result

```lean
collinear_central_reflection
```

expressing that collinearity is preserved when the three points are
reflected about a common center.

The underlying affine principle is isolated explicitly as:

```lean
collinear_doubling_common_center
```

which states schematically:

```text
Col C X D
->
Col (Dbl X M) (Dbl C M) (Dbl D M).
```

Mathematically, this is the familiar fact that a central reflection is
an affine transformation and therefore maps lines to lines.

Once `parallel_symm` is available, reversal of the first segment follows
economically from symmetry and reversal of the second segment:

```text
AB || CD
-> CD || AB
-> CD || BA
-> BA || CD.
```

This avoids duplicating the internal proof of the reversal property.

---

## 8. Transitivity and Suppes Theorem 16(iii)

The remaining parallelism property needed by the interface is the
transitivity statement represented by:

```lean
parallel_transitive
```

Its mathematical form is:

```text
ab || pq
ab || rs
T(p,q,r)
---------
pq || rs.
```

This is exactly Suppes Theorem 16(iii).

Rather than splitting its unfolded conclusion into separate technical
assumptions for the parallelogram and collinearity components, the
current interface records the missing theoretical step as one explicit
assumption corresponding to the complete Suppes theorem.

This preserves the mathematical boundary:

```text
Suppes Theorem 16(iii)
        |
        v
parallel_transitive
```

and avoids introducing assumptions whose statements would be artifacts
of the Lean representation of `SuppesParallel`.

---

## 9. Returning to Finlay's Construction

With the parallelism interface and parallelogram recognition theorem in
place, Finlay Step 2 regains its classical form.

The two applications of the Midsegment Theorem give the two pairs of
parallel sides. The recognition theorem then identifies:

```text
BPCG
```

as a Suppes parallelogram.

Thus the high-level proof no longer needs to know that
`PrimParallelogram` is internally defined by midpoint equality.

This is the desired interface boundary:

```text
Midsegment construction
        |
        v
two pairs of opposite parallel sides
        |
        v
parallelogram recognition
        |
        v
PrimParallelogram B P C G
```

The mathematical narrative of Finlay's proof is therefore restored
above the lower-level Suppes machinery.

---

## 10. Diagonals and the Remaining Finlay Steps

The next classical fact required by Finlay's argument is:

> The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

In ordinary synthetic geometry this is a standard theorem. Conversely,
a quadrilateral whose diagonals bisect each other is a parallelogram.

The forward direction is the one required in Finlay Step 4. In the
current Suppes interface it is represented explicitly by the statement
that if `D` lies on both diagonals of a `PrimParallelogram`, then `D` is
the midpoint of the appropriate diagonal.

This separates a classical theorem about parallelograms from the
particular details of the Finlay proof.

Once the midpoint identity is obtained, the final step becomes simple:
the point already known to lie on the relevant line is rewritten using

```text
D = Mid B C.
```

The third median conclusion then follows without another geometric
construction.

---

## 11. Derived Results and Explicit Assumptions

An important outcome of the development is a clearer distinction between
three levels of the Suppes formalization.

First, there are direct consequences of the primitive midpoint and
collinearity algebra. Examples include the midpoint identities and
`collinear_double_mid_swap`.

Second, there are geometric interface theorems built from those
properties, including:

```text
parallel_reverse_second
parallel_symm
parallel_reverse_first
parallelogram_of_opposite_sides_parallel
```

Third, a small number of results are currently exposed as explicit
theoretical assumptions when their derivation has not yet been
formalized at the lower level. These assumptions are stated as general
affine or Suppes results rather than as Finlay-specific shortcuts.

This organization keeps the proof file short while making the remaining
foundational debt visible.

## 12. Current Architecture

The relevant part of the Suppes route can now be viewed as:

```text
FinlayProofSuppes
|
+-- MidsegmentParallelSuppes
|   |
|   `-- SuppesInterface
|
+-- ParallelogramRecognitionSuppes
|   |
|   `-- SuppesInterface
|
`-- classical Finlay steps
    |
    +-- parallelogram recognition
    +-- intersection of diagonals
    `-- diagonal bisection
```

At the interface level, the main mathematical dependencies are:

```text
Suppes midpoint/doubling algebra
        |
        +--> MidsegmentTheoremSuppes
        |
        +--> SuppesParallel properties
        |       |
        |       +--> Theorem 16(ii)
        |       +--> Theorem 16(iii)
        |       +--> Theorem 16(iv)
        |       `--> Theorem 16(v)
        |
        `--> parallelogram recognition
                |
                `--> Finlay Step 2
```

The high-level Finlay proof is consequently insulated from the internal
definition of Suppes parallelism.

## 14. Next Step

The immediate Finlay route is now structurally complete.

The next task is therefore no longer to add local lemmas merely to push
the proof forward. The remaining assumptions should instead be examined
as mathematical questions about the strength of the current Suppes
formalization.

In particular, future work can ask whether:

```text
collinear_doubling_common_center
```

can be derived directly from the existing midpoint and collinearity
axioms, and whether the current interface assumption corresponding to
Suppes Theorem 16(iii) can be replaced by a formal derivation.

The same applies to the classical theorem that the diagonals of a
parallelogram bisect each other.

This changes the role of `FinlayProofSuppes`: it is no longer primarily
a debugging target. It now serves as a test case exposing which parts of
ordinary affine geometry are already derivable from the formalized
Suppes system and which parts still require explicit theoretical
support.
