# Log 016 - Reconstructing Suppes' Midsegment Theorem

## 1. Overview

The project now turns to a substantially different axiomatic route.

The objective is to reconstruct the argument surrounding the Midsegment Theorem in Patrick Suppes' *Quantifier-Free Axioms for Constructive Affine Plane Geometry* (2000) and compare it with the synthetic development already used in the project.

The important difference is architectural.

Suppes' system is organized around primitive operations and relations from which familiar affine configurations are defined. Parallelism is therefore not taken as the starting language for the midsegment argument.

This makes the reconstruction useful not only as another proof of the same theorem, but as a test of how the same geometry appears under a different foundation.

---

## 2. The Primitive Language

In the Suppes system studied here, the basic language includes the midpoint operation, the doubling operation, and a collinearity relation.

We write these schematically as

$$
a \oplus b,
$$

$$
a * b,
$$

and

$$
L(a,b,c).
$$

The midpoint and doubling operations allow constructions to be represented by terms rather than introduced only through existential statements.

This is one of the characteristic features of the quantifier-free approach: geometric constructions are named directly by operations in the language.

The precise logical role of each primitive and axiom must still be read from Suppes' system itself; the important point for the present reconstruction is that the familiar high-level notions used in the earlier Finlay development are not assumed in the same form.

---

## 3. Primitive Parallelograms

A central derived notion is the primitive parallelogram.

For points \(a,b,c,d\), the relevant condition combines non-collinearity with equality of the diagonal midpoints:

$$
P(a,b,c,d)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
T(a,b,c)\ \land\ a\oplus c=b\oplus d.
$$

Thus parallelogram structure is expressed through midpoint algebra rather than introduced through two pairs of parallel sides.

This gives an immediate contrast with the Hilbert-oriented synthetic interface used elsewhere in the project:

```text
parallel-side description
          vs.
equal-diagonal-midpoint description
```

The two descriptions concern the same familiar affine configuration, but they arise from different primitive languages.

---

## 4. Suppes' Midsegment Configuration

The theorem corresponding to the midsegment configuration does more than state one parallel relation.

The constructed points satisfy a primitive-parallelogram condition of the form

$$
P(a\oplus b,\ b\oplus c,\ a\oplus c,\ a).
$$

This is important for the reconstruction.

In the earlier synthetic development, the Midsegment Theorem is consumed through a parallelism interface.

In Suppes' presentation, the midpoint construction naturally produces a parallelogram configuration.

Schematically:

```text
midpoint operations
        |
        v
midpoint identities
        |
        v
primitive parallelogram
        |
        v
affine consequences
```

The same classical configuration is therefore packaged differently by the two theories.

---

## 5. Lean Reconstruction

The Lean experiment follows the Suppes route rather than translating the proof immediately into the existing Hilbert-style interface.

The relevant objects are represented through:

- midpoint operations,
- doubling operations,
- collinearity,
- the derived primitive-parallelogram predicate.

This is deliberate.

If the proof were translated too early into the existing language of parallelism and congruence, the architectural difference between the two foundations would disappear.

The purpose of the reconstruction is precisely to preserve that difference long enough to understand it.

---

## 6. Current Status on 22 July

At the stage recorded by this log, the midpoint identities required for the target theorem had been formalized.

The remaining step concerned a cyclic-permutation property of primitive parallelograms used near the end of Suppes' argument.

Thus the reconstruction was not yet complete.

This status is historical: it records the state of the Suppes path on 22 July rather than the later state of the project.

The important research result at this point was already visible, however. The Midsegment Theorem could be approached without taking the Hilbert-style parallelism interface as the fundamental language.

---

## 7. Comparison with the Existing Path

The two developments can be compared schematically.

The earlier synthetic route exposes:

```text
midpoints
   |
   v
SAS / angle reasoning
   |
   v
parallelism
   |
   v
parallelogram reasoning
```

The Suppes route begins differently:

```text
midpoint and doubling operations
            |
            v
     midpoint identities
            |
            v
 primitive parallelogram
            |
            v
    affine consequences
```

This comparison should not yet be read as an equivalence theorem between the two formal systems.

At this stage it is an architectural observation: the same geometric configuration can be reached through substantially different primitive languages.

---

## 8. Significance

The Suppes reconstruction changes the role of the Midsegment Theorem in the project.

Previously it had served mainly as a theorem needed by Finlay and as a case study in axiom elimination.

Now it also becomes a comparison point between axiomatic systems.

This raises a new question:

> Can different foundations support a common higher-level language of synthetic geometry?

The answer is not yet supplied by this log.

But the Suppes experiment provides the first concrete reason to investigate such an interface.
