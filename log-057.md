# Log 057 --- Coxeter Geometry in the Plane: Reflections, Braid Relations, and Finite Period

## 1. Why the Coxeter development begins in the plane

The Coxeter part of CGJteamLab did not begin in three or four dimensions.

It began with the simplest geometric question in which the
generators-and-relations language can be reconstructed synthetically:

```text
two reflection lines in a Hilbert plane
```

This planar stage became much more than a preliminary example.

It established the transformation language later reused in the spatial
developments, clarified the distinction between a Coxeter relation and its
geometric origin, and exposed the braid relation as a theorem of synthetic
geometry rather than as an imported group-theoretic identity.

The progression was

```text
synthetic line reflection
        |
        v
reflection as a point transformation
        |
        v
reflection words
        |
        v
pairwise Coxeter relations
        |
        +--> p = 2
        |
        +--> p = 3
                |
                v
             braid
        |
        v
general finite period
```

The decisive lesson was that the algebraic relation should appear only after
the geometry has explained why it is true.

## 2. Reflection is reconstructed rather than postulated

The first step was not to introduce an abstract involution.

A line reflection was reconstructed from the existing Hilbert geometry.

For a point on the reflecting axis, the image is the point itself.

For a point off the axis, reflection is characterized by a perpendicular foot
and a midpoint condition. Schematically,

```text
P -- H -- P'
H lies on the axis
PH is perpendicular to the axis
PH is congruent to HP'
```

The initial object is therefore a relation between `P` and `P'`.

Only after existence and uniqueness have been proved is that relation promoted
to an actual point transformation.

This ordering is important.

The formal architecture is

```text
geometric relation
        |
        +--> existence
        |
        +--> uniqueness
        |
        v
point map
        |
        v
equivalence of the point set
```

The resulting reflection is involutive because the geometry proves that the
same perpendicular-midpoint configuration works in the reverse direction.

Thus the first Coxeter relation,

$$
r^2 = 1,
$$

is derived from synthetic geometry.

It is not part of the primitive definition of a generator.

## 3. The transformation layer changes the kind of formalization

This was the point at which the project ceased to be only a collection of
formalized Euclidean propositions.

Once a reflection becomes a genuine transformation, one can compose it with
other reflections.

That introduces a new level of structure:

```text
geometry
    |
reflection transformation
    |
permutation
    |
word in generators
```

The corresponding production modules include

```text
Coxeter/Reflection.lean
Coxeter/ReflectionWords.lean
Coxeter/ReflectionIsometry.lean
Coxeter/CoxeterRelations.lean
```

The detailed declaration registry belongs to the Geometry Library Index
`Geometry.md`. The role of this log is different: it records why these layers
appeared and what mathematical mechanism connects them.

## 4. A relation that holds is not yet a Coxeter exponent

The word layer forced an important distinction.

The statement

$$
(r_1 r_2)^p = 1
$$

only says that the displayed exponent is satisfied.

It does not say that `p` is the least positive exponent.

For Coxeter geometry the stronger notion is essential.

The development therefore distinguishes

```text
ReflectionPairRelation
```

from

```text
ReflectionPairExactPeriod
```

The second contains both the relation and its minimality.

This distinction later became especially important for the general finite
period construction. Closure of a geometric configuration proves a relation;
a separate nonreturn condition proves that no smaller positive exponent works.

The formalization therefore separates

```text
closure
```

from

```text
first return.
```

That distinction is mathematically cleaner than treating every displayed
power relation automatically as a Coxeter exponent.

## 5. Period two: perpendicular axes

The first nontrivial pairwise case is

$$
p_{12}=2.
$$

Geometrically the two reflection axes are perpendicular.

The crucial fact is that reflection in either axis preserves the other axis
setwise.

That permits the reflection data for one axis to be transported through the
other reflection and yields the conjugation identity

$$
r_1 r_2 r_1 = r_2.
$$

Since each generator is involutory,

$$
r_1^2=r_2^2=1,
$$

one obtains

$$
(r_1r_2)^2=1.
$$

Equivalently, the two reflections commute.

This case is already a genuine Coxeter relation, but structurally it is
exceptional. The preserved perpendicular carrier makes the conjugation
collapse immediately back to the original reflection.

The next case does not work that way.

## 6. Period three is the first genuinely noncommutative case

The transition from `p = 2` to `p = 3` was a major conceptual step.

A standard Euclidean description would say that two reflection axes meeting
at an angle of `pi/3` produce a rotation of order three.

That language was deliberately not used.

The project did not introduce

```text
numerical angle measure
trigonometric functions
cosines
coordinates
rotation matrices
```

Instead, the geometric content of the angle was encoded by an equilateral
triangle.

Let `a`, `b`, and `c` be its three median reflection axes.

The proof then becomes a theorem about how reflections transport entire
carrier lines.

The key geometric statements are

$$
r_b(a)=c,
$$

and

$$
r_a(b)=c.
$$

These two transports are the source of the braid relation.

## 7. Why the third median axis matters

The third axis `c` is not an auxiliary construction inserted for convenience.

It is the geometric witness explaining the algebra.

The reflection in `b` sends the carrier `a` to `c`.

The reflection in `a` sends the carrier `b` to the same `c`.

Thus both conjugations produce reflection in one and the same geometric line.

Schematically,

```text
a --reflect in b--> c
b --reflect in a--> c
```

This is the crucial synchronization.

Had the proof produced two unrelated existentially chosen target axes, an
additional identification problem would have remained.

Instead, the construction produces one triple `a`, `b`, `c` and proves both
carrier transports on that same triple.

The braid identity is therefore forced by geometry.

## 8. Carrier transport is the geometric engine

A reflection acts on points, but the Coxeter argument needs control of whole
reflection carriers.

The formal proof therefore developed a reusable pattern:

```text
choose two distinct points on a line
        |
reflect both points
        |
identify their images
        |
use line uniqueness
        |
identify the transported carrier
```

Midpoint transport is central.

The median axes of the equilateral triangle are determined by vertices and
midpoints. Reflection exchanges appropriate vertices, transports the midpoint
configuration, and midpoint uniqueness identifies the reflected midpoint with
the designated midpoint on the target median.

This yields exact carrier equalities such as

$$
r_b(a)=c.
$$

The pointwise reflection theory has now become a theory of geometric transport.

That mechanism later reappears in higher-dimensional form when reflecting
planes and hyperplanes.

## 9. Conjugation turns carrier transport into reflection identities

Once a reflection sends the carrier of one reflection to another carrier, the
corresponding conjugation statement becomes available.

From

$$
r_b(a)=c
$$

the development obtains

$$
r_b r_a r_b = r_c.
$$

From

$$
r_a(b)=c
$$

it obtains

$$
r_a r_b r_a = r_c.
$$

The two sides therefore have the same geometric meaning: both are reflection
in the third median axis.

Consequently,

$$
r_a r_b r_a = r_b r_a r_b.
$$

This is the braid relation.

The order of reasoning matters:

```text
carrier geometry
        |
        v
conjugation
        |
        v
same third reflection
        |
        v
braid relation
```

The braid relation is not guessed algebraically and then verified.

It is a compressed expression of a geometric transport theorem.

## 10. Coxeter braid and Artin braid

The word

$$
r_a r_b r_a = r_b r_a r_b
$$

has the same formal shape as the three-strand Artin braid relation

$$
b_1b_2b_1=b_2b_1b_2.
$$

But the geometric Coxeter generators satisfy additional involution relations,

$$
r_a^2=r_b^2=1.
$$

This distinction is conceptually useful.

The braid relation captures the interaction of the two generators.

The involution relations encode the special fact that the generators are
reflections.

Together they imply the Coxeter order-three relation.

Thus the planar synthetic geometry gives a direct bridge between

```text
geometric carrier transport
        |
        v
braid word
        |
        v
Coxeter relation.
```

## 11. From braid to period three

After the braid relation is established, no further geometry is required.

The remaining argument is a short word calculation.

Using

$$
r_a r_b r_a = r_b r_a r_b
$$

and

$$
r_a^2=r_b^2=1,
$$

one reduces

$$
(r_a r_b)^3
$$

to the identity.

The architecture deliberately separates these stages.

All geometry occurs before the final word reduction:

```text
equilateral configuration
        |
        v
midpoint transport
        |
        v
carrier transport
        |
        v
conjugation
        |
        v
braid
        |
        v
word calculation
        |
        v
(r_a r_b)^3 = 1
```

The word layer then proves minimality, so the positive period is exactly three.

This is the first genuinely noncommutative Coxeter period reconstructed in the
project.

## 12. The general planar finite-period mechanism

After `p = 2` and `p = 3`, the next question was whether the argument could be
understood without introducing numerical angles.

The general development again answered yes.

The central object is not an angle `pi/p`.

It is a finite radial configuration around a center `O`.

Successive radial carriers are related by local reflection transport. The
local step propagates one carrier to the next, producing a finite carrier
telescope.

The general mechanism is

```text
local symmetric configuration
        |
        v
reflection transports one radial carrier to the next
        |
        v
iterate carrier transport
        |
        v
closure after p steps
        |
        v
(r_0 r_1)^p = 1
```

Minimality is controlled separately by nonreturn of the radial carriers before
the final step.

Thus the Coxeter exponent is again interpreted as a first-return phenomenon.

## 13. The square exposed a hidden degeneracy

The attempt to formulate the general radial mechanism produced an important
correction.

An early interface required the midpoint of every two-step chord to be
different from the center.

That looked natural from the nondegenerate isosceles picture.

But it excluded `p = 4`.

For a square, a two-step chord is a diameter and its midpoint is exactly the
center.

Therefore the correct local theory has two branches:

```text
non-diameter case
diameter case
```

The diameter case is not a pathological exception.

It is required by one of the basic finite Coxeter periods.

This is a characteristic example of the value of formalization: a hypothesis
that looked harmless was shown to encode more geometry than intended.

## 14. Orientation replaces numerical angle data

Equal radii and equal side lengths are not sufficient to force a finite radial
sequence to move coherently around the center.

The sequence could backtrack.

The general construction therefore requires an order-theoretic orientation
condition.

The production interface records that successive outer vertices lie on
opposite sides of the appropriate radial line.

This condition supplies the directional information that a coordinate or
angle-measure treatment would hide in a numerical parameter.

The synthetic replacement for

```text
successive angle = pi / p
```

is therefore a combination of

```text
congruence
incidence
Hilbert order
local separation
finite closure.
```

## 15. The remaining existence boundary

The carrier telescope proves the relation once the appropriate finite radial
configuration exists.

The remaining issue is existence of such configurations for arbitrary `p`.

This boundary is made explicit through a synthetic equal half-turn division
interface.

Its role is not to reintroduce numerical angle measure.

It asserts the existence of a finite chain of equal angular steps across a
half-pencil.

Thus the development separates two questions:

```text
if the finite configuration exists,
does it force the Coxeter period?
```

and

```text
does the geometry provide such a configuration for every p?
```

The first belongs to the established Coxeter mechanism.

The second is an independent geometric existence problem.

That separation later became important when deciding which assumptions belong
to the reusable Coxeter theory and which belong to a particular ambient
geometry.

## 16. Why the two-dimensional development mattered for higher dimensions

The planar work supplied the local engine for the later `A3` and `A4`
developments.

The essential insight is that a relation between two reflections is controlled
in a two-dimensional active geometry.

In higher dimensions the reflecting objects change:

```text
line in E2
plane in E3
hyperplane in E4
```

but a pairwise Coxeter relation can still be reduced to a suitable normal
two-plane.

The planar theory therefore was not discarded when the project moved to
higher dimensions.

It became the reusable local theory to which higher-dimensional reflection
geometry is reduced.

This explains the later architecture:

```text
ambient reflection geometry
        |
        v
normal two-plane
        |
        v
planar reflection axes
        |
        v
2D Coxeter relation
        |
        v
transport back to ambient space
```

The higher-dimensional problem is largely the problem of constructing this
reduction synthetically and proving that it preserves the relevant reflection
data.

## 17. Formalization and library architecture

The planar Coxeter stage contributed several reusable ideas to the Geometry
Library.

It established:

```text
reflection as a derived transformation
reflection as an equivalence
words in geometric generators
exact period rather than mere satisfied relation
carrier transport
conjugation by geometric transport
braid as a geometric theorem
finite carrier recurrence
first-return interpretation of Coxeter period
```

These are not proposition-specific facts.

They form a transformation layer over synthetic Hilbert geometry.

The permanent declaration-level organization belongs in `Geometry.md`.
The present log records the methodological result: Coxeter relations can be
reconstructed from the geometry of carriers without replacing the synthetic
theory by coordinates, trigonometry, or an abstract reflection-group model.

## 18. Conclusion

The first Coxeter stage of CGJteamLab is fundamentally two-dimensional.

It begins by reconstructing a reflection from Hilbert geometry and ends with a
general mechanism for finite pairwise periods.

The decisive intermediate result is the braid relation.

For the equilateral configuration, the two conjugations

$$
r_b r_a r_b
$$

and

$$
r_a r_b r_a
$$

are equal because both are reflection in the same third median axis.

Thus

$$
r_a r_b r_a = r_b r_a r_b.
$$

With involutivity this gives

$$
(r_a r_b)^3=1,
$$

and the word layer proves that the period is exactly three.

This is the central methodological pattern established by the planar work:

> first prove how geometry transports the reflecting carriers; only then read
> the resulting equality as a relation between generators.

The later `A3` and `A4` developments build on this mechanism rather than
replacing it.
