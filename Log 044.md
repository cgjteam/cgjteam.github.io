# Log 044 --- Completing Book Zero

## 1. Completing the first reconstruction stage

The reconstruction of the Beeson--Narboux--Wiedijk Book Zero has now been
completed over the CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation.

From the beginning of this experiment, the objective was not merely to
reproduce the BNW theorem statements in Lean 4. The purpose was to determine
whether the elementary synthetic geometry isolated by Beeson, Narboux, and
Wiedijk could be reconstructed directly from the Hilbert axioms together with
the previously developed derived theory.

The completion of Book Zero therefore marks more than the end of a particular
collection of theorems.

It provides the first opportunity to evaluate one of the original hypotheses
of CGJteamLab.

Can a substantial working language of classical geometry be reconstructed from
a relatively small foundational axiom system, or must such a language be
introduced through an ever-growing collection of additional interface axioms?

The reconstruction of Book Zero now allows this question to be examined using
an independently developed proof corpus rather than isolated examples.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. What Book Zero is

An important distinction should be maintained throughout this discussion.

According to Beeson, Narboux, and Wiedijk, Book Zero is not a replacement for
Euclid's Book I and it is not presented as a new axiomatization of geometry.

Instead, Book Zero consists of elementary derived results that Euclid uses
implicitly but does not formulate explicitly as propositions.

Its purpose is to make those preliminary facts available before the formal
verification of Book I begins.

Consequently, the completion of Book Zero does not mean that the whole of
classical geometry has been reconstructed.

It means that the preliminary synthetic machinery isolated by BNW is now
available over the CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. What the experiment has shown

The significance of the reconstruction is therefore not that another theorem
corpus has been translated into Lean.

The more interesting observation concerns the behaviour of the temporary
Hilbert interface developed during the earlier stages of CGJteamLab.

Originally, several interface axioms were introduced whenever an elementary
geometric result required by a classical proof had not yet been reconstructed.

These axioms were never intended to become permanent additions to the
foundation.

Instead, they represented derived mathematics whose reconstruction had merely
been postponed.

Book Zero has now provided the first systematic opportunity to revisit those
decisions.

As the reconstruction progressed, the temporary interface axioms gradually
disappeared.

Results concerning ray order, half-plane reasoning, segment comparison,
congruence transport and angle addition became ordinary derived theorems built
from the accumulated Book Zero infrastructure rather than remaining primitive
assumptions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. The remaining primitive assumptions

The reconstruction has now reached a point where the remaining primitive
assumptions can be examined individually.

This is an important moment in the experiment.

If the temporary interface had continued to grow throughout the reconstruction,
one possible conclusion would have been that the Hilbert foundation by itself
is not a sufficiently convenient basis for formal synthetic geometry.

The present outcome points in a different direction.

After completing Book Zero, almost all temporary interface axioms have been
replaced by formally verified theorems.

Only two primitive interface assumptions remain:

```lean
bookZero_nullSegment1
bookZero_nullSegment2
```

Their status is different from that of the temporary interface axioms that have
now disappeared.

They are not missing Book Zero theorems.

Nor do they belong to Hilbert's original axiomatics.

Instead, they express local assumptions concerning the behaviour of null
segments that were introduced explicitly in the present formal model.

Their continued presence therefore should not be interpreted as evidence that
the BNW reconstruction is incomplete.

Rather, they identify a small part of the current CGJteamLab formalization that
lies outside both the original Hilbert system and the BNW Book Zero corpus.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Consequences for the architecture

This observation has an architectural consequence.

At the beginning of the project, the Hilbert interface was primarily a
collection of auxiliary assumptions required by the first formalizations of
classical proofs.

After the completion of Book Zero, its role has changed.

The interface is no longer expanding through additional primitive assumptions.

Instead, it increasingly consists of derived synthetic results established from
the Hilbert axioms and the accumulated Book Zero theory.

Schematically, the current picture is closer to

```text
Hilbert axioms
      |
      v
reconstructed Book Zero
      |
      v
derived synthetic interface
      |
      v
classical geometric proofs
```

This picture should still be regarded as a working hypothesis rather than a
final architectural claim.

Nevertheless, the experiment has shown that a substantial part of the language
required by classical synthetic geometry can be reconstructed rather than
postulated.

This represents a significant reduction of the trusted mathematical core of
the project.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. What has been learned

The completion of Book Zero does not answer the original architectural question
posed by CGJteamLab.

It does, however, provide the first substantial body of experimental evidence.

The reconstruction confirms that a significant amount of elementary synthetic
geometry can be derived systematically from the Hilbert foundation without
introducing new primitive assumptions.

Equally important, it demonstrates that the temporary interface developed
during the early stages of the project was largely provisional.

Most of its additional assumptions disappeared once the corresponding
preliminary geometry had been reconstructed.

This observation supports the original methodological principle of the project.

Rather than designing a geometry library in advance, it is possible to allow
the required mathematical structure to emerge from the reconstruction of
classical proofs.

The BNW Book Zero provides the first independent corpus against which this idea
could be tested systematically.

The outcome suggests that such an approach is viable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. The next stage

The completion of Book Zero should not be regarded as the end of the present
experiment.

On the contrary, it creates the conditions for its continuation.

Book Zero represents only the preliminary geometry required before Euclid's
Book I.

The full BNW reconstruction contains many additional derived results introduced
while formalizing the propositions themselves.

These results now become the natural object of investigation.

The central question is no longer whether the elementary synthetic language can
be reconstructed.

That question has been answered positively by the completion of Book Zero.

The next question is different.

Which of the additional results appearing throughout the reconstruction of
Euclid's Book I represent stable components of a reusable classical geometry
layer, and which should remain local consequences of individual propositions?

The answer cannot be determined a priori.

It must emerge from the continued reconstruction of the BNW corpus using the
same methodology that has guided the project from the beginning.

The completion of Book Zero therefore marks not the conclusion of the
experiment, but the transition to its next stage.
