# Log 043 --- What Should We Look for in the BNW Corpus?

## 1. Why a new question appears after Book Zero

The reconstruction of the Beeson--Narboux--Wiedijk Book Zero has now
reached theorem 34.

The immediate experiment remains unchanged: we are implementing the BNW
proofs over the CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation, using the existing
Hilbert interface and previously reconstructed Book Zero results.

However, the purpose of continuing through the BNW corpus now needs to
be stated more precisely.

The original CGJteamLab project began with the hope that a useful
language of classical geometry could emerge from actual proofs rather
than being designed in advance. The Finlay experiments produced the
first reusable interfaces, but they were extracted from a very small
proof corpus.

BNW provide something much larger. Their reconstruction of Euclid's Book
I contains not only the 48 propositions of Euclid and the preliminary
results of Book Zero, but a much larger collection of derived theorems
introduced in the course of making Euclid's arguments formally complete.

This gives us an opportunity that was not available in the first
CGJteamLab experiments.

We can use the BNW corpus not merely as a source of theorems to
translate, but as empirical material for asking:

> What is the actual reusable structure of classical synthetic reasoning
> when it is expressed in Lean 4?

The phrase "reusable structure" must not yet be identified with a single
list of lemmas.

Lean 4 provides several different mechanisms for organizing mathematical
knowledge and proof construction. Before deciding what the desired
interface should be, we should keep these mechanisms clearly in view
while reconstructing the corpus.

This log records the questions that should guide that observation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. The first possibility: a theorem-level interface

The most immediate interpretation of a classical geometry interface is a
collection of reusable theorems.

Book Zero already contains many examples:

-   collinearity transformations;
-   consequences of betweenness;
-   non-collinearity propagation;
-   uniqueness of line intersection;
-   congruence transformations;
-   five-line variants;
-   segment comparison;
-   rays;
-   connectivity and order arguments.

Such results can be viewed as mathematical building blocks placed
between foundational axioms and substantial classical proofs.

A first schematic picture is therefore:

``` text
foundational axioms
        |
        v
reusable derived theorems
        |
        v
classical geometric proofs
```

This picture remains useful, but Lean 4 suggests that it may be
incomplete.

The reusable structure may not consist only of theorem statements. The
dependencies between those theorems may themselves reveal a hierarchy of
mathematical capabilities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Lean 4 classes and geometric capabilities

The current CGJteamLab Hilbert development already uses Lean type
classes such as

``` lean
[HilbertIncidence Geo]
[HilbertOrder Geo]
[HilbertCongruence Geo]
```

These are not merely organizational labels.

They allow theorem statements to specify which geometric structure is
actually required for a proof, while Lean's instance mechanism supplies
the corresponding implementation.

During the Book Zero reconstruction this information is already visible
in theorem signatures.

Some results require only incidence. Others require order. Others
require congruence together with incidence. Segment comparison
introduces another level of structure.

This suggests that while reading the BNW corpus we should watch for a
possible hierarchy of capabilities.

A provisional picture, based only on what has appeared so far, is:

``` text
Incidence
   |
   +-- collinearity
   +-- line uniqueness
   +-- intersection uniqueness

Order
   |
   +-- betweenness machinery
   +-- rays
   +-- connectivity

Congruence
   |
   +-- segment transport
   +-- five-line
   +-- segment construction

Segment Order
   |
   +-- less-than
   +-- transport by congruence
   +-- trichotomy
```

This diagram is not a proposed final architecture.

It is a working picture that should remain visible while further proofs
are reconstructed.

Lean 4 makes it possible, in principle, for such relationships to become
explicit through structures, classes, inheritance, and instances.

A purely schematic possibility would be:

``` lean
class ClassicalIncidence (Geo) where
  ...

class ClassicalOrder (Geo)
    extends ClassicalIncidence Geo where
  ...

class ClassicalCongruence (Geo)
    extends ClassicalIncidence Geo where
  ...

class ClassicalSegmentOrder (Geo)
    extends ClassicalOrder Geo,
            ClassicalCongruence Geo where
  ...
```

Again, this is not a design decision.

The question is whether the BNW corpus itself provides evidence for
boundaries of this kind.

The current implementation has already produced a small but instructive
example. In Book Zero theorem 29, Lean's linter detected that explicitly
requesting both `HilbertPlaneIncidence` and `HilbertOrder` was redundant
because the latter already supplied the former.

That warning was not only a technical issue. It exposed a dependency in
the hierarchy of geometric assumptions.

This is precisely the kind of information that should now be observed
systematically.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. A second possibility: proof automation

There is another level that should not be confused with either axioms or
reusable mathematical theorems.

Many formal proofs contain repeated micro-arguments that are
mathematically routine but syntactically necessary.

Examples already encountered include:

-   permuting collinearity hypotheses;
-   reversing the orientation of congruence statements;
-   deriving inequalities of points from betweenness;
-   transporting segment comparison through congruence;
-   splitting elementary cases of point order;
-   composing short chains of previously proved transformations.

A mathematician often performs such operations without treating each one
as a visible step of the argument.

In Lean they can initially appear as explicit theorem applications.

This raises a separate question:

> Which recurring parts of the BNW proofs represent mathematical results
> that should remain visible in the theorem API, and which represent
> routine proof manipulation that could eventually be delegated to
> tactics?

The distinction matters.

A tactic is not a replacement for the mathematical theory. It operates
using definitions, hypotheses, lemmas, and inference rules that already
exist.

The conceptual relation is therefore closer to:

``` text
proof goal
    |
    v
proof automation / tactics
    |
    v
reusable mathematical API
    |
    v
foundational structure
```

The theorem API determines what mathematical facts are available.

A tactic may determine how much of the routine composition of those
facts must be written explicitly by the author of a proof.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Why tactics matter for the original goal

The original goal of CGJteamLab was not simply to accumulate formally
verified geometric statements.

The project began from classical proofs and from the question of whether
their mathematical structure could be expressed in a reusable formal
language.

For that purpose, the final surface of a proof matters.

Suppose a classical argument conceptually says:

``` text
A, B, C are collinear.
P and Q lie on the same line.
Therefore ...
```

If the Lean proof requires a long sequence of permutations of
`PrimCollinear`, symmetry conversions, inequality extractions, and
orientation changes before reaching the actual geometric step, then the
formal proof language is still exposing infrastructure that the
classical argument regards as routine.

Some of those steps may indicate missing reusable lemmas.

Others may indicate an opportunity for automation.

Therefore the BNW corpus can potentially reveal not only **what facts
classical geometry repeatedly uses**, but also **what proof operations
classical geometers repeatedly perform without mentioning them**.

These are different kinds of evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Four things to distinguish while reconstructing BNW

For each theorem and proof in the BNW corpus, we should therefore keep
at least four possibilities separate.

``` text
BNW proof
    |
    +--> reusable mathematical fact
    |        |
    |        v
    |     theorem API
    |
    +--> recurring required structure
    |        |
    |        v
    |     possible class boundary
    |
    +--> repeated mechanical reasoning
    |        |
    |        v
    |     possible tactic / automation
    |
    +--> argument specific to one theorem
             |
             v
          remains local to the proof
```

This is the central working diagram for the next phase of the
experiment.

It prevents several premature identifications.

Not every BNW theorem must become a permanent interface theorem.

Not every repeated theorem dependency must become a new type class.

Not every short proof should become a tactic.

And not every technical-looking step is merely technical: some may
encode genuine geometry that deserves to remain explicit.

The purpose of continuing through the corpus is precisely to acquire
enough evidence to distinguish these cases.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Book Zero is only the first observation layer

The current reconstruction of Book Zero remains important, but its role
is now clearer.

Book Zero gives us the first systematically organized collection of
preliminary geometric results from the BNW development.

It is therefore an excellent place to begin observing:

-   theorem reuse;
-   dependency structure;
-   required geometric capabilities;
-   recurring proof transformations.

But Book Zero alone cannot determine the complete answer.

The full BNW reconstruction of Book I contains many additional derived
results. Some are needed to make implicit Euclidean reasoning explicit,
some repair gaps, and some are useful variants of propositions.

Those later results may provide stronger evidence about what genuinely
deserves to become part of a reusable classical geometry layer.

The correct procedure is therefore not:

``` text
implement Book Zero
        |
        v
declare Book Zero to be the interface
```

but rather:

``` text
implement Book Zero
        |
        v
observe its structure
        |
        v
continue through the larger BNW corpus
        |
        v
compare recurring patterns
        |
        v
only then identify stable abstractions
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. What Lean 4 changes in the experiment

BNW solved the practical problem of making Euclid's proofs formally
checkable.

CGJteamLab is using their work for a different question.

Because the reconstruction is being carried out in Lean 4, we should not
assume that the organization appropriate to the original proof-checking
environment is automatically the organization we should reproduce.

Lean 4 gives us mechanisms for separating several levels:

``` text
mathematical foundations
        |
        v
typeclass / structural capabilities
        |
        v
reusable theorem API
        |
        v
tactic-level proof automation
        |
        v
written classical proof
```

Whether classical geometry actually wants all of these levels, and where
their boundaries should lie, remains an empirical question.

The BNW corpus gives us material on which to investigate it.

The important methodological decision is therefore:

> Do not design these layers in advance. Observe the corpus while
> keeping the available Lean 4 mechanisms in view.

This preserves the original CGJteamLab principle that architecture
should emerge from proof practice.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. The role of different foundations

There is also a later test that should remain in view.

The present reconstruction uses the Hilbert route because the existing
CGJteamLab Hilbert development provides a mature foundation and
interface on which the BNW proofs can be tested.

However, if a genuinely reusable classical geometry layer is eventually
identified, its dependence on Hilbert should be examined.

A possible long-term picture is:

``` text
Hilbert --------\
                 \
Tarski ----------> classical geometry layer ---> classical proofs
                 /
other foundation /
```

This is not yet a claim that such a common layer exists.

It is a criterion by which a proposed interface could eventually be
tested.

If a supposedly classical theorem API merely encodes Hilbert-specific
choices, moving it to another foundation should expose that fact.

Conversely, if substantial parts of the API can be implemented over
different foundations while the higher-level proofs remain unchanged,
that would provide strong evidence that a genuine intermediate language
of classical geometry has been isolated.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Research objective after Book Zero 34

The objective of the present phase can now be stated more precisely.

We are not merely trying to complete Book Zero.

We are not yet trying to design a final geometry library.

We are using the BNW proof corpus as experimental material in order to
determine what kinds of reusable structure appear in formal classical
geometry when reconstructed in Lean 4.

During the continuation of Book Zero and later the larger BNW corpus, we
should therefore observe three distinct levels especially carefully:

1.  **Reusable mathematical theorems**\
    Facts that repeatedly serve as genuine geometric building blocks.

2.  **Structural requirements**\
    Recurring groups of assumptions that may reveal natural boundaries
    between geometric capabilities and therefore possible Lean class
    structure.

3.  **Routine proof transformations**\
    Repeated micro-reasoning that may belong not to the visible
    mathematical API but to a tactic or other automation layer.

The remaining material should be allowed to remain local to individual
proofs.

The purpose is not to force every observation into one of these
categories immediately.

The purpose is to collect enough evidence that the eventual architecture
is derived from the proof corpus rather than imposed on it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Working question

The central question for the next stage is therefore:

> When the BNW reconstruction of Euclid is reimplemented over the
> CGJteamLab Hilbert foundation in Lean 4, what stable structure emerges
> between foundational axioms and the written classical proof?

More specifically:

``` text
What should be a theorem?

What should be a class-level capability?

What should be automated by a tactic?

What should remain local to a particular proof?
```

Book Zero 1--34 has given the first examples.

The rest of Book Zero and the larger BNW corpus should now be read with
these questions explicitly in view.

The goal is not yet to answer them.

The goal is to make sure that we are looking for the right things.
