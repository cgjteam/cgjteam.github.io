# Log 013 - Refactoring the Incidence Layer

## Motivation

The previous reductions concerned high-level predicates and individual axioms.

The next experiment went deeper: the representation of collinearity itself.

In the earlier architecture, collinearity was available directly as a primitive relation on points. This was convenient for synthetic proofs, but it obscured a more elementary incidence structure based on points, lines, and membership of a point in a line.

The purpose of this refactoring was to test whether collinearity could be moved out of the primitive layer and reconstructed from incidence without changing the mathematical argument of the Finlay development.

---

## Introducing Points, Lines, and Incidence

The refactoring introduced lines explicitly alongside points and separated incidence into its own structure.

Schematically:

```text
Point
Line
  |
  v
OnLine
```

The relevant Lean architecture used an incidence relation of the form

```lean
OnLine : Point -> Line -> Prop
```

rather than taking three-point collinearity as the starting relation.

This created a lower-level representation from which collinearity could be defined.

The change should be understood as a choice of foundation for the Hilbert-oriented path being investigated, not as a claim that every synthetic geometry must take points and lines as primitive objects.

---

## Defining Primitive Collinearity from Incidence

Three points are collinear when there exists a line incident with all three.

The corresponding definition has the form:

```lean
def PrimCollinear
    (A B C : Point) : Prop :=
  ∃ l : Line,
    OnLine A l ∧
    OnLine B l ∧
    OnLine C l
```

Thus the logical direction becomes:

```text
Point + Line + OnLine
          |
          v
     PrimCollinear
```

This is the central result of the refactoring.

Collinearity no longer needs to be primitive on this path; it can be reconstructed from incidence.

---

## Recovering Collinearity Properties

Once collinearity is expressed existentially through a common line, several elementary reorderings become straightforward consequences of the definition.

If

$$
A,\;B,\;C
$$

lie on one witnessing line, then the same witness establishes the corresponding reordered collinearities.

Properties such as symmetry and cyclic permutation can therefore be proved rather than postulated independently.

This is another instance of the pattern already observed in the previous logs:

```text
stronger primitive representation
        |
        v
definition
        |
        v
elementary derived theorems
```

The important gain is not merely a smaller list of declarations marked `axiom`. The logical source of the collinearity properties becomes explicit.

---

## Preserving a Higher-Level Interface

The rest of the geometry development should not have to reason repeatedly with existential witnesses for lines.

For synthetic proofs, a three-point collinearity predicate remains the natural language.

The refactoring therefore separates two levels:

```text
incidence foundation
        |
        v
derived collinearity
        |
        v
synthetic geometry interface
```

This distinction allows the lower-level representation to change while higher-level proofs continue to reason in familiar geometric terms.

It is an early example of a pattern that will become increasingly important in the project: foundations and proof language need not be identical.

---

## Migration Through the Existing Development

The change affected the dependency path leading to Finlay.

At the time of this refactoring, the relevant modules formed a chain approximately of the form:

```text
GeometryCore
        |
        v
HilbertAxioms
        |
        v
GeometryBase
        |
        v
MidsegmentParallel
        |
        v
FinlayProof
```

The names reflect the architecture at this stage of the project and should be read historically.

The important experimental fact is that the incidence refactoring could be propagated through the existing development.

After the necessary interface adjustments, the Finlay proof compiled on top of the new representation.

---

## What the Experiment Established

The result was more specific than a general claim that the geometry kernel had become minimal.

For the Hilbert-style architecture under investigation, the experiment established that:

- points and lines could be represented explicitly,
- incidence could serve as the lower-level relation,
- collinearity could be defined from common incidence with a line,
- elementary collinearity transformations could be derived,
- the existing high-level Finlay argument could survive the change.

This was the first refactoring in the project that substantially changed the representation beneath the synthetic interface.

---

## What It Did Not Establish

The experiment did not show that incidence with primitive lines is the unique or universally minimal foundation for geometry.

Other axiomatic systems may organize the same mathematics differently.

In particular, a point-only theory can take relations such as betweenness and equidistance as primitive and recover line-like structure in another way.

Thus the result should be understood relative to the architectural path being investigated.

The significance of the refactoring lies in exposing one coherent reduction and demonstrating that the high-level proof can remain stable above it.

---

## Conclusion

Log 013 records the transition from primitive three-point collinearity to an incidence-based representation on the Hilbert-oriented path.

The key structural change is:

```text
primitive Point / Line / OnLine
              |
              v
       defined collinearity
              |
              v
      synthetic interface
              |
              v
          Finlay proof
```

This provided concrete evidence that the logical foundation and the language used by synthetic proofs can be separated.

That separation would become important as the project began to compare different axiomatic routes to the same higher-level geometry.
