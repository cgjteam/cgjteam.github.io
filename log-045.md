# Log 045 --- Beginning the Reconstruction of Book I

## From Book Zero to Book I

The reconstruction of Book Zero established a working layer of elementary synthetic geometry above the Hilbert axioms.

The next stage of the experiment is to reconstruct Book I using this layer.

The purpose is not simply to formalize another collection of propositions. The reconstruction provides a direct test of whether the elementary results developed in Book Zero actually form a useful interface for larger classical proofs.

## First objective

The immediate objective is deliberately limited:

- reconstruct the first part of Book I,
- reuse Book Zero results whenever possible,
- avoid introducing new axioms merely to complete individual proofs,
- record which additional geometric constructions or lemmas are genuinely required.

This changes the role of Book Zero. It is no longer being developed in isolation. It is now being tested by the proofs for which it was intended.

## Current direction

The reconstruction will proceed proposition by proposition.

Rather than designing a complete Book I interface in advance, the required structure will be allowed to emerge from the formal proofs themselves.

This continues the dependency-driven method used throughout the CGJteam Lab geometry project.

## Why Book I matters

Book Zero was reconstructed as a collection of elementary results:
betweenness lemmas, collinearity transformations, segment and ray
properties, congruence tools, and other small components repeatedly used
in synthetic arguments.

During that reconstruction it was not yet clear which of these results
would become important outside Book Zero itself.

Book I provides the first substantial test.

Its propositions are large enough to require genuine geometric
constructions and multi-step synthetic arguments, but they remain close
enough to the elementary theory that their dependencies can still be
examined directly.

The question is therefore not only whether the propositions of Book I
can be formalized.

The more interesting question is:

> How much of a classical synthetic proof becomes routine once Book Zero
> is available?

## Reconstruction rather than translation

The present work is not intended as a mechanical translation of a
printed proof into Lean.

For each proposition we first identify its mathematical structure:

- the construction being performed,
- the elementary results used implicitly in the classical argument,
- the Book Zero results that already express those steps,
- and any genuinely missing theorem or construction principle.

Only after this dependency structure is understood is the Lean proof
assembled.

This distinction is important. A classical proof often compresses many
elementary geometric facts into a single sentence. In Lean these hidden
dependencies become explicit, making it possible to determine which
parts belong to the general geometry library and which belong only to
the proposition being proved.

## Testing the Book Zero layer

The reconstruction therefore gives Book Zero a second role.

Initially, Book Zero was itself the object of reconstruction. Its
elementary propositions were derived over the Hilbert foundation and
organized into a usable theorem layer.

In Book I, the direction is reversed.

Book Zero is now treated as infrastructure.

If the reconstruction succeeds with repeated reuse of its results, this
provides evidence that the layer captures useful units of synthetic
reasoning rather than merely reproducing the internal organization of
the source text.

Conversely, recurring missing lemmas will reveal where the current
working layer is incomplete.

This makes Book I not only the next formalization target, but also an
experiment in the architecture of the geometry library.

## What will be recorded

As the reconstruction proceeds, the logs will record only significant
changes in the mathematical picture.

In particular, attention will be paid to:

- Book Zero results that recur across different propositions,
- new constructions required by Book I,
- proof patterns that may deserve reusable theorems or tactics,
- and dependencies that reveal a boundary between elementary,
  neutral, and specifically Euclidean reasoning.

The objective is not to predict this architecture in advance.

As with the earlier stages of the project, it should emerge from the
formal proofs.
