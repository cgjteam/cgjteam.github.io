# Log 019 - Tarski as a Third Axiomatic Route

## 1. Overview

After the Hilbert-oriented and Suppes-oriented developments, the project opened a third axiomatic route based on Tarski's geometry.

The Tarski language is point-based and takes betweenness and segment congruence as its principal geometric relations. This makes it structurally different from both the incidence-oriented Hilbert path and the constructive affine language investigated through Suppes.

The purpose of this experiment was not to replace either existing route. It was to determine how far the same higher-level geometric development could be reconstructed from a third foundation and to make the required interpretation boundaries explicit.

This log records the architecture as it existed on 23 July. File names are therefore historical names from that stage of the project.

---

## 2. The Tarski Axiom Hierarchy

The module

```lean
TarskiAxioms.lean
```

organized the Tarski axioms into three levels:

```text
TarskiNeutral
    |
    v
TarskiPlane
    |
    v
TarskiEuclideanPlane
```

`TarskiNeutral` contained the neutral part of the theory, including congruence axioms, segment construction, the five-segment axiom, betweenness identity, and inner Pasch.

`TarskiPlane` added the two-dimensional assumptions.

`TarskiEuclideanPlane` added a Euclidean axiom.

The continuity schema was deliberately left outside the development at this stage.

The important architectural point was that the Tarski route did not introduce a second type of points. It reused the existing point type while supplying a different axiomatic structure over the relevant primitive relations.

---

## 3. Tarski Collinearity and Midpoint

The integration layer introduced Tarski-specific versions of two familiar notions:

```lean
TarskiCollinear
TarskiIsMidpoint
```

Tarski collinearity was expressed through betweenness alternatives, while midpoint combined betweenness with congruence of the two component segments.

Schematically:

```text
betweenness + congruence
          |
          +--> TarskiCollinear
          |
          +--> TarskiIsMidpoint
```

These definitions made it possible to formulate geometric input data directly in the Tarski vocabulary rather than immediately translating everything into the existing synthetic interface.

---

## 4. The Bridge Problem

The crucial issue was again the relation between a new foundation and the existing higher-level geometry language.

At this stage the project used an explicit compatibility object:

```lean
TarskiGeometryBaseBridge
```

Its role was to connect Tarski collinearity with the incidence-based collinearity already used by the shared geometry development.

This bridge must be interpreted cautiously.

It did **not** establish that the complete shared incidence interface had been derived from the Tarski axioms.

Rather, it isolated the compatibility required for the current experiment.

Schematically:

```text
Tarski axiomatic language
          |
          v
Tarski-specific definitions
          |
          v
explicit bridge
          |
          v
existing synthetic interface
```

Thus the Tarski branch, like the Suppes branch at this stage, was not yet a completely isolated derivation of the high-level development from its own axioms alone.

---

## 5. The Tarski Midsegment Experiment

The module

```lean
MidsegmentParallelTarski.lean
```

provided the Tarski-oriented entry point to the Midsegment Theorem.

Its hypotheses used `TarskiIsMidpoint`.

The bridge converted the required information into the language consumed by the existing geometric construction, after which the proof could use reusable operations involving construction, congruence, parallelism, parallelogram recognition, and collinearity transfer.

This produced a useful separation:

```text
Tarski-specific input
        |
        v
interpretation / bridge
        |
        v
shared midsegment construction
```

The theorem module remained separate from the existing `MidsegmentParallel.lean`, making the experimental route visible in the file structure.

---

## 6. The Tarski Finlay Experiment

A corresponding file,

```lean
FinlayProofTarski.lean
```

tested the Finlay argument with hypotheses stated partly in the Tarski language.

The midpoint assumptions were expressed through `TarskiIsMidpoint`, and the relevant collinearity assumptions through `TarskiCollinear`.

The Tarski midsegment result supplied the parallel relations required by the high-level argument, while bridge conversions connected the Tarski data with the shared parallelogram and intersection machinery.

This should not yet be described as a complete reduction of Finlay's theorem to the Tarski axioms.

The experiment demonstrated a Tarski-oriented route **through an explicit compatibility layer**.

That distinction is essential for understanding the logical status of the development on 23 July.

---

## 7. Three Experimental Routes

At this point the project contained three visible routes:

```text
Hilbert-oriented:
FinlayProof
    |
    v
MidsegmentParallel

Suppes-oriented:
FinlayProofSuppes
    |
    v
MidsegmentParallelSuppes

Tarski-oriented:
FinlayProofTarski
    |
    v
MidsegmentParallelTarski
```

The diagrams describe the public theorem routes, not complete foundational dependency trees.

The three branches differed substantially below these interfaces:

- the Hilbert-oriented route emphasized incidence and the existing synthetic geometry development,
- the Suppes route explored midpoint and doubling operations in constructive affine geometry,
- the Tarski route explored a point-based language of betweenness and congruence.

The comparison therefore concerned not merely three proofs, but three ways of connecting different foundations with a higher-level synthetic argument.

---

## 8. What the Experiment Revealed

The introduction of the Tarski branch strengthened a conclusion already suggested by the Suppes work.

A shared high-level theorem statement does not imply a shared foundation.

Between the axiomatic layer and the synthetic proof language there may be a substantial interpretation problem.

The emerging architecture was therefore better understood as:

```text
Hilbert        Suppes        Tarski
   \             |             /
    \            |            /
     v           v           v
      interpretation boundaries
                |
                v
       synthetic proof language
                |
                v
        higher-level geometry
```

This picture was still provisional.

In particular, some of the arrows represented explicit compatibility assumptions rather than derived interpretation theorems.

Identifying the exact status of these arrows became a central task for the subsequent work.

---

## 9. Licensing and Independent Formalization

The mathematical source for the Tarski development was Tarski and Givant's presentation of the axiomatic system.

GeoCoq was consulted as a reference for the organization and completeness of the axiom hierarchy, but its formal proofs were not copied into the project.

This distinction was relevant both mathematically and practically: the objective was an independent Lean reconstruction of the theory used by CGJteam Lab.

The detailed licensing and source-provenance discussion belongs more naturally to project documentation or Wiki than to the mathematical core of the research log.

---

## 10. Outlook

The immediate next question was whether the explicit bridge could be reduced.

For each compatibility declaration the same reverse-engineering questions now applied:

- Is it derivable from the Tarski axioms?
- Is it an interpretation theorem between two representations?
- Is it genuinely additional structure?
- Is the shared high-level notion exactly the same notion on both sides?

The Tarski experiment therefore did more than add a third branch.

It turned the problem of **interpretation between axiomatic systems and a shared synthetic interface** into an explicit research problem.
